About Nihao World

Why I built it

Nihao World began in 2025, when my younger brother started learning Chinese as a second language at a local Chinese school.

I saw how much time beginners could spend memorizing words without remembering or comfortably using them. Their reading level could also keep them from exploring the stories, history, and everyday life that make a language interesting.

I began building Nihao World as a summer project in 2026. Based on Easy Steps to Chinese for Kids, Books 1A through 4B, it combines interactive practice with cultural content chosen for children’s age and curiosity—not only their reading level.

The central idea

A learner’s current vocabulary or character level should not determine how much of Chinese culture they are able to explore.

Chinese culture goes far beyond red envelopes and Chinese New Year. It includes history, art,
inventions, stories, geography, traditions, and everyday life.

Stories and culture were some of my favorite parts of advanced Chinese classes. I wanted beginners like my brother to experience them from the start. Nihao World builds on classwork with engaging practice and cultural discovery.

Building Nihao World

Technology as a creative tool

Nihao World is also an experiment in what one student can build with today’s technology. Codex helped me code, revise, test, and coordinate image work. ChatGPT helped develop content, research cultural topics, compare sources, and organize references.

One surprise was that early conversations with DeepSeek helped shape the site’s visual style, even though I used it much less throughout the project.

AI helped with drafting, illustrations, audio, background research, and coding, but the core ideas—child-friendly UI, engaging learning activities that balance fun and challenge, and cultural stories adapted for different reading levels—came from hundreds of hours of human judgment, design, and revision.
