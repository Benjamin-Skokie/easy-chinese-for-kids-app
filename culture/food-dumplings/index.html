饺子里藏着什么惊喜？

Dumplings: A Folded Little Packet of Luck

饺子不只是食物，它还会带来好运、团圆和冬天里热腾腾的故事。

Dumplings carry more than filling. They carry luck, family time, and warm winter memory.

01

中国版的"惊喜蛋"

Chinese

中国饺子和西方的复活节彩蛋有一个很像的地方——里面藏着惊喜。不是巧克力，是硬币。大年三十晚上，全家人围在一起包饺子，其中某个饺子里偷偷塞进一枚洗干净的小铜钱或者硬币。谁吃到了这枚饺子，谁在新的一年里就会特别幸运。吃到硬币的人会大喊一声"我吃到了"，全桌的人都鼓掌，好像他中了大奖。

但小孩为了找那枚硬币，会把一整盘饺子每个咬一口。大人赶紧拦住——"不准挑！随机夹！"硬币饺子必须是无意中咬到的才灵验——刻意去捞不算数。这个小小的忌讳，让每口饺子都带着一点期待。

02

为什么是饺子？

China's Version of a Surprise Egg

Chinese

饺子的形状像什么？很多人会说像元宝——古代中国的银锭。所以过年吃饺子，常被说成把"元宝"吃进肚子里，图个吉利。而且饺子往往要一家人一起包——和面的和面、擀皮的擀皮、剁馅的剁馅、包的包。很多时候，包的过程比吃本身还重要，因为大家终于能一起站在案板前说话、帮忙、笑来笑去。

English

Chinese dumplings have something a little like Easter eggs in the West.

There can be a surprise hidden inside.

Not chocolate.

A coin.

On Chinese New Year's Eve, the whole family may gather to make dumplings. In one dumpling, someone secretly places a clean coin.

Whoever eats that dumpling is believed to have extra good luck in the new year.

The lucky person may shout, "I got it!"

Everyone at the table claps, as if that person has won a big prize.

But children who want to find the coin may try to bite every dumpling on the plate.

Then adults quickly stop them.

"No choosing! Pick one by chance!"

The coin dumpling only counts if you find it by accident.

If you hunt for it on purpose, that does not count.

That tiny rule gives every bite a little bit of excitement.

03

张仲景和饺子的起源

Why Dumplings?

Chinese

饺子是谁发明的？民间最有名的说法，会提到东汉名医张仲景。故事里，他冬天回乡，看到很多穷人耳朵冻坏了，就把羊肉和药材包进面皮里，做成像耳朵的样子分给大家吃。这道药膳叫"娇耳"，后来慢慢被人讲成了饺子的来历。

冬至吃饺子的习俗，也常和这个故事连在一起。今天北方还有一句话——"冬至不端饺子碗，冻掉耳朵没人管。"不一定真是从张仲景那时一步步传下来的，但这句话确实把冬天里那碗热饺子的分量说出来了。

English

What does a dumpling look like?

Many people say it looks like a *yuanbao*, an old Chinese silver ingot.

So eating dumplings at New Year can feel like eating little pieces of good fortune.

Dumplings also often bring the whole family into the kitchen.

One person mixes the dough.

One person rolls the wrappers.

One person chops the filling.

One person folds the dumplings.

Very often, the making matters as much as the eating.

Everyone finally stands around the same table, talking, helping, and laughing.

04

全世界都在包饺子

Zhang Zhongjing and the Origin of Dumplings

Chinese

饺子不是中国独有的——意大利有ravioli，波兰有pierogi，俄罗斯有pelmeni，土耳其有manti，韩国有mandu，日本有gyoza。把馅料包进面皮里，也许是很多地方的人都会想到的办法。不同地区之间当然也可能互相影响、互相借鉴，只是具体哪条线怎么传，还不总是说得那么死。

你在意大利吃到一枚ravioli，在西安咬开一只羊肉饺子，味道当然很不一样。可它们背后都藏着一个很朴素的主意：拿一张面皮，把好吃的东西包进去。

English

Who invented dumplings?

The most famous folk story points to Zhang Zhongjing, a great doctor from the Eastern Han dynasty.

In the story, he returned home in winter and saw many poor people with badly frozen ears.

So he wrapped lamb and medicinal herbs in dough, shaped the pieces like ears, and gave them to people to eat.

This healing food was called *jiao'er*, or "tender ears."

Later, people connected that story with the origin of dumplings.

The custom of eating dumplings at the Winter Solstice is also often tied to this story.

In northern China, people still say, "If you do not eat dumplings at Winter Solstice, no one will care if your ears freeze off."

That saying is playful, not a medical rule.

It may not truly come step by step from Zhang Zhongjing's time.

But it does show how important a hot bowl of dumplings can feel in winter.

05

小结

The Whole World Wraps Dumplings

Chinese

饺子里可以藏硬币，冬至和过年时常常会端上桌，包的时候还总是一家人一起动手。至于它到底是谁最先发明的，故事和历史未必能严丝合缝地对上。但这不妨碍它变成很多中国家庭最熟悉、也最有团圆味道的一种食物。

English

Dumplings do not belong to China alone.

Italy has *ravioli*.

Poland has *pierogi*.

Russia has *pelmeni*.

Turkey has *manti*.

Korea has *mandu*.

Japan has *gyoza*.

Wrapping tasty filling inside dough is an idea many people around the world have loved.

Different places may have influenced one another too, though it is not always easy to trace every path.

A piece of *ravioli* in Italy and a lamb dumpling in Xi'an do not taste the same.

But behind both is one simple idea:

take a wrapper, put something delicious inside, and close it up.

Summary

Dumplings can hide a lucky coin.

They often appear at Winter Solstice and Chinese New Year.

Making them is usually a family job, with many hands around one table.

As for who invented dumplings first, stories and history do not always line up perfectly.

But that does not stop dumplings from becoming one of the most familiar and family-filled foods in many Chinese homes.
