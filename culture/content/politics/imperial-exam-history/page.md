heading_zh: 最早的时候，先看你是谁家的孩子
heading_en: Long Ago, Family Came First
image: 1-family-first.webp
caption_zh: 大门还没打开，普通人家的孩子已经被挡在外面了。
caption_en: Before the door even opens, an ordinary child is already standing outside it.
[zh]
想象一下，你生在很久很久以前。你很聪明，记性也好，做事又认真。可这些本事，一开始常常都不重要。

别人先问的不是“你会什么”，而是“你是谁家的孩子”。在很早的时候，当官往往跟出身关系最大。贵族的孩子，长大以后更容易继续做官。普通人家的孩子，大多还是种地、打铁、织布，走原来的老路。

门还没开，你已经站在门外了。这不是因为你不努力，而是因为很多人根本还没打算认真看看你。
[/zh]
[en]
Imagine being born a very long time ago. You are smart, you remember things well, and you work hard. But at first, those talents may not matter much.

People do not begin by asking, "What can you do?" They ask, "What family are you from?" Long ago, becoming an official often depended more on birth than on ability. Children from noble families were much more likely to stay near power. Children from ordinary families usually followed the old family path instead.

The door was not even open yet, and you were already outside it. That was the hard part. It was not only that you had to prove yourself. Many people were not planning to look at you in the first place.
[/en]
[/section]

[section]
page: 2
layout: text-wrap-image
heading_zh: 到了汉朝，门开了一条缝
heading_en: In the [[Han dynasty]], the Door Opened a Crack
image: 2-han-recommendation.webp
caption_zh: 地方官会在本地打听，看看谁做事好、名声也好。
caption_en: Local officials looked around their area to see who was known as honest, helpful, and hardworking.
[zh]
后来，[[汉朝]]的人发现，只靠贵族来管国家不行。因为有些贵族子弟没什么本事，可民间明明也有很能干的人。

于是，他们用了“察举”这个办法。简单说，就是地方官在本地看一看：谁名声好？谁做事认真？谁最被大家夸？然后把这些人的名字往上报。

如果你是个普通孩子，这比从前公平一点。你帮邻居、照顾父母、读书认真，慢慢就可能被人看见。可这条路还是不够稳，因为名声有时是真的，有时也能装出来。要是推荐你的人不喜欢你，你还是可能上不去。
[/zh]
[en]
Later, people in the [[Han dynasty]] saw a problem. A country could not be run only by nobles. Some noble sons were not very capable, while talented people in ordinary families had no path upward.

So the door opened a little. Officials began using recommendations. Local leaders looked around and asked: Who is known for honesty? Who works hard? Who helps others? Then they sent those names upward.

For an ordinary child, this was fairer than before. If people knew you as kind, responsible, and serious about learning, you might finally be noticed. But the system still had a weakness. A good reputation can be real, or it can be an act. And if the person choosing names simply did not like you, your talent could still be left behind.
[/en]
[/section]

[section]
page: 3
layout: text-wrap-image
heading_zh: 到了魏晋，大家族又排到了前面
heading_en: In the [[Wei and Jin periods]], Great Families Moved to the Front Again
image: 3-great-families-front.webp
caption_zh: 制度看起来更整齐了，可前排的人常常还是那些大家族。
caption_en: The new ranking system looked orderly, but the best places often still went to famous families.
[zh]
再往后，到了[[魏晋时期]]，朝廷用了“九品中正制”。听上去好像更有规矩了：有人专门给地方上的读书人打分，从高到低排队。

可真正到了下面，事情常常不是这样。大门第、大姓氏的人，还是更容易排在前面。普通人家的孩子，再努力，也常常排不上去。

如果你生在那个时候，最难受的地方不是“我不够好”，而是“别人根本没打算认真看我”。连上场的机会都没有，当然更难往前走。
[/zh]
[en]
Later came the [[Wei and Jin periods]], when the court used a ranking system to sort local educated men from high to low. On paper, that sounded more organized.

But in real life, famous families still had a big advantage. Sons from powerful clans often stood near the front, while children from ordinary homes stayed far back, no matter how hard they worked.

That was a painful lesson. The problem was not always, "I am not good enough." Sometimes the problem was, "No one truly plans to give me a fair look." Losing an exam is hard. Never getting a real chance can feel even worse.
[/en]
[/section]

[section]
page: 4
layout: text-wrap-image
heading_zh: 到了隋唐，终于有了一张考卷
heading_en: In the [[Sui dynasty]] and [[Tang dynasty]], There Was Finally an Exam Paper
image: 4-first-exam-paper.webp
caption_zh: 不管家里穷还是富，考场里先摆在你面前的是同一张卷子。
caption_en: Whether a family was rich or poor, the first thing in front of each student was the same sheet of paper.
[zh]
真正的大变化，发生在[[隋朝]]，也在[[唐朝]]慢慢长大。朝廷开始说：不光看出身，也来考试吧。

这就是科举最了不起的地方。它不是一下子把天下变得完全公平，也不是每个穷孩子都能立刻翻身。可它第一次让很多普通人觉得：也许，我也可以靠自己的笔试一试。

如果你一路从乡里考到京城，终于走进考场，你旁边的人也许是官员的儿子，也许是富商的孩子，也许和你一样，袖口还打着补丁。可当卷子发下来，至少表面上，大家先面对的是同一张纸。

这件事本身，就已经和从前很不一样了。
[/zh]
[en]
The truly big change began in the [[Sui dynasty]] and grew stronger in the [[Tang dynasty]]. The court started saying, "We will not look only at family. Come take an exam."

That was the bold idea behind the imperial exams. It did not make the whole world fair overnight, and it did not mean every poor child could suddenly change his life. But for the first time, many ordinary people could think, "Maybe I can try with my own brush."

If you made your way from local tests to the capital, the child beside you might be an official's son, a merchant's son, or another poor student wearing patched sleeves. But when the paper was handed out, each person faced the same sheet first.

That alone was already a huge change from the older world.
[/en]
[/section]

[section]
page: 5
layout: text-only
heading_zh: 后来，这扇门修得越来越严
heading_en: Later, the Door Became Stricter
[zh]
后来的人，也一直在想怎么让考试更公平。怕作弊，就把考生名字糊起来。怕考官认出字迹，就让别人重抄一遍卷子。怕考官偷偷和外面联系，就把他们锁在里面，批完卷再出来。你能看出来，后来的朝廷很在意一件事：怎么让关系少一点，让卷子自己多说一点话。

可这条路后来也出了问题。到了明清，考试的格式越来越死，很多学生最后拼的，不只是学问，也是背诵、套路和谁更会按规矩说话。科举在1905年废除了，可“通过考试选人”这个想法并没有消失。今天的人虽然不再去古代考场，可很多人还是相信：不该只看你家里是谁，也该看看你自己能不能写、能不能想、能不能做。
[/zh]
[en]
Later governments kept trying to make the exam road fairer. To stop cheating, they covered students' names. To keep judges from recognizing handwriting, they had papers copied again. To stop secret messages, they sometimes shut examiners inside until grading was done. You can see what they cared about: how to let the paper speak more loudly than personal connections.

But this road had problems too. By the Ming and Qing dynasties, the rules grew stiff, and many students ended up competing in memorization, patterns, and the skill of sounding correct inside a narrow form. The imperial exams were abolished in 1905, yet the idea behind them did not disappear. People today no longer sit in those old exam halls, but many still believe this: we should not ask only who your family is. We should also ask what you can write, what you can think through, and what you can do.
[/en]
[/section]

[ending]
heading_zh: 小结
heading_en: In Summary
[zh]
做官这条路，最早先看出身。后来，看推荐。再后来，看考卷。它从来没有完美过，可它确实一点一点地把门往更多普通人那边推开了。

所以，科举真正留下来的，不只是古代考场，而是一个一直有人在想的问题：选人，到底该先看家世，还是先看本事？
[/zh]
[en]
The road to office changed slowly. First it depended on birth. Later it depended on recommendations. Still later it depended more on exam papers. It was never perfect, but little by little it pushed the door open for more ordinary people.

So the deepest thing the imperial exams left behind was not only an old test hall. It was a question people still care about: when choosing people, should we look first at family, or first at ability?
[/en]
[/ending]
