# Imperial Exam Content — References and Editorial Notes

This file is for editorial research and does not render on the website.

## Tang Examination Categories and the Changing *Jinshi* Exam

Source: *Xin Tang Shu* (New Book of Tang), “Selection System I,” Chinese Text Project
URL: https://ctext.org/wiki.pl?chapter=304555&if=en
Accessed: 2026-08-20

Source: Nathan Vedal, “Never Taking a Shortcut: Examination Poetry of the Tang Dynasty,” *Chinese Literature: Essays, Articles, Reviews*
URL: https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1179/0737503415Z.00000000014
Accessed: 2026-08-20

Source: “论唐代进士科考试内容的嬗变,” *华东师范大学学报（哲学社会科学版）*
URL: https://xbzs.ecnu.edu.cn/CN/10.16382/j.cnki.1000-5579.2001.03.011
Accessed: 2026-08-20

Supports:

- Tang examinations included multiple categories, including *mingjing*, *jinshi*, law, writing, and calculation.
- The *jinshi* exam changed over time: policy questions were important early, and poetry and *fu* became especially prominent later, alongside other components.
- Regulated examination poetry tested command of a literary tradition and constrained composition, not merely spontaneous poetic brilliance.

Editorial boundary:

- Do not say every Tang candidate took the same subjects or that the first question in a *jinshi* exam was always a poem.
- Do not claim that passing the calculation examination sent a candidate directly into finance or engineering; the reviewed sources support the subject category, not that simple job pipeline.
- The lamplit boy and sewing adult in `1-tang-poetry-exam.webp` are modern imagined figures, not documented people.

## Northern Song Subjects and the Wang Anshi Reforms

Source: Zhang Xiqing, “论王安石的贡举改革,” *北京大学学报（哲学社会科学版）*
URL: https://ccj.pku.edu.cn/Article/DownLoad?id=271015516&type=ArticleFile
Accessed: 2026-08-20

Source: Liu Pei, “北宋科场改革与律赋沉浮——以熙宁变法为中心,” Peking University journal platform
URL: https://ccj.pku.edu.cn/article/info?aid=113680099
Accessed: 2026-08-20

Source: Zhuge Yibing, “北宋神宗朝科举制度之演变,” hosted by Henan University, originally summarized in *历史与社会*
URL: https://sxyk.henu.edu.cn/info/1014/3069.htm
Accessed: 2026-08-20

Supports:

- Before the reforms, Northern Song *jinshi* candidates mainly wrote poetry and *fu* while also facing essays, policy responses, and other components.
- The 1071 reform removed poetry and *fu* from the *jinshi* exam and emphasized classical interpretation, essays, and policy responses.
- The reform was contested, and poetry and *fu* were partly restored during later political change.

Editorial boundary:

- The Yellow River, border-grain, and tax questions in the old story were illustrative inventions, not verified exam questions, and are omitted.
- Do not frame the change as “style versus thinking.” Poetry, classical interpretation, and policy writing all required trained reading, reasoning, and expression.
- `2-song-policy-questions.webp` is a modern symbolic illustration. Its officials, map, room, and question are not a reconstruction of a documented exam.

## Ming Examination Sessions and the Eight-Legged Essay

Source: *Ming Shi* (History of Ming), “Selection System II,” Chinese Text Project
URL: https://ctext.org/wiki.pl?chapter=287810&if=en
Accessed: 2026-08-20

Source: National Museum of China, “明万历三十二年余懋孳会试朱卷”
URL: https://www.chnmuseum.cn/zp/zpml/gjwxbt/202008/t20200824_247146.shtml
Accessed: 2026-08-20

Supports:

- Ming metropolitan exams were held in three sessions; the first tested Four Books and Five Classics writing, while later sessions included essays, official document forms, judgments, and policy questions.
- The classical essay demanded adherence to authorized texts and increasingly regulated composition.

Editorial boundary:

- The article describes the mature eight-legged essay in broad terms rather than claiming its final form existed unchanged from the first year of the Ming.
- Do not reduce the form to memorization. It was restrictive and criticized, but successful writing also required close textual knowledge, structure, parallel language, and practice.
- Do not imply that all three Ming sessions consisted solely of eight-legged essays.

## Qing Subjects and the Addition of Regulated Poetry

Source: Renmin University of China, *Qing History Journal*, article abstract on the 1757 addition of regulated verse to the examinations
URL: https://qsyj.ruc.edu.cn/CN/abstract/abstract2534.shtml
Accessed: 2026-08-20

Source: Chen Shengzheng, “清代科场诗文的共通性探论,” *华东师范大学学报*
URL: https://tsla.ecnu.edu.cn/CN/Y2023/V43/I5/62
Accessed: 2026-08-20

Supports:

- Regulated examination poetry was permanently added to the Qing examination program in 1757.
- Eight-legged essays and regulated examination poems both remained important Qing examination forms.

Editorial boundary:

- Do not present Qing examination content as only eight-legged prose.
- The article uses 1757 only for the permanent institutional addition, not as the first appearance of every form of poetry in Qing examinations.

## Three Sessions, Timing, and Examination Cells

Source: National Museum of China, “光绪年间会试朱卷”
URL: https://www.chnmuseum.cn/zp/zpml/201812/t20181218_24853.shtml
Accessed: 2026-08-20

Source: National Museum of China, “同治九年顺天府发给段溥源的乡试卷票”
URL: https://www.chnmuseum.cn/zp/zpml/gjwxbt/202008/t20200824_247151.shtml
Accessed: 2026-08-20

Source: Henan University, “河南贡院：中国科举考试终结地,” reprinting *Henan Daily*
URL: https://xyh.henu.edu.cn/sjhd/mlws/show-2930.html
Accessed: 2026-08-20

Source: Academia Sinica Museum of the Institute of History and Philology, “貢院號舍透視圖”
URL: https://museum.sinica.edu.tw/exhibition/132/item/1084/
Accessed: 2026-08-20

Supports:

- Qing provincial examinations normally followed a three-year cycle and had three sessions.
- Qing metropolitan sessions began on the ninth, twelfth, and fifteenth; candidates entered a day early and left after submitting the next day, making each session about three days.
- Large late imperial examination compounds contained rows of individual cells in which candidates wrote and remained during a session.
- In a documented Qing cell design, movable wooden boards could serve as a desk, seat, or sleeping platform.

Editorial boundary:

- “Three days and two nights” describes the confinement of one typical Ming-Qing provincial or metropolitan session, not the duration of the entire examination system.
- Exact cell dimensions, furnishings, temperature, and rules varied by place and period and are not specified in the article.
- `3-ming-qing-exam-cell.webp` is a modern imagined scene, not a measured reconstruction of one named cell or candidate.

## Story Frame, Access, and Fairness

Editorial synthesis based on the sources above.

Supports:

- Changing examination content changed the education and practice that candidates needed.
- Uniform papers and anti-cheating procedures did not erase differences in family resources, education, travel, health, gender, or legal eligibility.

Editorial boundary:

- The article no longer follows “the same poor child,” because no source documents the invented boy, his mother, or his movement through four dynasties.
- The article does not call the system completely fair or open to everyone. Women were generally excluded from the official civil examination route, and access varied across time and social status.
- The final questions about what tests value are modern reflections, clearly separated from specific historical claims.
