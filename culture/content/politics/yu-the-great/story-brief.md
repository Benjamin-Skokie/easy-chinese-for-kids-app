# Yu the Great Shared Story and Facts Brief

## Core ideas and evidence

1. Later tradition says Gun failed to control the waters and Yu succeeded by opening waterways. The neat `block versus guide` contrast is memorable but not a surviving engineering report.
2. Early texts give Yu's work an enormous scope: rivers, ditches, mountains, marshes, fields, food, travel, and ordered land. The images do not reconstruct exact tools or sites.
3. The *Mencius* says Yu worked away for eight years and passed his gate three times without entering. The later *Records of the Grand Historian* says thirteen years and passing home without entering. Modern childbirth and growing-child scenes are later additions.
4. Later tradition connects Yu's water work, succession from Shun, and the founding of Xia. Some scholars argue these links developed over time.
5. Erlitou proves a complex early Bronze Age society, not a written identification of Yu or Xia. The identification remains debated.
6. A 2016 study proposed a great outburst flood at Jishi Gorge around 1920 BCE and a possible link to the legend. Other researchers dispute parts of the reconstruction. Geology cannot name Yu.

## Boundaries

- Label every retained image as imagination or a concept picture.
- Do not invent Yu's tools, teams, routes, thoughts, or family scenes.
- Keep eight years and thirteen years in their separate sources.
- Present archaeology and geology as evidence with limits, not proof of a hero.

## Level assignments

- Early Reader: tell the traditional flood story while clearly labeling what is story, and center teamwork, public work, and the doorway scene.
- Story: add the competing doorway versions, the later Xia connection, Erlitou, and the disputed flood hypothesis.
- Advanced: preserve textual chronology, scholarly disagreement, archaeology, geology, and the political meaning of the legend.
