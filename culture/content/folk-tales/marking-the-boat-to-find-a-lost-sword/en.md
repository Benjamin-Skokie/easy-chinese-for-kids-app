# Marking the Boat to Find a Lost Sword

## Marking the Boat to Find a Lost Sword

Long ago, a man from the state of [[Chu|chu-state]] was crossing a river by boat. As the boat moved through the water, his sword suddenly slipped from his hand. **Splash!** It sank into the river. The man quickly took out a knife and scratched a mark on the side of the boat. Someone nearby asked, “What are you doing?” “My sword fell into the water right here,” the man said. “I’m marking the spot so I can find it later.” The boat kept moving. At last, it reached the other side of the river. The man hurried to the mark he had made, jumped into the water, and began searching for his sword. But the sword was nowhere to be found. Of course it wasn’t. The boat had moved down the river. The sword had not. This old story gave us the expression **kè zhōu qiú jiàn**—“marking the boat to look for a sword.” Today, people use it when someone keeps using an old idea even though the situation has already changed. The mark was still in the same place on the boat. But the boat was no longer in the same place on the river.
