# Editorial References: The Monkey King Makes Trouble in Heaven

This file is for editorial research and does not render on the website.

Accessed: 2026-09-01

Supports:

- The golden-hooped staff, the Book of Life and Death, Sun Wukong's low-ranking job in Heaven, the title “Great Sage Equal to Heaven,” his battles with Heaven, the Buddha's palm, Five Elements Mountain, and his later release.

## 1. The staff and the Book of Life and Death

Source: Wikisource, *Journey to the West*, Chapter 3
URL: https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E8%A5%BF%E9%81%8A%E8%A8%98/%E7%AC%AC003%E5%9B%9E

- Confirms that Sun Wukong obtains the Ruyi Jingu Bang from the Dragon Palace.
- Includes his journey to the underworld and his removal of his own name and the names of other monkeys from the Book of Life and Death.

Editorial boundary:

- The page calls the weapon the “golden-hooped staff” and focuses on its place in the story rather than listing its measurements or every magical property.
- The underworld episode is reduced to one sentence because this short story focuses on the trouble in Heaven.

## 2. The heavenly job and the Great Sage

Source: Wikisource, *Journey to the West*, Chapter 4
URL: https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E8%A5%BF%E9%81%8A%E8%A8%98/%E7%AC%AC004%E5%9B%9E

- Confirms that Heaven gives Sun Wukong a low-ranking post caring for the heavenly horses.
- Confirms that he leaves Heaven after learning how unimportant the post is and takes the title “Great Sage Equal to Heaven.”
- Describes the heavenly forces sent against him and their failure to defeat him.

Editorial boundary:

- The child-facing page says “a small official job” rather than stopping to explain the full heavenly rank system.
- Several battles and named warriors are combined into one short sequence so readers ages six to ten can follow the main action.

## 3. The Buddha's palm and Five Elements Mountain

Source: Wikisource, *Journey to the West*, Chapter 7
URL: https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E8%A5%BF%E9%81%8A%E8%A8%98/%E7%AC%AC007%E5%9B%9E

- Confirms the challenge in which Sun Wukong tries to leap beyond the Buddha's palm, mistakes the Buddha's fingers for pillars at the end of the world, and discovers that he never left the palm.
- Confirms that he is trapped beneath Five Elements Mountain.

Editorial boundary:

- A crude joke in the original marking scene is omitted for this age group.
- The long series of events leading to the Buddha's arrival is compressed without changing the challenge or its outcome.

## 4. Sun Wukong is released

Source: Wikisource, *Journey to the West*, Chapter 14
URL: https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E8%A5%BF%E9%81%8A%E8%A8%98/%E7%AC%AC014%E5%9B%9E

- Confirms that the monk traveling west reaches Five Elements Mountain and Sun Wukong is released to join the journey.

Editorial boundary:

- The page ends at the beginning of the westward journey. The monk's full background, the other travelers, and the later trials belong to the site's longer *Journey to the West* article.
- “Sacred books” is used as a short child-facing explanation of the Buddhist scriptures the monk is seeking.
