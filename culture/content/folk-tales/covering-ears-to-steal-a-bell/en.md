# The Man Who Covered His Ears to Steal a Bell

## The Man Who Covered His Ears to Steal a Bell

Long ago, a man saw a large bronze bell and wanted to steal it. This was not a little bell that he could pick up and carry away. It was big and heavy. The man tried to move it, but he could not. Then he had an idea. “I’ll break it into smaller pieces,” he thought. “Then I can carry them away one at a time.” He found a hammer, raised it high, and struck the bell. **CLANG!** The loud sound rang out. The man jumped. If other people heard the bell, they might come running! He thought for a moment. Then he came up with another idea. He covered his ears tightly with both hands. “There,” he thought. “Now I can’t hear it.” Then he tried to strike the bell again. Of course, covering his own ears did not make the sound disappear. Everyone else could still hear it. The man had fooled only himself. The earliest version of this old story was about stealing a large bell, or **zhōng**. Over time, the story became the expression **yǎn ěr dào líng**—“cover your ears while stealing a bell.” Today, people use the expression when someone tries to ignore an obvious truth, as if refusing to notice a problem could make it disappear. Covering your ears may keep **you** from hearing the bell. But the bell is still ringing.
