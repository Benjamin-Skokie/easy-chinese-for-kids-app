# The Willing One Takes the Hook

## The Willing One Takes the Hook

Long ago, an old man named [[Jiang Ziya|jiang-ziya]] sat beside a river with a fishing rod. People passing by noticed something strange. His hook had no bait. Even stranger, the hook was straight. “How are you going to catch a fish like that?” someone asked. Jiang Ziya did not seem worried. He was not really waiting for a fish. He was waiting for the right person to notice him. Jiang Ziya had spent many years studying how rulers could govern wisely. He believed he could help a good ruler, but he did not want to chase after one asking for a job. So he waited by the river. One day, [[King Wen of Zhou|king-wen-zhou]] heard about the unusual old fisherman and came to meet him. The two talked. King Wen quickly realized that Jiang Ziya knew far more about running a country than catching fish. He invited Jiang Ziya to become one of his advisers. And that was the catch Jiang Ziya had really been waiting for. Later, people told the story with an even more unusual detail: Jiang Ziya fished with a straight hook and no bait, saying that whoever was willing could come to him. That is where the saying **“Jiang Taigong goes fishing—the willing one takes the hook”** comes from. Sometimes, the person holding the fishing rod is not really waiting for a fish at all.
