# Ma Liang and the Magic Brush: Everything He Draws Comes Alive

## A Poor Child Gets a Magic Brush

Long, long ago, in a legend, there was a boy named Ma Liang.

His family was very poor. He could not even buy a paintbrush.

But he wanted to draw more than anything.

He lay outside the village school window and secretly watched the teacher show students how to write and paint.

He dipped his finger in river water and traced bird wings on stone.

He used a burned stick to draw fish scales in the sand.

Other people laughed at him.

"You do not even have a brush. What are you drawing for?"

One night, Ma Liang had a dream.

An old man with a white beard handed him a brush.

"Take it. Draw what you want to draw."

The next morning, Ma Liang woke up.

Beside his pillow was the brush.

That is the moment the whole story suddenly lights up.

---

## He Draws Wind and Rain, and a Whole World Appears

Ma Liang picked up the brush and painted a rooster on the wall.

The rooster came alive, jumped down, and cried, "Cock-a-doodle-doo!"

He ran outside and drew a river.

Real water began to rush and splash.

The villagers stared with round eyes. No one knew what to say.

Soon, news spread.

A rich landlord, a man who owned lots of land, sent people to grab Ma Liang.

"Use that brush to draw gold and silver for me!"

Ma Liang was taken into a big house. The landlord had a large table set up with paper.

"Draw a mountain of gold! Draw a money tree!"

Ma Liang calmly painted a wide sea.

The landlord shouted, "I asked for a gold mountain! Why did you draw the sea?"

Ma Liang drew a small island in the middle of the sea. On the island stood a shining mountain of gold.

"It is ready," Ma Liang said. "Go get it."

The landlord's eyes lit up.

"A boat! Draw me a boat!"

Ma Liang drew a wooden boat. The landlord climbed in with his family and shouted, "Wind! Draw wind for us!"

Ma Liang drew a few strokes of wind.

The sail filled, and the boat moved away from shore toward the golden mountain.

"Stronger wind!" the landlord yelled.

Ma Liang kept drawing.

The wind grew harder. The waves rose higher. The boat tipped wildly.

The landlord's shouting was torn apart by the storm.

Then one great wave passed.

The sea became quiet again.

Nothing was left.

Some versions say Ma Liang later went to poorer places and drew plows, oxen, and houses for people who needed them.

Some say the magic brush never appeared again.

Maybe it only needed to shine once, in the hand of someone who would use it for the poor.

---

## Summary

Ma Liang was so poor he could not buy a brush, but he still wanted to draw.

The magic brush made everything he painted come alive.

It also gave the story its power.

When the greedy landlord wanted gold, Ma Liang drew a sea, an island, a boat, and a storm.

In the end, children often remember more than the magic brush.

They remember that Ma Liang always used it on the side of poor people.
