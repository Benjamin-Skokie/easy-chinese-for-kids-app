# Make Noise Here, Move There

## Make Noise Here, Move There

Long ago, a commander named [[Zhu Jun|zhu-jun]] was trying to enter a walled town. The people defending the town were watching carefully. If his soldiers came from one side, they would hurry there to stop them. So Zhu Jun gave them something very noisy to watch. On one side of the wall, his soldiers beat drums and made it look as if an attack was about to begin. **Boom! Boom! Boom!** The defenders heard the drums and rushed toward the noise. That was exactly what Zhu Jun wanted. While everyone was looking one way, he quietly led another group of soldiers around to the other side of the town. That side was now lightly guarded. Before the defenders realized what had happened, Zhu Jun’s soldiers had found their way in. This kind of trick became known as **shēng dōng jī xī**—literally, “make noise in the east, strike in the west.” The words “east” and “west” do not have to mean those exact directions. The idea is simple: **Make people look here.** **Then do what you really planned over there.**
