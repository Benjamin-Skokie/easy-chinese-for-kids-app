# The Spear and the Shield

## The Spear and the Shield

Long ago, a man in the state of Chu went to market to sell spears and shields. He lifted one of his shields and called out, “Look at this shield! It is so strong that nothing in the world can pierce it.” People stopped to listen. Then he picked up a spear. “And look at this spear! It is so sharp that there is nothing in the world it cannot pierce.” That sounded impressive too. But someone in the crowd looked at the spear, then at the shield. “What would happen,” he asked, “if you used that spear to pierce that shield?” The seller froze. If the spear pierced the shield, then the shield was not as strong as he had claimed. But if the shield stopped the spear, then the spear could not pierce everything. He had no answer. This old story gave us the expression **zì xiāng máo dùn**. **Máo** means “spear,” and **dùn** means “shield.” Today, the expression is used when two things someone says or does cannot both be true. The spear and shield are gone, but the question is still useful: **Can both things really be true?**
