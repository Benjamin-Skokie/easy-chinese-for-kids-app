## 1. 《庄子·秋水》中的井蛙与东海之鳖

Source: Chinese Text Project, *Zhuangzi*, “The Floods of Autumn”
URL: https://ctext.org/zhuangzi/floods-of-autumn/zh

- The shallow-well frog boasts about jumping, resting, swimming, standing in mud, and ruling his little pool among insects, crabs, and tadpoles.
- The turtle from the Eastern Sea cannot fit through the well opening and describes a sea whose size and depth cannot be measured by ordinary distances.
- Flood years do not make the Eastern Sea fuller, and drought years do not make it smaller; hearing this leaves the frog astonished.

Editorial boundary:

- The Chinese and English stories retell the classical passage in child-friendly modern language rather than presenting a literal translation.
- The frog's thoughts, the connective narration, and the closing lesson are editorial framing based on the source's contrast between limited experience and the vast sea.
- “大鳖” is briefly explained for children as a water animal resembling a turtle; the English story uses “giant turtle” for immediate readability.
- The picture is a modern storybook illustration, not a zoological or historical reconstruction.
