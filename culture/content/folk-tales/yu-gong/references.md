# Editorial References: Yu Gong Moves the Mountains

This file is for editorial research and does not render on the website.

Accessed: 2026-09-01

Supports:

- The story's source in the “Questions of Tang” chapter of the *Liezi*, its main events and characters, and its traditional reading as a fable about continuing in the face of a difficult task.

## 1. The story text

Source: Hong Kong Education Bureau, recommended classical passage, “Yu Gong Moves the Mountains” from *Liezi*
URL: https://www.edb.gov.hk/attachment/tc/curriculum-development/kla/chi-edu/recommended-passages/ks3_03_text.pdf

- Preserves the traditional sequence used by this article: the Taihang and Wangwu mountains block travel; the nearly ninety-year-old Yu Gong discusses digging with his family; family members and a neighbor's child carry baskets of earth and stone; Zhi Sou laughs; Yu Gong answers that later generations can continue while the mountains do not grow; and the Lord of Heaven sends two mighty spirits to carry the mountains away.

Editorial boundary:

- The child-facing article keeps the original problem, argument, answer, and magical ending but uses new, simple language and concrete sound and weather details.
- Small images such as tired legs, a water break, and the first visible notch are storybook connective details, not quotations or historical claims.
- Exact ancient measurements and place names beyond the two mountains are omitted because they do not help a beginning reader follow the fable.

## 2. The fable and its common meaning

Source: State Council Information Office of the People's Republic of China, “Traditional Culture: Yu Gong Moves the Mountains”
URL: https://english.scio.gov.cn/featured/chinakeywords/2024-08/30/content_117397300.htm

- Identifies the tale as an ancient fable from the *Liezi*.
- Summarizes its long association with confidence, perseverance, and continuing to work through hardship.

Source: Jiyuan Municipal Government, “The Spirit of Yu Gong Moves the Mountains”
URL: https://rbj.jiyuan.gov.cn/rbj_xwzx/rbj_rsdt/t373200.html

- Also identifies the source as *Liezi: Questions of Tang* and describes the tale as an old fable centered on Yu Gong's refusal to stop digging.

Editorial boundary:

- The ending stays close to the fable's central idea: Yu Gong did not expect to finish alone or quickly. He trusted that later generations would continue, and he refused to give up because the task looked impossible.

## Image note

The four pictures are modern storybook illustrations, not historical reconstructions. They match the ancient fable's sequence: the mountains blocking the house, the family digging and carrying baskets, Zhi Sou laughing at Yu Gong, and heavenly spirits carrying the mountains away.
