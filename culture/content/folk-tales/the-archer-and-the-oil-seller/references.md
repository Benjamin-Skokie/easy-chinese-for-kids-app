# Editorial References: The Archer and the Oil Seller

Supports:

## 1. 欧阳修《卖油翁》原文

Source: Hong Kong Government, Assessment and Assessment for Learning, “賣油翁”
URL: https://www.ate.gov.hk/tchinese/doc/aw_CLE_1819/reference/B_pdf/Song_Mai%20you%20weng.pdf

- Identifies Chen Yaozi as an exceptional archer who could hit eight or nine times out of ten.
- Describes the old oil seller watching, nodding slightly, and explaining that the archer's ability came from熟练的手法.
- Records the gourd, the coin over its opening, the oil passing through the coin hole, and the coin remaining dry.
- Preserves the old man's conclusion: “我亦无他，惟手熟尔。”

Editorial boundary:

- The child-facing story is a gentle narrative adaptation rather than a literal translation of the classical text.
- It softens Chen's anger, expands brief actions into natural dialogue, and omits the final line in which Chen sends the old man away.
- The English title keeps “Oil Seller,” but the story introduces him as “an old man who sold oil” and then calls him “the old man” for natural children's-story English.
- The picture is a modern storybook illustration, not a historical reconstruction.

## 2. Readable modern reference text

Source: 古文岛（原古诗文网）, 欧阳修《卖油翁》
URL: https://www.gushiwen.cn/shiwenv_2e3bc3aa2f6b.aspx

- Provides the complete classical passage together with a modern Chinese explanation of the archery and oil-pouring actions.
- Confirms that “熟能生巧” is a later summary of the lesson rather than a four-character phrase spoken in the original text.

Editorial boundary:

- The article uses “熟能生巧” as a child-friendly explanation of the story's later meaning; it does not claim that those four characters appear in Ouyang Xiu's original wording.
