# The Man Who Pretended to Play

## The Man Who Pretended to Play

Long ago, King Xuan of Qi loved listening to the **yú**, an ancient wind instrument made with bamboo pipes. But he did not like hearing just one musician. He wanted hundreds of musicians to play together. A man named Nanguo heard about this and joined the group. There was only one problem. **He could not play the yú at all.** Whenever the musicians performed, Nanguo sat among them, held up his instrument, and pretended to play. With so many people playing at once, no one noticed. For years, his trick worked. Then King Xuan died, and King Min became the new ruler. King Min liked the yú too—but he wanted to hear each musician play **one at a time**. Nanguo knew he was in trouble. The moment he played alone, everyone would discover that he could not play at all. So before his turn came, he quietly ran away. This story gave us the expression **làn yú chōng shù**. Today, it describes someone who does not really have the skill but hides among others and pretends that they do. It may be easy to disappear in a crowd. **Until it is your turn.**
