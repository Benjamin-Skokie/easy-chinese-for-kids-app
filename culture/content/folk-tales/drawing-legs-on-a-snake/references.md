# Editorial References: Drawing Legs on a Snake

This file is for editorial research and does not render on the website.

## 《战国策·齐策二》

Source: Chinese Text Project, *Strategies of the Warring States*, “Strategies of Qi II”
URL: https://ctext.org/zhan-guo-ce/qi-er/zh
Accessed: 2026-09-06

Supports:

- The original passage tells of several attendants given one cup of wine. Because it was too little to share but enough for one person, they agreed that the first person to finish drawing a snake would receive it.
- The first man finishes, holds the wine, and begins adding feet to his snake. Another man finishes, says that snakes have no feet, and takes the wine.

Editorial boundary:

- The child-facing story follows the sequence and meaning supplied by the user without adding characters or changing the outcome.
- The dialogue and modern explanation are child-facing retellings rather than verbatim translations of the classical text.
- The picture is a modern storybook illustration of the drawing contest, not a historical reconstruction.

## The larger Zhāo Yáng episode

Source: Chinese Text Project, parallel text for the Zhāo Yáng passage
URL: https://ctext.org/text.pl?if=gb&node=49761&show=parallel
Accessed: 2026-09-06

Supports:

- Chén Zhěn tells the snake story to the Chǔ general Zhāo Yáng after Zhāo Yáng has defeated Wèi, killed a commander, and taken eight cities.
- The story warns that continuing to attack after already achieving a great victory could cause him to lose what he had gained.

Editorial boundary:

- The children’s article summarizes this larger political and military setting in one sentence rather than introducing every person, title, and state named in the source.
- The article does not present the attendants in the embedded story as documented historical individuals.
