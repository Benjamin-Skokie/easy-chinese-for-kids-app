# Heading North to Go South

## Heading North to Go South

Long ago, a traveler was riding in a carriage when someone by the road called out, “Where are you going?” “To Chu,” the traveler replied. The man pointed behind him. “But Chu is the other way. You’re heading north!” The traveler smiled. “That’s all right. My horse is excellent.” “But your horse is running in the wrong direction.” “I have plenty of money for the journey,” said the traveler. “That won’t help if you’re going the wrong way.” The traveler was still not worried. “My driver is very skilled.” The man stared at him. A fast horse was useful. Plenty of money was useful. A skilled driver was useful. But the better they were, the faster they would carry the traveler away from Chu. The carriage rolled on. This old story gave us the expression **nán yuán běi zhé**—heading south in your plans but north on the road. Today, people use it when someone works hard toward a goal but goes in the wrong direction. Sometimes, going faster does not bring you closer. First, you have to make sure you are going the right way.
