# Editorial References: The Cowherd and the Weaver Girl

This file is for editorial research and does not render on the website.

Accessed: 2026-09-01

Supports:

- The popular family-separation and magpie-bridge story, the meaning and date of Qixi, older star traditions, Qiqiao customs, and the real stars behind the tale.

## 1. The popular children's-story sequence

Source: Ministry of Culture and Tourism of the People's Republic of China, “Qixi: The Origin and Customs of the Traditional Qiqiao Festival”
URL: https://www.mct.gov.cn/whzx/whyw/201112/t20111201_707482.htm

- Gives the familiar modern sequence: the Cowherd and Weaver Girl marry and have children; the Queen Mother takes the Weaver Girl back to heaven; the Cowherd follows but is stopped by the River of Heaven; magpies form a bridge; the couple may meet on the seventh day of the seventh lunar month.
- Describes Qiqiao activities in which girls and women threaded needles, made small objects, and displayed fruit while asking to become skillful.

Editorial boundary:

- The article opens with one clear child-facing version rather than combining every regional version of the folktale.
- The brief conversation, the small home, and the image of the family working side by side are connective storybook details, not historical claims.
- The old ox helps the family rise into the sky without dwelling on the darker cowhide episode found in some versions. This keeps the causal link clear for young children and matches the existing illustrations.

### Why the Weaver Girl visits the human world

Source: Fuzhou Municipal People's Government, “The Qixi Festival Grew from Star Worship and Qiqiao Customs”
URL: https://www.fuzhou.gov.cn/zgfzzt/zjrc/mdfc/msfq/202210/t20221026_4457393.htm

- Gives a version in which the Weaver Girl often looks down at the scenery of the human world, notices the lonely Cowherd, and comes down with other heavenly maidens.

Editorial boundary:

- The story uses this motivation so the Weaver Girl does not arrive in the human world without a reason.
- Versions of the legend differ. Some include bathing and hidden clothing; those details are omitted because they add coercion that is unnecessary for a story aimed at ages six to ten.
- The river meeting is a gentle connective detail chosen to fit the existing illustration and narrative.

## 2. The tale grew over many centuries

Source: Beijing Civilization Office, “Qixi Scenes in Beijing”
URL: https://www.bjwmb.gov.cn/zxfw/wmwx/wskt/t20180817_877616.htm

- Explains that the Cowherd and Weaver Girl first appeared as stars in the *Book of Songs*, before they became lovers in later storytelling.
- Notes later Han-dynasty traditions in which the two became story characters separated by the River of Heaven and magpies made a bridge for their seventh-night meeting.
- Records that later storytellers brought together elements from different periods into fuller versions of the tale.

Editorial boundary:

- The article says, in effect, “in this story” when a picture-book event should not be mistaken for history or astronomy.
- It does not present the present-day family narrative as one unchanged ancient text.

## 3. Qixi means the evening of the seventh day

Source: Central Commission for Discipline Inspection, “The Character Xi: What Evening Is This?”
URL: https://m.ccdi.gov.cn/content/36/84/28914.html

- Explains that 七夕 refers to the evening of the seventh day of the seventh lunar month.
- Connects the date with the Cowherd and Weaver Girl meeting over the River of Heaven and with girls asking the Weaver Girl for skillful hands.

## 4. The stars behind the story

Source: NASA Science, “The Next Full Moon Is a Supermoon; the Sturgeon Moon”
URL: https://moon.nasa.gov/news/196/the-next-full-moon-is-a-supermoon-the-sturgeon-moon/?unit=m

- Identifies Vega with the Weaver Girl and Altair with the Cowherd, with the Milky Way between them.
- Notes that the legend has many versions and that in some of them magpies form the bridge.

Editorial boundary:

- The child-facing article explains the Milky Way as a pale band of stars without asking beginning readers to remember the names Vega and Altair.

## Image note

The four pictures are modern storybook illustrations, not historical reconstructions. They support the selected narrative sequence: the human world and heaven, a family home, the Queen Mother separating the family, and a bridge of magpies. The first and third pictures give the old ox a visible place in the story, so the text briefly explains that it helps the Cowherd follow the Weaver Girl.
