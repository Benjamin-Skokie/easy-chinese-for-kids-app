# Philosophy Articles — Research Notes

This file records the factual checks behind revisions. It is an editorial reference, not child-facing page copy.

## Confucius — checked 2026-08-20

- The exchange between Zigong and Confucius — “有一言而可以终身行之者乎？” / “其恕乎！己所不欲，勿施于人。” — appears in *The Analects*, “Wei Ling Gong” (15.23 in common editions).
  - Chinese Text Project: https://ctext.org/analects/wei-ling-gong/zhs
- The traditional figures of 3,000 students and 72 notable disciples should be presented as later tradition, not as a verifiable enrollment list. Scholarly treatments note that the large counts come from much later biographical traditions and may reflect the growth of Confucius’s authority over time.
  - Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, “Confucius”: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/confucius/
- *The Analects* should not be described as a book personally written by Confucius or simply “compiled by his students immediately after his death.” Its formation and early transmission are historically complex; the page therefore says that sayings and conversations circulated and were later gathered by followers of his thought.
  - Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, “Confucius”: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/confucius/

## Editorial rule for remaining philosophy articles

- Clearly label traditional biographies, symbolic stories, and later interpretations as tradition, legend, or a common explanation where appropriate.
- Avoid presenting a philosophical metaphor as a scientific claim.
- Do not turn yin-yang associations into rigid claims about gender, personality, or moral value.
