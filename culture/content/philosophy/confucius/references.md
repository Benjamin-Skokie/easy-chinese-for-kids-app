# Confucius — References and Editorial Notes

## Zǐgòng’s Question and Confucius’s Answer

Source: *Analects*, “Duke Ling of Wei,” Chinese Text Project
URL: https://ctext.org/analects/wei-ling-gong/zhs
Accessed: 2026-09-06

Supports:

- Zǐgòng asks whether there is one saying that can be practiced throughout a person’s life.
- Confucius answers, “其恕乎！己所不欲，勿施於人。”

Editorial boundary:

- The article presents this as a dialogue preserved in the *Analects* and does not invent a location, time of day, or surrounding action.
- “What you do not want done to yourself, do not do to others” is a concise child-facing translation rather than the only possible scholarly rendering.

## The Traditional Explanation of Shù

Source: *Commentary and Subcommentary on the Analects*, volume 15, Wikisource
URL: https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hant/%E8%AB%96%E8%AA%9E%E8%A8%BB%E7%96%8F/%E5%8D%B715
Accessed: 2026-09-06

Supports:

- The traditional commentary preserves the same exchange and explains shù through not imposing on others what one dislikes for oneself.

Editorial boundary:

- The modern examples about damaging another person’s work or pushing ahead in line explain how the question might be used; they are not scenes from the classical commentary.

## Learning from Other People

Source: *Analects*, “Shu Er,” Chinese Text Project
URL: https://ctext.org/analects/shu-er
Accessed: 2026-09-06

Supports:

- Confucius says that when walking with others he can find teachers among them, following what is good and using what is not good to examine and change himself.

Editorial boundary:

- The article paraphrases the passage and connects it to self-examination without presenting it as part of Zǐgòng’s separate question.

## The Formation of the *Analects*

Source: Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, “Translating and Interpreting Chinese Philosophy”
URL: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/chinese-translate-interpret/
Accessed: 2026-09-06

Supports:

- The *Analects* is a composite collection whose materials and textual form developed after Confucius’s death rather than a book directly written by Confucius himself.

Editorial boundary:

- The child-facing description summarizes a long and complex transmission history without claiming that the present collection was assembled at one known moment by one identified editor.
