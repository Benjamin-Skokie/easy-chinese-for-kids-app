> Difficulty: Easy

# Waiting for a Seed to Grow

## Nothing to See Yet

Ann plants a bean in a flowerpot. She gives it a little water and sets it by a warm window.

The next day, she looks. All she sees is dark soil. On the third day, there is still no green shoot.

“Is something wrong?” Ann wants to dig up the bean and check. But digging could hurt it. She pulls her hand back.

This is a story made for readers today. It is not an event from an ancient book.

---

## What Is Happening Under the Soil?

The flowerpot looks quiet. Under the soil, the bean may be taking in water. Its coat begins to soften. A tiny root usually comes first. The shoot grows upward later.

Seeds need water, air, and the right temperature. Too little water can be a problem, but so can too much. Ann keeps the soil damp without turning it into a puddle.

She cannot order the bean, “Grow right now!” She can give it good care and then give it time.

---

## Helping Is Not Pulling

The *Dao De Jing* is a very old book. It reminds us that a huge tree begins very small and that a long journey begins with one step.

Another old book, the *Mencius*, tells about a man who thought his young plants were growing too slowly. He pulled every shoot higher. The plants wilted.

The man worked hard, but he did not truly help. Care gives a living thing what it needs. Force tries to make it skip a step that cannot be skipped.

---

## A Green Shoot

After some time, Ann sees a green shoot push through the soil. She did not make the bean sprout, but she did not do nothing either.

She gave it water, air, warmth, and care. Then she did not pull on it.

Sometimes real help means doing what is needed—and giving growth enough time.
