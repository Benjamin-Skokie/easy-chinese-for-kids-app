# A Great Tree Starts Small — References and Editorial Notes

This file is for editorial research and does not render on the website.

## The Connected Images in Chapter 64

Source: *Dàodéjīng*, chapter 64, Chinese Text Project
URL: https://ctext.org/text.pl?if=en&node=11655&show=parallel
Accessed: 2026-09-06

Supports:

- Chapter 64 connects the instructions “為之於未有，治之於未亂” with the images of a great tree beginning tiny, a high terrace beginning with earth, and a long journey beginning beneath one’s feet.
- The same chapter includes “慎終如始，則無敗事” and “以輔萬物之自然而不敢為.”

Editorial boundary:

- The tree, terrace, and journey remain philosophical images rather than being presented as botanical, architectural, or travel instructions.
- The loose thread, disagreement, and messy room are child-facing examples, not scenes found in the classical text.
- The English quotations are concise teaching translations, not presented as the only possible scholarly renderings.

## The Mawangdui Text

Source: Mawangdui *Laozi* A, Chinese Text Project
URL: https://ctext.org/text.pl?if=en&node=83015&remap=gb&show=parallel
Accessed: 2026-09-06

Supports:

- An early excavated form of the text preserves the same cluster of images about large things beginning small, a long journey beginning underfoot, careful completion, and assisting the zìrán of things.

Editorial boundary:

- The article uses the familiar received wording in the child-facing text and does not attempt to teach manuscript variants.
- The manuscript evidence supports the age of the connected images without proving one exact author or one moment of composition.

## Zìrán and Avoiding Excessive Force

Source: Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, “Laozi”
URL: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/laozi/
Accessed: 2026-09-06

Supports:

- Zìrán in the *Dàodéjīng* concerns things being or developing in accordance with themselves rather than the modern category of mountains, forests, and wildlife.
- The text repeatedly explores action that avoids excessive, coercive, or unnecessary forcing.

Editorial boundary:

- The article does not turn chapter 64 into a separate lesson on Dao or wuwei.
- Caring for a young tree is an explanatory comparison: it illustrates the difference between support and overcontrol without claiming that every outcome can be guaranteed.
