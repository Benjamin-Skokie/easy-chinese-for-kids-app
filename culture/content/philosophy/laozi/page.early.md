heading_en: Water Meets a Rock

caption_zh: 水分开，绕过石头，再合在一起继续流。
caption_en: Water divides, flows around a rock, and joins together again.
[zh]
把手伸进浅浅的小溪。水从手指旁边滑过去。前面有一块石头，水会停下来吗？

水分成两股，绕过石头，又合在一起。它没有把石头撞倒，却还是找到了向前的路。
[/zh]
[en]
Put your hand in a shallow stream. Water slips between your fingers. Then it reaches a rock. Will the water stop?

The stream divides and flows around both sides. Then it joins together and keeps going. The water does not knock down the rock. It finds another way forward.
[/en]
[/section]

[section]
page: 2
layout: text-only
heading_zh: 水帮助很多生命
heading_en: Water Helps Living Things
[zh]
一本叫《道德经》的古书说，最好的善像水。雨水落进泥土，种子会发芽。动物需要喝水，我们每天也需要水。

水帮助了这么多生命，却不会举起牌子说：“快看，都是我的功劳！”它也总是往低处流，不急着站在最高的地方。

这本书用水来问我们：帮助别人以后，一定要抢着得到表扬吗？
[/zh]
[en]
An old book called the *Dao De Jing* says that the greatest goodness is like water. Rain falls into the soil, and seeds begin to grow. Animals need water. We need it every day too.

Water helps so many living things, but it never holds up a sign that says, “Look! I did everything!” It flows to low places instead of rushing to stand at the top.

The book uses water to ask us a question: When we help, do we always need to grab the praise?
[/en]
[/section]

[section]
page: 3
layout: two-column
heading_zh: 不抢，不是什么都不做
heading_en: Not Grabbing Does Not Mean Doing Nothing
image: 3-3-water-drop-stone.webp
caption_zh: 流动的水、沙粒和时间会慢慢改变石头。
caption_en: Moving water, sand, and time can slowly change a rock.
[zh]
不争，不是比赛时不努力，也不是有人欺负你时只能安静。它是在提醒我们：不要把每一件事都变成“我一定要赢过你”。

大家一起做东西时，有人抢走所有材料，大声命令别人，事情可能反而做不好。另一个人认真听，把大家的好办法放在一起，也是在努力。

像水一样绕过石头，不是认输。水换了一条路，仍然向前。
[/zh]
[en]
“Not competing” does not mean that you cannot try hard in a game. It does not mean staying quiet when someone hurts you. It means that every moment does not have to become “I must beat you.”

When a group makes something, one person might grab all the supplies and shout. That can make the job harder. Another person can listen and help connect everyone's good ideas. That person is working hard too.

Water going around a rock has not given up. It changed direction and kept moving.
[/en]
[/section]

[section]
page: 4
layout: two-column reverse
heading_zh: 一个很紧的鞋带结
heading_en: A Very Tight Knot
image: 3-4-writing-dao-de-jing.webp
caption_zh: 《道德经》用水、山谷和空房间帮助人们思考。
caption_en: The *Dao De Jing* uses water, valleys, and empty rooms to help readers think.
[zh]
鞋带打了一个死结。你使劲向两边拉，结却越来越紧。怎么办？

先停下来，看看哪一根绳子压在下面。轻轻把它松开，可能更快。你并不是什么都没做。你只是没有一直用蛮力。

有时候，最有用的行动并不吵闹。先观察，再找到合适的办法，也是一种力量。
[/zh]
[en]
A shoelace is tied in a tight knot. You pull both ends as hard as you can, but the knot gets tighter. What can you do?

Stop and look. Find the loop that is trapped underneath. Loosen it gently. You are not doing nothing. You are acting without using more force than you need.

The most helpful action is not always the loudest one. Looking first and finding the right way can be a kind of strength.
[/en]
[/section]

[ending]
heading_zh: 再看一次水
heading_en: Look at Water Again
[zh]
我们不知道老子是不是一个能清楚找到的历史人物，也不知道《道德经》是不是一个人一次写完的。不过，两千多年来，人们一直用这本书思考。

下一次看见水流过石头，可以问：我是在解决问题，还是只想赢？我能不能少用一点蛮力，换一条路继续向前？

水不会回答。可是它可以帮助我们提出更好的问题。
[/zh]
[en]
We do not know whether Laozi was one person we can clearly find in history. We also do not know whether one person wrote the whole *Dao De Jing*. But readers have used this little book to think for more than two thousand years.

The next time you see water moving around a rock, ask: Am I solving the problem, or do I only want to win? Can I use less force and find another way forward?

Water will not answer. It may help us ask a better question.
[/en]
[/ending]
