# The 24 Solar Terms: How Chinese People Made Friends with Nature

## A Calendar Written by the Sun

The traditional Chinese calendar is not the same as the calendar you usually see on a phone.

It watches both the moon and the sun.

The moon helps mark the months. The sun helps mark the solar terms.

Long ago, people divided the sun's path through the year into 24 parts. Each part became one solar term.

Beginning of Spring, Rain Water, Awakening of Insects, Spring Equinox, Qingming, Grain Rain: the names sound like a string of short poems.

They are about more than temperature.

They tell what the land is doing at that time of year.

---

## Awakening of Insects: The Bugs Wake Up

Around March each year, the weather begins to warm. Spring thunder also becomes more common.

Of course, insects do not wake up just because they hear one clap of thunder.

More important things are changing: the ground is warmer, and the air and soil have more moisture.

Ancient farmers noticed that around this time, insects that had hidden through winter began to move again. So they gave this solar term the name Awakening of Insects.

The name feels as if spring is calling even the tiny creatures under the ground to wake up.

---

## Grain Rain: Planting Melons and Beans

The name Grain Rain means "rain helps many grains grow."

Around this solar term, many places have more rain, and the air grows warmer. It is a good time to plant melons, sow beans, and raise rice seedlings.

Old farmers did not judge the season only by a date written on paper.

They looked at the mud in their hands, the clouds in the sky, and the dampness under their feet.

That is what makes the solar terms useful. They are not empty words. They are tied to real work in the fields.

---

## White Dew and Frost's Descent: Two Levels of Cool

White Dew is a thin, gentle kind of autumn coolness.

After sunset, water vapor in the fields becomes tiny drops of dew. If you step into a field early in the morning, the cool feeling can climb up from your ankles.

About a month later comes Frost's Descent. Now the coolness is heavier.

In many places, people use this time to finish harvest work, store vegetables, dry persimmons, and get ready for winter.

White Dew and Frost's Descent are like the first half and second half of an autumn sentence.

---

## Winter Solstice: The Coldest Day, the Warmest Kitchen

Winter Solstice is the time of year when the day is shortest and the night is longest.

In many northern places, people eat dumplings. In many southern places, people eat tangyuan, sweet round rice balls. Some families make soup, cook lamb, or gather around a steaming pot for the evening.

After Winter Solstice, the days slowly begin to grow longer again.

So even though it is cold, it also seems to whisper:

Spring is already on its way back.

---

## Summary

The 24 solar terms are not a modern weather forecast. But they are like a diary kept by the land.

Awakening of Insects, Grain Rain, White Dew, Frost's Descent, and Winter Solstice: say the names, and you can almost picture bugs, rain, dew, persimmons, and a warm kitchen full of steam.

This is how people long ago watched the sky, worked the fields, and turned the year into 24 breathing moments.
