# Great Wall Shared Story and Facts Brief

## Core ideas and evidence

1. The Great Wall is a family of walls, trenches, beacon stations, passes, and forts built, reused, abandoned, and changed by many governments. It is not one continuous wall.
2. The 2012 survey's 21,196.18 kilometers is a combined heritage total, not one walkable line. The Wall is not visible to unaided eyes from the Moon and is usually hard to distinguish from low orbit.
3. Warring States walls had different routes and purposes. Qin reused and extended some northern defenses and removed some internal walls; it did not invent or connect every wall.
4. Builders used local materials. Han desert systems could use earth, gravel, reeds, and red willow. Famous Beijing visitor walls are mainly Ming layers.
5. Beacon systems sent limited prearranged warnings and depended on fuel, visibility, staffed stations, and sometimes mounted messages. `Wolf smoke` does not prove universal wolf dung.
6. Frontier life included defense, warfare, trade, diplomacy, migration, and communities that changed across time.
7. Construction required enormous labor. Meng Jiangnu is a legend that grew over centuries, not a documented Qin event, but it carries memory of grief and cost.

## Boundaries

- Every image is a modern Ming-style or symbolic scene, not a named reconstruction.
- Avoid permanent `inside/outside` ethnic groups across two thousand years.
- Do not promise continuous routes, universal materials, or fixed signal speeds.
- Admire engineering without hiding labor and suffering.

## Level assignments

- Early Reader: replace the one-wall picture with several forms, explain layers, beacons, gates, markets, and workers.
- Story: add survey length, space myth, Warring States/Qin/Han/Ming layers, signal limits, border exchange, and Meng Jiangnu.
- Advanced: preserve dates, materials, administrative functions, changing political communities, and all evidence cautions.
