[section]
page: 1
layout: text-wrap-image
heading_zh: 不是一条完整石龙
heading_en: Not One Unbroken Stone Dragon
image: 1-1-stone-dragon-ridge.webp
caption_zh: 北京山地的明长城风格，不代表所有地方和时代。
caption_en: This imagined Ming-style Wall near Beijing does not represent every place or period.
[zh]
北京附近的砖墙和敌楼太有名，让人以为长城到处都这样。甘肃戈壁里的残土墙、壕沟、烽燧和关堡却也属于长城。

长城不是一条连续墙，也不是一个朝代一次建完。2012年官方调查把历代墙体、壕堑等线性遗存合计为21,196.18千米，还记录烽燧、关堡等设施。这个总数不表示有人能沿一条完整墙走完。

肉眼也不能从月球看见长城。即使在较低地球轨道，它通常也很难分辨，因为窄而且颜色接近地面。长城不需要太空传说来证明伟大。
[/zh]
[en]
Brick walls and towers near Beijing are so famous that many people imagine the entire Great Wall that way. Yet a worn earthen ridge, trench, beacon mound, pass, or fort in the Gansu desert also belongs to its history.

The Great Wall is not one continuous structure, and one dynasty did not build it at once. A 2012 official survey counted 21,196.18 kilometers—13,170 miles—of recognized walls, trenches, and other linear remains from many periods, plus related stations, passes, and forts. The total is not one complete path anyone can walk.

The Wall is not visible to unaided eyes from the Moon. It is usually hard to distinguish even from low Earth orbit because it is narrow and similar in color to the land. The Great Wall does not need a space myth to be extraordinary.
[/en]
[/section]

[section]
page: 2
layout: text-wrap-image
heading_zh: 战国、秦与不同方向的墙
heading_en: Warring States, Qin, and Walls Facing Many Ways
image: 1-2-building-frontier-wall.webp
caption_zh: 现代砖石施工图更像后世，不是秦代现场复原。
caption_en: This modern masonry scene looks later. It does not reconstruct Qin building.
[zh]
战国时期，楚、齐、魏、赵、燕、秦等国修过各自的墙。方向不同，彼此不连；有的防北方骑马人群，有的防邻国。最早的墙不是一道简单的“农民对游牧人”边界。

公元前221年秦始皇统一后，拆除一些内地旧墙，在北方利用、修整和连接秦、赵、燕部分防线，也建新段。秦常用当地泥土夯筑，路线也不同于明长城。

士兵和征发劳工要挖土、夯实、搬运，还要在边地得到粮食和工具。历史保存皇帝将军的名字，却很少记住普通修墙人。
[/zh]
[en]
During the Warring States period, Chu, Qi, Wei, Zhao, Yan, Qin, and other states built walls. They faced different directions and did not join into one line. Some guarded against northern riders; others defended one state from another. The earliest walls were not a simple border between farmers and nomads.

After unification in 221 BCE, the First Emperor ordered some internal walls removed. In the north, the Qin government reused and repaired parts of older Qin, Zhao, and Yan lines and built new sections. Qin builders often rammed local earth, and their routes did not match the Ming Wall.

Soldiers and drafted laborers dug, pounded, and carried materials while supplies had to reach distant frontiers. Histories saved emperors' and generals' names far more often than workers' lives.
[/en]
[/section]

[section]
page: 3
layout: text-only
heading_zh: 汉代向西，明代上山
heading_en: Han Defenses West, Ming Walls on the Mountains
[zh]
汉代防线向西延伸到河西走廊和戈壁，帮助守卫道路、屯田、传警和管理往来。缺少石料的地方用夯土、砂砾、芦苇和红柳。它不像北京砖墙，却同样属于长城史。

北魏、北齐、隋、金等政权后来也修建或使用类似防线。不是一个固定民族永远在墙内、另一个永远在墙外；王朝、人群称呼和政治关系一直改变。

明代从1368到1644年，又大规模加强北方防务。在关口和北京附近山地，砖石墙、敌楼、城堡和多道防线出现。八达岭、慕田峪主要展示这层保存较好的明长城。
[/zh]
[en]
Han frontier systems extended west through the Hexi Corridor and into desert country. They helped guard routes, support military farms, send warnings, and manage movement. Where stone was scarce, builders used packed earth, gravel, reeds, and red willow. The remains look unlike Beijing brickwork but belong to the same history.

Northern Wei, Northern Qi, Sui, Jin, and other governments later built or reused frontier defenses. One fixed people did not remain forever “inside” while another remained “outside.” Dynasties, group names, and political relationships kept changing.

From 1368 to 1644, the Ming again built on a large scale. Brick and stone strengthened mountain passes and areas near Beijing, with towers, forts, and several defensive lines. Badaling and Mutianyu mainly display this well-preserved Ming layer.
[/en]
[/section]

[section]
page: 4
layout: text-wrap-image
heading_zh: 烽火能说多少话？
heading_en: How Much Could a Beacon Say?
image: 1-3-beacon-tower-smoke.webp
caption_zh: 现代想象的烽火接力，信号、燃料和速度会随条件改变。
caption_en: This imagined beacon relay cannot show the signals, fuel, or speed of every system.
[zh]
墙不能自己看见危险。守军、道路、关口、城堡和能互相望见的烽燧让防线运转。白天可以用烟和旗，夜里用火，不同时代还用鼓或炮声。

信号只能传预先约定的短消息，例如发现敌情和大概规模。“狼烟”不表示每处都烧狼粪。各地燃料和规则不同。

大雨、湿柴、风沙和漏接都会让信号失败，守军还要骑马送文书。真实速度看能见度、距离和人员，不能保证几小时从任何边境直达首都。
[/zh]
[en]
A wall cannot see danger. Guards, roads, passes, forts, and beacon stations within sight of one another made the defense work. Smoke and flags could signal by day, fire by night, while some periods also used drums or cannon blasts.

Signals carried short, prearranged messages such as a warning and perhaps the rough size of a threat. The term “wolf smoke” does not prove that every station burned wolf dung. Fuel and rules varied.

Rain, wet fuel, dust, and missed relays could break the chain, so riders carried written messages too. Speed depended on sightlines, distance, and staffed stations. No system guaranteed news from every border point to the capital within hours.
[/en]
[/section]

[section]
page: 5
layout: text-wrap-image
heading_zh: 战争旁边也有市场
heading_en: Markets Beside the Battlements
image: 1-4-soldier-night-watch-wall.webp
caption_zh: 现代想象的守军和山谷村落，不复原一次真实巡逻。
caption_en: This imagined guard and valley village do not reconstruct a real patrol.
[zh]
长城可以减慢骑兵、把出入口集中到关口、给守军准备时间，却不是密封盒子。军队能攻破、绕过，防线也会因缺人少粮失去作用。

关口也会开放。和平或议和时期，边口市场交换马、牲畜、粮食、布和日用品。使者、商人、士兵和迁居者来往。战争、贸易、结盟和移民会在同一边地发生。

两边不是永不相见的世界。军户、农民、牧民、商贩和家庭曾经冲突，也一起生活，交换食物、语言和技术。
[/zh]
[en]
The Wall could slow mounted forces, move crossings toward guarded gates, and buy preparation time. It was never a sealed box. Armies broke through, went around, or found lines weakened by too few soldiers and supplies.

Gates opened as well as closed. During peace agreements, border markets traded horses, livestock, grain, cloth, and daily goods. Envoys, merchants, soldiers, and migrants passed through. War, trade, alliance, and migration could share the same frontier.

The two sides were not worlds that never met. Military families, farmers, herders, traders, and children fought, lived together, and exchanged food, language, and skills.
[/en]
[/section]

[ending]
heading_zh: 谁的伟大，谁的代价？
heading_en: Whose Achievement, and Whose Cost?
[zh]
长城展示测量、选址、夯筑和组织能力，也留下劳役与死亡的记忆。孟姜女哭倒秦长城、找到丈夫遗骨不是可查证事件。这个传说从更早的哭夫故事经过几百年变化，才和秦始皇、长城连起来。

传说不是事实，却能保存人对真实经历的感受。她的眼泪问：谁搬土？谁离开家庭？谁承担风险？欣赏工程和记住工人代价并不矛盾。

长城不是切开历史的石龙，而是一部由土、砖、烽火、关门和人群往来写成的边疆史。
[/zh]
[en]
The Great Wall displays remarkable surveying, site choice, building, and organization. It also carries memories of forced labor and death. Meng Jiangnu's tears bringing down a Qin wall and uncovering her husband's bones are not a documented event. The legend grew from an older mourning-wife story and became attached to the First Emperor and the Wall centuries later.

A legend is not a fact, but it can preserve feelings about real experience. Her tears ask who carried the earth, left family, and faced danger. Admiring the engineering and remembering workers' cost are not opposites.

The Great Wall is not a stone dragon cutting history in two. It is frontier history written in earth, brick, warning fires, gates, and human movement.
[/en]
[/ending]
