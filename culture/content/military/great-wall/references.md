# The Great Wall — References and Editorial Notes

This file is for editorial research and does not render on the website.

## What “the Great Wall” Includes and How Length Is Counted

Source: Xinhua, “万里长城有多长？长度为何难测量？,” hosted by Tianjin Municipal Culture and Tourism Bureau
URL: https://whly.tj.gov.cn/tjswlzxw/sy1/rmzx/202312/t20231203_6471416.html
Accessed: 2026-08-20

Source: UNESCO World Heritage Centre, “The Great Wall”
URL: https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/438/
Accessed: 2026-08-20

Supports:

- China’s 2012 heritage survey reported a combined 21,196.18 kilometers of recognized Great Wall remains from multiple periods.
- The survey measured recognized walls, trenches, and other linear remains while also recording fortresses, passes, beacon towers, and related features as parts of a complex heritage system rather than one continuous wall.
- The commonly cited Ming total is 8,851.8 kilometers; different totals depend on dynasty, heritage definition, surviving evidence, and measuring rules.

Editorial boundary:

- The article gives the 2012 figure as an official survey result, not an eternal or perfectly complete measurement. The Xinhua report notes that definitions and future discoveries may change totals.
- Do not describe the full 21,196.18 kilometers as a continuous route from Shanhaiguan to Jiayuguan or compare the summed remains directly to one trip around Earth.
- UNESCO’s older World Heritage criterion text repeats the claim that the Wall can be seen from the Moon. NASA evidence below contradicts that legacy statement, so it is not used.

## The View from Space

Source: NASA, “Great Wall”
URL: https://www.nasa.gov/image-article/great-wall/
Accessed: 2026-08-20

Source: NASA, “China’s Wall Less Great in View from Space”
URL: https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/history/PDFs/great_wall_less_great_from_space.pdf
Accessed: 2026-08-20

Supports:

- The Great Wall is not visible to the unaided eye from the Moon.
- It is generally difficult or impossible to distinguish with the unaided eye even from low Earth orbit because it is narrow and similar in color and texture to its surroundings.
- Some sections can appear in images made under favorable conditions or with long lenses, radar, or other instruments.

Editorial boundary:

- Do not replace one myth with “the Wall can never be photographed from space.” Instrument-assisted and favorable-condition images exist.
- The article says the unaided-eye Moon claim is false and leaves the qualified orbital explanation intact.

## Warring States and Qin Walls

Source: Zhengzhou Municipal Cultural Heritage Bureau, “魏长城”
URL: https://wwj.zhengzhou.gov.cn/country/3174937.jhtml
Accessed: 2026-08-20

Source: Metropolitan Museum of Art, “Qin Dynasty (221–206 B.C.)”
URL: https://www.metmuseum.org/de/essays/qin-dynasty-221-206-b-c
Accessed: 2026-08-20

Source: National Ethnic Affairs Commission, “你绝对想不到：中国最早的长城竟然在河南！”
URL: https://www.neac.gov.cn/seac/c103391/202301/1160021.shtml
Accessed: 2026-08-20

Source: “Survey and Research of the Qin Great Wall of the Warring States Period,” *China Cultural Heritage Scientific Research*, hosted by Shaanxi Normal University
URL: https://lishiwenhua.snnu.edu.cn/__local/7/C0/99/6943304A4E70E00E236779B3EFF_088C40F9_144560.pdf?e=.pdf
Accessed: 2026-08-20

Supports:

- Several Warring States built walls with different routes and purposes, including defense against rival states as well as northern groups.
- After unification, the Qin government joined and expanded parts of preexisting northern defenses.
- The Qin government also dismantled or abandoned walls that had separated the former rival states inside the new empire.
- Archaeological research shows that some remains were reused, repaired, or rebuilt across Warring States Qin, imperial Qin, and early Han periods; simple one-date labels can hide this layering.

Editorial boundary:

- Do not say Qin Shi Huang invented the Great Wall or connected every earlier state wall.
- Do not present all early walls as a single farming-versus-nomad border.
- The exact routes and dates of some early sections remain debated or incompletely surveyed.
- `1-2-building-frontier-wall.webp` is a modern imagined, Ming-looking masonry scene. It must not illustrate Qin construction as a literal reconstruction.

## Han Frontier Systems, Materials, and Routes

Source: Gansu Culture and Tourism, “源远流长的甘肃长城文化”
URL: https://www.gswbj.gov.cn/a/2024/02/01/19649.html
Accessed: 2026-08-20

Source: Hong Kong and Macao Work Office of the CPC Central Committee, “世界文化遗产——长城”
URL: https://www.locpg.gov.cn/tp/2014-01/04/c_125957087.htm
Accessed: 2026-08-20

Supports:

- Han defenses extended through the Hexi Corridor and desert regions, where walls, beacon stations, forts, and routes formed a system.
- Builders used local materials, including rammed earth, sand, gravel, reeds, red willow, and other plant layers in arid regions.
- Some Han lines were discontinuous, with beacon stations or forts operating where no continuous wall was necessary.

Editorial boundary:

- Do not make all Han walls sound identical or claim that every western route was built solely to “protect the Silk Road.” Defense, military supply, settlement, communication, and control of travel overlapped.
- Surviving earthen remains should not be judged as unfinished versions of Ming brick walls.

## Ming Construction and the Familiar Tourist Image

Source: Beijing Mentougou District People’s Government, “军事遗址——京西长城”
URL: https://www.bjmtg.gov.cn/bjmtg/zwxx/qdaj/201702/1015759.shtml
Accessed: 2026-08-20

Source: Beijing Municipal Cultural Heritage Bureau, *Badaling Great Wall Conservation Plan*
URL: https://wwj.beijing.gov.cn/bjww/resource/cms/article/bjww_482911/325986292/2023060116522338710.pdf
Accessed: 2026-08-20

Supports:

- Many prominent Beijing-area remains are Ming walls built or strengthened with stone, brick, packed-earth cores, watchtowers, beacon stations, and forts.
- Width, height, materials, and tower placement vary even within one famous area.
- Badaling preserves Ming wall systems and also has earlier Northern Qi remains.

Editorial boundary:

- The article does not treat Badaling or Mutianyu as the appearance of all Great Wall remains.
- Claims such as “five horses abreast” describe certain wide Ming sections, not the Wall everywhere, and are omitted from the general story.
- `1-1-stone-dragon-ridge.webp`, `1-3-beacon-tower-smoke.webp`, and `1-4-soldier-night-watch-wall.webp` are modern imagined Ming-style scenes, not photographs or exact reconstructions of named sites.

## Beacon Signals and Their Limits

Source: Beijing Municipal Cultural Heritage Bureau, “中国古代信息传递方式”
URL: https://wwj.beijing.gov.cn/bjww/wwjzzcslm/1731063/1731073/1731074/1728054/index.html
Accessed: 2026-08-20

Source: Beijing Civilization Office, “烽火台如何传递军情”
URL: https://www.bjwmb.gov.cn/zxfw/wmwx/wskt/t20181123_916550.htm
Accessed: 2026-08-20

Source: Tianjin University News, “北京日报：19分钟可‘飞越’天津长城”
URL: https://news.tju.edu.cn/info/1005/301819.htm
Accessed: 2026-08-20

Supports:

- Beacon systems used prearranged combinations of smoke, fire, flags, suspended baskets, drums, and—in Ming examples—cannon blasts.
- Signals could encode limited information such as the direction or scale of danger.
- Weather, wet fuel, blocked sightlines, missed relays, or uncertainty could require mounted written messages.
- Scholarship and public history sources question the simple claim that “wolf smoke” proves universal burning of wolf dung; Ming smoke/fire facilities could also stand apart from occupied towers for safety.

Editorial boundary:

- Do not say wolf dung always produced straight smoke that wind could not bend.
- Do not identify every watchtower or enemy tower as a beacon tower; functions and placements differed.
- Do not promise that news could travel from any border point to the capital in a few hours. Speed depended on the specific system and conditions.

## Border Trade and Multiethnic Interaction

Source: Renmin University of China Institute of Qing History, “长城边口贸易” research summary
URL: https://iqh.ruc.edu.cn/kxyj/kydt/1b3da8336fa04155b23cd3acc4eb9b52.htm
Accessed: 2026-08-20

Source: National Ethnic Affairs Commission, “长城：世界语境的中国符号”
URL: https://www.neac.gov.cn/seac/c103391/202210/1158975.shtml
Accessed: 2026-08-20

Source: UNESCO World Heritage Centre, “The Great Wall”
URL: https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/438/
Accessed: 2026-08-20

Supports:

- Border gates and markets supported trade among farming, pastoral, and other frontier communities during periods of agreement.
- The Great Wall region saw both conflict and exchange, not permanent isolation.
- Different dynasties, political powers, and ethnic communities built, guarded, crossed, traded near, or lived alongside Great Wall systems.

Editorial boundary:

- The article avoids treating “Chinese” and “nomads” as two permanent, uniform sides across two thousand years.
- Trade did not erase warfare or coercive border control; warfare did not end trade, diplomacy, migration, and cultural exchange.
- The list of goods is illustrative of documented border commerce and is not tied to every gate or dynasty.

## Labor, Suffering, and the Meng Jiangnu Legend

Source: University of Science and Technology Beijing, “秦汉时期北方边境防御墙建设的劳动力投入研究” research summary
URL: https://ihmm.ustb.edu.cn/kxyj/kycg/xslw/yjycls/5dd00f9b523347ef9be8530ac3f3d2fb.htm
Accessed: 2026-08-20

Source: Zhao Shiyu, “传说·历史·历史记忆——从20世纪的新史学到后现代史学,” East China Normal University
URL: https://www.clhm.ecnu.edu.cn/_upload/article/files/8c/2f/6b37fc1a49269e5f1fba947eaebc/65f5ac3f-f969-4e14-9bbd-668428845fc6.pdf
Accessed: 2026-08-20

Source: Peking University journal platform, “孟姜女不姓孟——说古人称行第的习俗”
URL: https://ccj.pku.edu.cn/article/info?id=286658599
Accessed: 2026-08-20

Supports:

- Qin-Han frontier construction demanded organized labor and worked together with military campaigning, state migration, and other large projects to draw heavily on people and supplies.
- The Meng Jiangnu legend developed from earlier accounts of Qiliang’s mourning wife.
- The wall-collapse motif and later attachment to the Great Wall and Qin First Emperor grew over centuries.
- The legend became a cultural way to remember grief and the human cost of large state projects.

Editorial boundary:

- Meng Jiangnu is not presented as a documented Qin woman, and her husband is not claimed to have been found beneath a historical wall section.
- The article does not invent one named worker’s death, daily mileage, food, sleeping conditions, or private thoughts.
- The legend is used as evidence of cultural memory, not as evidence that crying physically destroyed a wall.
