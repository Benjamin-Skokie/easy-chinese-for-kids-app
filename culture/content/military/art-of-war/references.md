# The Art of War — References and Editorial Notes

This file is for editorial research and does not render on the website.

## The Received Text: War, Comparison, and Deception

Source: Chinese Text Project, *The Art of War*, “Laying Plans”
URL: https://ctext.org/art-of-war/laying-plans/zh
Accessed: 2026-08-20

Supports:

- The opening statement “兵者，国之大事，死生之地，存亡之道，不可不察也.”
- The five headings “道、天、地、将、法” and the instruction to compare conditions before war.
- The broader text’s military setting and its use of deception; the article must not present it as a general children’s self-help book.

Editorial boundary:

- The tabletop terrain model is an imaginative illustration, not an ancient teaching scene documented in the text.
- The child-facing explanations of the five headings are concise paraphrases. They do not claim that each heading has only one uncontested English equivalent.

## Knowing Both Sides and Avoiding Costly Battle

Source: Chinese Text Project, *The Art of War*, “Attack by Stratagem”
URL: https://ctext.org/art-of-war/attack-by-stratagem/zh
Accessed: 2026-08-20

Supports:

- “知彼知己，百战不殆” appears in the received text in that order.
- The surrounding passage discusses knowing when to fight, relative forces, readiness, and command.
- “百战百胜，非善之善者也；不战而屈人之兵，善之善者也.”
- The chapter ranks attacking plans and alliances ahead of armies and treats attacking walled cities as the worst option because of time, equipment, and casualties.

Editorial boundary:

- Do not reverse the wording to “知己知彼” and then build a lesson from the changed order.
- “百战不殆” is not rendered as an absolute promise to win every battle. “殆” is explained through danger or peril.
- “不战而屈人之兵” remains an act of military coercion. It is not converted into a story about shops driving competitors out of business, popularity in a class election, friendship, or modern conflict resolution.
- The sports example is explicitly limited to preparation under agreed rules; it does not equate games with war.

## Water and Adaptation

Source: Chinese Text Project, *The Art of War*, “Weak Points and Strong”
URL: https://ctext.org/art-of-war/weak-points-and-strong/zh
Accessed: 2026-08-20

Supports:

- “兵形象水,” “避实而击虚,” and “水因地而制流，兵因敌而制胜.”
- The idea that neither water nor military conditions keeps one constant shape.

Editorial boundary:

- The riverbank figures are imaginative and are not identified as historical portraits of Sun Wu.
- The source does not say that water never uses force, that nothing can stop it, that it always goes around every obstacle, or that it must reach the sea. Those claims from the old draft were removed.
- “Try another method when the first one is stuck” is a modern application, clearly separated from the military teaching and limited by ethics and rules.

## The Five Dangers of a Commander

Source: Chinese Text Project, *The Art of War*, “Variation in Tactics”
URL: https://ctext.org/art-of-war/variation-in-tactics/zh
Accessed: 2026-08-20

Supports:

- The received list “必死可杀，必生可虏，忿速可侮，廉洁可辱，爱民可烦.”
- The text identifies these as five dangers or failings that can bring disaster to a commander and army.

Editorial boundary:

- Exact interpretation of compact phrases such as “廉洁可辱” and “爱民可烦” varies among translators and commentators. The article notes this instead of pretending that the old draft’s “too proud” and “too soft-hearted” are exact definitions.
- The modern explanation concerns traits pushed to an exploitable extreme. It does not claim that courage, self-preservation, honor, or concern for soldiers is inherently wrong.

## Traditional Biography and Modern Authorship Debate

Source: Sima Qian, *Records of the Grand Historian*, “Biographies of Sunzi and Wu Qi,” Chinese Text Project
URL: https://ctext.org/shiji/sun-zi-wu-qi-lie-zhuan/zh
Accessed: 2026-08-20

Supports:

- The later traditional account identifying Sun Wu, connecting him with King Helü of Wu, and describing thirteen chapters of military teaching.

Source: Peter Lorge, *Sun Tzu in the West*, introduction and Chapter 1, Cambridge University Press
URL: https://assets.cambridge.org/97811088/30652/excerpt/9781108830652_excerpt.pdf
Chapter record: https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/sun-tzu-in-the-west/brief-history-of-sunzi-in-china/7D72E0D2B07EE4C01679C56D821A0436
Accessed: 2026-08-20

Source: Scott Boorman, *Three Faces of Sun Tzu*, “Background: Historical and Textual,” Cambridge University Press
URL: https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/three-faces-of-sun-tzu/background-historical-and-textual/9198C86AED57CA34F1BE7831683310DC
Accessed: 2026-08-20

Supports:

- Sima Qian’s biography is a later literary and historiographic source rather than a contemporary eyewitness record.
- Major modern scholarship questions the traditional biography and treats the received text as developing through a military intellectual tradition during the Warring States era.
- An accretional or multi-compiler model is a serious scholarly position; the history should not be reduced to “Sun Wu wrote the book 2,500 years ago.”

Editorial boundary:

- The article says that scholars disagree and that many favor gradual formation. It does not claim that every scholar accepts one conclusion about Sun Wu’s historicity or every chapter’s exact date.
- The dramatic palace-women training story is omitted. If added later, it must be labeled as Sima Qian’s much later traditional account, not a verified eyewitness scene.

## Yinqueshan Bamboo Manuscripts

Source: National Museum of China, “Yinqueshan *Art of War* Bamboo Slips”
URL: https://www.chnmuseum.cn/zp/zpml/kgfjp/202108/t20210806_250983.shtml
Accessed: 2026-08-20

Source: National Archives Administration of China, “Yinqueshan Han Tomb Bamboo Slips” nomination dossier
URL: https://www.saac.gov.cn/mowcn/cn/6sbs/202512/d58b9dd7d61e4344a7a5c0a1699011f6.shtml
Accessed: 2026-08-20

Supports:

- The bamboo slips were excavated in 1972 from early Western Han tombs at Yinqueshan in Linyi, Shandong.
- The manuscripts include both *The Art of War* associated with Sun Wu and the distinct *Sun Bin’s Art of War*.
- The manuscripts were copied more than 2,000 years ago and provide early physical evidence for the transmission of both texts.

Editorial boundary:

- The discovery demonstrates separate early transmission of the two works and supplies an early manuscript witness. It does not by itself settle every question about Sun Wu’s biography, single authorship, or the date at which every chapter first formed.
- The ending’s anonymous copyist brushing text onto bamboo is a general description of manuscript production, not an identified historical person or reconstructed moment.

## Modern Illustrations and Ethical Boundary

The hide-and-seek, sports, tabletop model, wooden puzzle, and everyday problem-solving passages are modern illustrative scenes. None is presented as an event from *The Art of War* or as proof of the text’s historical origin.

The article intentionally states that military deception and coercion cannot be transferred directly into friendship, school, family life, or ordinary competition. The text includes warfare, deception, spies, and fire attacks; preserving that context prevents selective quotations from turning a military classic into a harmless collection of motivational slogans.
