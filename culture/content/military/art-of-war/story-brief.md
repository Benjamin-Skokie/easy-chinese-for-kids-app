# The Art of War Shared Story and Facts Brief

## Core ideas and evidence

1. The work treats war as a matter of life, death, survival, and ruin that demands close study. Modern games are limited analogies, not small wars.
2. Tradition attributes the book to Sun Wu. Sima Qian wrote the biography centuries later. Many scholars favor gradual formation within a Warring States military tradition; disagreement remains.
3. The Yinqueshan Han tombs preserved bamboo copies of both *The Art of War* and the distinct *Sun Bin's Art of War*, proving early transmission but not settling authorship.
4. The opening compares unity, timing and weather, terrain, leadership, and organization before action.
5. `Know the other side and yourself` concerns avoiding peril through evidence and comparison, not guaranteed victory or mind-reading.
6. `Winning without fighting` remains coercion in a military setting. It is not modern kindness. Sieges are ranked last because of cost.
7. Water illustrates adaptation to conditions, not a rule that water always goes around, never uses force, or must reach the sea.
8. The commander's five dangers warn about traits pushed to exploitable extremes. The book remains a work about war, deception, spies, and force.

## Boundaries

- Label every game, sport, map, puzzle, and stream scene as a modern analogy or imagined picture.
- Do not carry military deception into friendship, family, or school as advice.
- Do not promise victory or turn the book into gentle self-help.
- Preserve authorship uncertainty and translation limits.

## Level assignments

- Early Reader: focus on looking before moving, checking information, changing a stuck method, and asking who bears the cost, with a clear war-versus-game boundary.
- Story: add authorship, bamboo manuscripts, the five comparisons, knowledge, siege cost, water, and character dangers.
- Advanced: retain original military context, textual quotations, scholarly debate, ethical limits, and translation nuance.
