[section]
page: 1
layout: text-wrap-image
heading_zh: 捉迷藏高手都知道的事
heading_en: What the Best Hide-and-Seek Players Know
image: 2-1-hide-and-seek-strategy.webp
caption_zh: 最会捉迷藏的人，不是跑得最快，而是最会想的人
caption_en: The best hide-and-seek player is not always the fastest. It is the one who thinks best.
[zh]
你玩过捉迷藏吗？

最厉害的那个人，不是跑得最快的，也不是力气最大的。是那个最了解房子的人 - 他知道衣柜后面可以藏，窗帘后面藏不住，楼梯下面的小隔间从来没有人找过。

他还知道另一件事：猜别人会去哪里。小明每次都躲在床底下，小红喜欢藏在门后面。你要是知道他们爱藏哪儿，你就能找到他们。

两千五百年前，一个叫孙武的人 - 大家叫他孙子 - 写了一本书，叫《孙子兵法》。这本书讲的，就是"怎么像捉迷藏高手一样思考"。只不过他不是在讲游戏，他是在讲打仗。
[/zh]
[en]
Have you ever played hide-and-seek?

The best player is not always the fastest one. It is not always the strongest one either. It is the person who understands the house best.

That player knows you can hide behind the wardrobe. The curtain is not a good hiding place. The tiny space under the stairs? Nobody ever checks there.

That player also knows how to guess where other people will go. Xiaoming always hides under the bed. Xiaohong likes hiding behind the door. If you know their habits, you can find them faster.

About 2,500 years ago, a man named Sun Wu, also called Sunzi, wrote a book called *The Art of War*. In a way, the book teaches people to think like a hide-and-seek master. But Sunzi was not writing about a game. He was writing about war.
[/en]
[/section]

[section]
page: 2
layout: text-wrap-image
heading_zh: 先知道你自己，再去看别人
heading_en: Know Yourself First, Then Look at Others
image: 2-2-know-yourself-map.webp
caption_zh: 先看清自己，再去观察对手，是孙子最重要的提醒之一
caption_en: One of Sunzi's biggest lessons: understand yourself, then study the other side.
[zh]
孙子说了一句后来很多人都知道的话：

知彼知己，百战不殆。

"知"是知道。"己"是自己。"彼"是对方。知道你和你对手的所有情况 - 你就不会输。但孙子接着说：如果你只了解自己、不了解对手，你赢一半、输一半。如果你连自己都不了解 - 每次都输。

注意了 - 他先说"知己"，然后才说"知彼"。先搞清楚你自己是谁、你擅长什么、你怕什么。然后才去看对手。就像打球之前，先看看自己的体能和手感，再去观察对方球队。
[/zh]
[en]
Sunzi said one sentence that many people still know today:

Know the other side and know yourself, and you will not be in danger through a hundred battles.

*Know* means understand. *Self* means you. *The other side* means your opponent. If you understand both yourself and the other side, you are much harder to defeat.

Then Sunzi gives a sharper warning. If you understand only yourself but not the other side, you may win sometimes and lose sometimes. If you do not even understand yourself, you may lose again and again.

So start with yourself. What are you good at? What are you afraid of? Where are you weak? After that, study the other side.

It is like getting ready for a game. First check your own energy and your own skills. Then look at the other team.
[/en]
[/section]

[section]
page: 3
layout: text-wrap-image
heading_zh: 最好的赢，是不用打就赢了
heading_en: The Best Win May Need No Fight
image: 2-3-winning-without-fight.webp
caption_zh: 最聪明的赢法，不一定靠拳头，而是靠更高明的办法
caption_en: The smartest win does not always come from fists. Sometimes it comes from a better plan.
[zh]
孙子说过一句让人想很久的话：最厉害的将军，不用打仗就能赢。

"不战而屈人之兵" - 不用打，对手自己就觉得赢不了。这是怎么回事？

两个商店卖一样的东西。一家店服务好、价钱公道，客人越来越多。另一家店的客人越来越少，最后自己关门了。第一家店没有去"攻打"第二家 - 它赢了。班上有个同学成绩好、帮人讲题、谁都服他。他不用喊"选我当班长" - 大家自然选他。

孙子的意思是：用智慧赢，比用拳头赢高级得多。
[/zh]
[en]
Sunzi said something that makes people think for a long time: the best general can win without even fighting.

"Win without battle" means the other side already knows it cannot win. The fight ends before it really begins.

How could that happen?

Imagine two shops selling the same things. One shop is kind to customers and has fair prices. More and more people go there. The other shop gets fewer customers and finally closes. The first shop did not attack the second shop, but it still won.

Or think about a student in class. She studies well, helps others with hard questions, and earns everyone's trust. She does not have to shout, "Choose me as class leader!" People choose her because they already believe in her.

Sunzi's point is simple: winning with wisdom is better than winning with fists.
[/en]
[/section]

[section]
page: 4
layout: text-wrap-image
heading_zh: 学水，也要先管住自己
heading_en: Learn from Water, Then Control Yourself
image: 2-4-water-around-stone-general.webp
caption_zh: 别硬撞石头，像水一样绕过去，也别先输给自己的脾气
caption_en: Do not crash into the stone. Move around it like water, and do not lose to your own temper first.
[zh]
孙子让所有打仗的人都学一样东西 - 水。

水往低处流。路上有块大石头挡着，水不撞石头 - 它绕着走。前面是悬崖，水不害怕 - 它跳下去。水从来不用蛮力。但你能挡住水吗？挡不住的。它早晚要流到大海里去。

打仗也是一样。别去碰对方最强的地方 - 那里最硬。去找他的弱点 - 他没注意到的地方、疏忽了的地方。绕开他的盾牌，找到他没有盾牌的那一侧。你不需要比他力气大 - 你只需要比他眼睛亮。

但孙子还提醒将军：你最大的敌人常常是你自己。冲动、胆怯、脾气、骄傲和心太软，这些都可能比敌人的刀更危险。先把自己管好，再去管别人。
[/zh]
[en]
Sunzi wanted people who planned war to learn from one thing: water.

Water flows to low places. If a big stone blocks the way, water does not crash into it. It goes around. If there is a cliff ahead, water does not panic. It falls and keeps moving. Water does not use brute force.

But can you stop water forever? No. Sooner or later, it finds a way to the sea.

War can be like that too. Do not smash into the other side's strongest place. Find the weak place, the place they forgot to watch, the place they did not notice. Go around the shield. Find the side with no shield.

You do not always need to be stronger. You need to see more clearly.

But Sunzi also warned generals about themselves. Your biggest enemy may be your own anger, fear, pride, or panic. Those can be more dangerous than the enemy's blade. First control yourself. Then try to lead others.
[/en]

heading_zh: 小结
heading_en: Summary
[zh]
《孙子兵法》讲的，不只是打仗。它也在讲怎么观察、怎么准备、怎么判断、怎么先管好自己。先知道自己，再知道别人；能不打就赢，是最好的赢；别和石头硬撞，要像水一样找路。这些道理过了两千五百年，很多人还是觉得有用。
[/zh]
[en]
*The Art of War* is not just about battle. It is also about watching carefully, getting ready, judging the moment, and controlling yourself first. Know yourself before you study others. The best win may need no fight. Do not crash into the stone. Find a path like water. That is why many people still find this book useful after 2,500 years.
[/en]
[/ending]
