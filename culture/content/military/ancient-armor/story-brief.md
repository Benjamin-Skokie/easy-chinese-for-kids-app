# Ancient Armor Shared Story and Facts Brief

## Core ideas and evidence

1. Organic material decays, iron corrodes, cords disappear, and images preserve shape without directly preserving material. No one clue gives a complete suit.
2. Leather, textile, bronze, iron, steel, lacquer, and mixed construction overlapped across time and place. There is no clean hide-to-bronze-to-iron-to-steel upgrade line.
3. Lamellar construction laces pierced plates to one another; scale construction commonly overlaps plates on a backing. Ancient and modern labels do not always match exactly.
4. Terracotta warriors preserve modeled shapes and differences in coverage, not directly testable armor materials or a census of Qin uniforms. Limestone armor from another pit was funerary, not battlefield equipment.
5. Han tomb finds preserve thousands of iron plates and several structures, not one standard suit for all troops.
6. Every modern illustration contains unsupported choices and must be treated as concept art.

## Boundaries

- Never present a standard armor for an entire dynasty from one find or image.
- Separate structure, material, date, region, role, and use.
- Do not treat shiny/hard as automatically later/better.
- Do not use the modern helmet, plume, European-style breastplates, or workshop details as evidence.

## Level assignments

- Early Reader: build the puzzle from leather, rust, and a clay warrior; explain plates and why armor materials did not simply replace one another.
- Story: add lamellar/scale structure, Qin sculpture and stone models, Han conservation, and the false upgrade image.
- Advanced: retain artifact examples, dates, metallurgical nuance, regional variation, and reconstruction limits.
