[section]
page: 1
layout: text-only
heading_zh: 三条线索
heading_en: Three Clues
[zh]
考古工作室里有三样东西：一小片旧皮革、一团锈在一起的铁片，还有一个穿着铠甲的陶俑。哪一个能告诉我们古代士兵穿什么？

三个都重要，却没有一个能给出完整答案。皮革和布会腐烂，铁会生锈，连接甲片的绳子也会消失。陶俑能保存外形，却不一定告诉我们甲片是皮还是铁。

研究铠甲像拼一幅缺了很多块的图。现代图片画得很清楚，不代表历史也同样清楚。
[/zh]
[en]
Three objects sit on an archaeologist's table: a scrap of old leather, a lump of iron plates rusted together, and a clay soldier wearing armor. Which one can tell us what an ancient soldier wore?

All three matter, but none gives the whole answer. Leather and cloth decay. Iron rusts. The cords between plates may disappear. A clay figure preserves a shape, but it may not tell us whether the real plates were leather or iron.

Studying armor is like building a puzzle with many missing pieces. A modern picture may look clear even when the history is not.
[/en]
[/section]

[section]
page: 2
layout: text-wrap-image
heading_zh: 皮、铜和铁一起存在
heading_en: Leather, Bronze, and Iron Overlapped
image: 3-1-hide-armor-shang-zhou.webp
caption_zh: 现代想象图只帮助我们看裁皮、打孔和连接，不是古代工坊复原。
caption_en: This imagined picture shows cutting, piercing, and joining leather. It does not reconstruct an ancient workshop.
[zh]
有些图把铠甲排成升级队伍：先是兽皮，再是青铜，然后铁和钢。真正历史没有这么整齐。

古代有皮甲片、铜甲片和铁甲片。铁甲出现以后，皮革和布也没有马上消失。不同地方、不同士兵和身体不同部位，会选择不同材料。

工匠要考虑重量、活动、天气、制作时间和修理。最新、最硬的材料，不一定适合每一个人。
[/zh]
[en]
Some pictures arrange armor like game upgrades: first hide, then bronze, then iron and steel. Real history was not so tidy.

Ancient armor used leather, bronze, and iron plates. Leather and cloth did not disappear when iron appeared. Different places, soldiers, and parts of the body could use different materials.

Armor makers thought about weight, movement, weather, time, and repair. The newest or hardest material was not always right for every job.
[/en]
[/section]

[section]
page: 3
layout: text-wrap-image
heading_zh: 小片也能保护身体
heading_en: Small Plates Can Protect a Body
image: 3-2-qin-armor-terracotta.webp
caption_zh: 现代真人和兵马俑放在一起，只能表示“根据雕塑想象实物”。
caption_en: A modern person beside clay warriors only shows how someone might imagine real armor from sculpture.
[zh]
很多中国古代铠甲不是一整块硬壳。工匠在小甲片上打孔，再用绳带把一排排甲片连起来。有些甲片像屋瓦或鱼鳞一样重叠。

小片能在身体弯曲时移动，坏了一部分也可能只换那一块。不过，打孔、涂漆和穿绳都很费时间，绳子还会磨坏。

兵马俑把甲片形状做得很细，却是陶土。它们不能证明所有秦兵都穿同一种材料和样式。
[/zh]
[en]
Much ancient Chinese armor was not one hard shell. Makers punched holes in small plates and tied rows together with cords. Some plates overlapped like roof tiles or fish scales.

Small plates could shift when the body bent. A damaged part might be replaced without remaking the whole suit. But punching, painting, and lacing took time, and cords wore out.

The Terracotta Warriors show armor shapes in great detail, but their armor is clay. They cannot prove that every Qin soldier wore the same material or design.
[/en]
[/section]

[section]
page: 4
layout: text-wrap-image
heading_zh: 从锈块找回铠甲
heading_en: Finding Armor Inside a Lump of Rust
image: 3-3-han-iron-armor.webp
caption_zh: 现代组合图能解释铁片编缀，不能证明汉军都穿这一套头盔和红缨。
caption_en: This modern composite shows laced iron plates. It does not prove that all Han troops wore this helmet and red plume.
[zh]
汉代墓里，一件铁甲已经锈成一团。修复者慢慢整理，发现它由将近三千片铁甲片组成。别的墓还留下上万片残片。

甲片上的孔、形状和绳子痕迹，帮助修复者找出它们怎样连接。不同结构说明，同一时代也没有一套所有人都穿的制服。

现代图里的护面头盔和红缨是设计者加的，不能变成汉代全军的历史事实。
[/zh]
[en]
In one Han tomb, an iron suit had rusted into a solid lump. Conservators slowly separated it and found nearly three thousand plates. Another tomb held more than ten thousand armor fragments.

Holes, shapes, and traces of cord helped researchers work out how the pieces joined. Different structures show that one period did not have a single uniform worn by everyone.

The face-covering helmet and red plume in the modern picture are design choices. They cannot become facts about the whole Han army.
[/en]
[/section]

[ending]
heading_zh: 像修复者一样提问
heading_en: Ask Questions Like a Conservator
[zh]
看见古代铠甲，可以问：这是出土实物、雕塑、文字，还是现代复原？它用什么材料？甲片怎样连接？保护身体哪里？属于什么时间、地方和使用者？

古代铠甲没有从皮到铜、铁、钢一路升级。真正的历史，是工匠用手边材料调整结构，也是考古学家从朽皮、锈片、孔洞和雕塑中一点点找回答案。
[/zh]
[en]
When you see ancient armor, ask: Is it an excavated object, a sculpture, a written description, or a modern reconstruction? What is it made from? How are the plates joined? What does it cover? When, where, and by whom was it used?

Ancient armor did not level up from hide to bronze to iron to steel. Its real history is the story of makers adjusting structures with available materials—and archaeologists recovering answers from leather scraps, rusted plates, holes, and sculpture.
[/en]
[/ending]
