# Míngguāng Armor — References and Editorial Notes

This file is for editorial research and does not render on the website.

## 《周书·蔡祐传》 (*Book of Zhou*, Biography of Cài Yòu)

Source: Chinese Text Project, *Book of Zhou*, chapter 27, “Biography of Cài Yòu”
URL: https://ctext.org/wiki.pl?chapter=196119&if=gb
Accessed: 2026-09-05
Supports: Records Cài Yòu wearing *míngguāng* iron armor in battle and says enemy soldiers called him an “iron beast.”
Editorial boundary: The story uses the historical nickname but does not imply that the armor gave Cài Yòu supernatural strength.

## 中国国家博物馆，“青瓷武士俑”

Source: National Museum of China, “Celadon Warrior Figures”
URL: https://www.chnmuseum.cn/zp/zpml/kgfjp/202111/t20211116_252167.shtml
Accessed: 2026-09-05
Supports: Identifies Northern Dynasties warrior figures as wearing *míngguāng* armor and describes the characteristic paired oval metal guards, shoulder armor, belt, and long armor skirt. It also gives the museum’s traditional explanation that polished guards reflected sunlight.
Editorial boundary: A ceramic figure preserves the represented shape of armor, not the material, thickness, joining method, or exact brightness of a surviving iron suit.

## 中国国家博物馆，“彩绘武服陶俑”

Source: National Museum of China, “Painted Pottery Warrior Figures”
URL: https://www.chnmuseum.cn/zp/zpml/kgfjp/202208/t20220810_256913.shtml
Accessed: 2026-09-05
Supports: Describes a Tang-dynasty warrior figure from the tomb of Zhèng Réntài wearing *míngguāng* armor, with shoulder guards and an armor skirt extending toward the boots.
Editorial boundary: The article describes what the tomb figure shows without treating it as a complete surviving suit of battlefield armor.

## 《唐六典》卷十六 (*Six Statutes of the Tang Dynasty*, juan 16)

Source: Wikisource transcription of *Six Statutes of the Tang Dynasty*, juan 16
URL: https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E5%94%90%E5%85%AD%E5%85%B8/%E5%8D%B716
Accessed: 2026-09-05
Supports: Lists thirteen official armor types with *míngguāng* armor first and identifies *míngguāng*, *guāngyào*, scale, mountain-pattern, black-hammered, and mail armor as iron armors.
Editorial boundary: The list establishes the name and official category but does not provide a construction diagram or prove that all armor called *míngguāng* looked identical.

## 故宫博物院，“明光铠”

Source: Palace Museum, “Míngguāng Armor”
URL: https://www.dpm.org.cn/lemmas/239742.html
Accessed: 2026-09-05
Supports: Describes the paired curved round guards on the chest and explains the traditional association between their shine and the name *míngguāng*.
Editorial boundary: The article presents this as a common explanation rather than claiming that the guards were giant mirrors or could blind an enemy army.
