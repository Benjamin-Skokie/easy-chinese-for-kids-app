## C Grade — Author Detailed Response

### 1. `chinese-characters/zh.md`
- ✅ **Agree.** Starts concept-heavy. Needs a scene: a child in a museum looking at oracle bone, or Wang Yirong finding characters on his medicine.
- Plan: Restructure to open with the 1899 discovery scene, then weave in character types.

### 2. `family-titles/zh.md`
- ✅ **Agree.** Reads like a classification sheet. Suggestion of a family dinner scene is perfect.
- Plan: Open with a kid at Chinese New Year dinner asking "Why do I call him 舅舅 not 叔叔?" — use that curiosity as the engine.

### 3. `exam-content/zh.md`
- ✅ **Strongly agree.** Best information in the set, worst storytelling. Needs a candidate POV.
- Plan: Follow one imaginary examinee through Tang, Song, Ming exams. What did he study? What was on the test? Show his confusion as formats changed.

### 4. `imperial-exam-history/zh.md`
- ✅ **Agree.** Institutional summary. Needs a human path.
- Plan: Use a "time travel" structure — if you were born in Han vs Tang vs Ming, how would your path to office differ?

### 5. `six-arts/zh.md`
- ✅ **Agree.** Curriculum explanation. Needs "a day in the life."
- Plan: Follow one noble boy through his day: dawn archery, morning calligraphy, afternoon chariot, evening music. Show, don't list.

### 6. `daoism/zh.md`
- ⚠️ **Partial.** Has some vivid moments (elixir deaths, talismans). But reviewer is right it's compressed.
- Plan: Start with an alchemist in his workshop — the moment the furnace explodes. Follow that character arc through external and internal alchemy.

### 7. `art-of-war/zh.md`
- ⚠️ **Partial.** Has the best storytelling among the C grades: the palace women training scene is genuinely gripping. But reviewer is right that there are too many abstract sections.
- Plan: Cut or compress some doctrine blocks. Keep the strongest story scenes (palace women, water-and-stone metaphor, 五危). Use Sun Tzu at the Wu king's court as the narrative spine.
