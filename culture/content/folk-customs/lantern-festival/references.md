## Festival date and customs

Source: Shanghai Municipal People's Government, “Lantern Festival”
URL: https://english.shanghai.gov.cn/en-KeyDatesAndCustoms/20260122/e6bf3e0801514c808f6b3d1545a88a3c.html
Accessed: 2026-09-04

Supports:

- Lantern Festival falls on the fifteenth day of the first lunar month.
- It is the first full-moon night of the Chinese New Year and the final major celebration of the Spring Festival season.
- Lantern viewing, lantern riddles, yuanxiao or tangyuan, and family reunion are associated with the festival.

Editorial boundary:

- These established customs may introduce the festival, but no single legend should be presented as its proven historical origin.

## Chang'an and present-day Xi'an

Source: Xi'an Municipal People's Government, “Xi'an, integrating historical and modern glory”
URL: https://en.xa.gov.cn/MediaCenter/News/1691691326091415553.html
Accessed: 2026-09-04

Supports:

- Present-day Xi'an was called Chang'an during the Han dynasty.

Source: UNESCO World Heritage Centre, “Chinese Section of the Silk Road”
URL: https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/5335
Accessed: 2026-09-04

Supports:

- The site of the Han-dynasty city of Chang'an is in Xi'an.
- UNESCO's description identifies the old capital Chang'an with present-day Xi'an.

## Dongfang Shuo and Yuanxiao legend

Source: Beijing Tourism, “Discover the Myths and Legends Behind the Lantern Festival”
URL: https://english.visitbeijing.com.cn/article/47OOPkdR4E5
Accessed: 2026-09-04

Supports:

- Dongfang Shuo appears in the folk legend as a Han-dynasty scholar-official and court jester.
- In the story, he helps the homesick palace maid Yuanxiao reunite with her family.
- The plan involves a citywide display of lanterns, fireworks, and firecrackers that makes the city appear to be on fire.

Editorial boundary:

- Beijing Tourism presents several different stories associated with Lantern Festival. The Dongfang Shuo story is one traditional explanation, not proof that he historically created the festival.
- Details omitted from the child-facing retelling, including Yuanxiao's threatened suicide, should not be silently reintroduced.

Source: CGTN, “The Legendary Origin of the Lantern Festival”
URL: https://news.cgtn.com/news/2021-02-26/The-legendary-origin-of-the-Lantern-Festival-YbJbWuEvss/index.html
Accessed: 2026-09-04

Supports:

- Dongfang Shuo sets up a fortune-telling stand and predicts a disastrous fire in Chang'an.
- He advises the emperor to fill the city with red lanterns, firecrackers, and crowds so it will look as though the city is already burning.
- Yuanxiao's parents enter the city, and she meets them among the Lantern Festival crowds.

Editorial boundary:

- The article itself calls this a legendary story. The published version must retain wording such as “one old folk legend” and must not present the events as verified history.
- The editor's gentler, child-facing retelling intentionally leaves out the story's darker and more complicated variants.
