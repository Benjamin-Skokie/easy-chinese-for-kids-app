## Festival traditions and regional variation

Source: UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage, “Dragon Boat festival”
URL: https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/dragon-boat-festival-00225
Accessed: 2026-09-03

Supports:

- The festival begins on the fifth day of the fifth lunar month.
- Common traditions include dragon boat racing, rice dumplings, scented plants, and remembrance of local heroes.
- Qu Yuan is especially honored in Hubei and Hunan; other regions connect the festival with other figures.

Editorial boundary:

- The article does not claim that every Dragon Boat Festival custom everywhere began with Qu Yuan.

## Qu Yuan and the rice-bundle legend

Source: International Dragon Boat Federation, “History & Culture”
URL: https://www.dragonboat.sport/about-us/history-culture/
Accessed: 2026-09-03

Source: Encyclopaedia Britannica, “Qu Yuan”
URL: https://www.britannica.com/biography/Qu-Yuan
Accessed: 2026-09-03

Supports:

- Qu Yuan was a poet and statesman of the ancient state of Chu.
- The best-known festival legend connects his death in the Miluo River with people searching in boats and placing rice in the water.

Editorial boundary:

- Boat races and wrapped-rice foods have complex histories. The draft calls this a legend rather than a proven one-step origin story.

## Modern dragon boat crews

Source: International Dragon Boat Federation, “History & Culture”
URL: https://www.dragonboat.sport/about-us/history-culture/
Accessed: 2026-09-03

Source: International Dragon Boat Federation, “Key Definitions”
URL: https://www.dragonboat.sport/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/IDBF-Key-Definitions.pdf
Accessed: 2026-09-03

Supports:

- A standard racing boat commonly carries 18–20 paddlers, a drummer, and a steerer.
- The drum maintains the crew's stroke rhythm, paddlers move the boat, and a long steering oar guides it from the rear.
- Racing boats carry a dragon head and tail.

## Zòngzi leaves, fillings, and shapes

Source: Chongqing Municipal Administration for Market Regulation, “如何选购粽叶”
URL: https://scjgj.cq.gov.cn/bkzs/xfts/202406/t20240606_13274800.html
Accessed: 2026-09-03

Source: Beijing Municipal Health Commission / Guang'anmen Hospital, “粽子好吃莫贪多”
URL: https://wjw.beijing.gov.cn/bmfw_20143/jkzs/jksh/202306/t20230627_3147634.html
Accessed: 2026-09-03

Source: Hainan Administration for Market Regulation, “端午节食用粽子消费提示”
URL: https://amr.hainan.gov.cn/zw/xfts/202406/t20240605_3675594.html
Accessed: 2026-09-03

Supports:

- Common zòngzi wrappers include broad *ruò* bamboo leaves and long reed leaves; other regional leaves also exist.
- Sticky rice is the base. Familiar sweet fillings include red dates and red bean paste, while savory versions may contain pork, beans, or salted egg yolk.
- Zòngzi may be triangular, long, or pillow-shaped, depending on regional and family wrapping traditions.

Editorial boundary:

- Mushrooms do appear in some regional southern zòngzi, but they are omitted from the child-facing list because they are not needed to explain the main contrast between sweet and savory fillings.
