## Tomb sweeping, spring outings, and kites

Source: The State Council of the People's Republic of China, “Spring boosts travel demand during Qingming holiday”

Source: Shanghai Municipal People's Government, “Eight things you may not know about Clear and Bright”

Accessed: 2026-09-03

Supports:

- Families clean graves and remember ancestors.
- Qingming began as a solar term, while grave visits became associated with the nearby Cold Food Festival.
- By the Tang dynasty, the neighboring observances were being joined. An imperial order in 732 formally recognized the popular custom of visiting graves around the Cold Food Festival.
- As the Cold Food Festival later lost its separate place, tomb sweeping became one of Qingming's central customs.
- Spring outings and kite flying are established Qingming activities.
- Historical accounts describe cutting kite strings to send away misfortune.

Editorial boundary:

- The article does not encourage releasing kites. Modern kite line can become litter, tangle wildlife, and create safety hazards.

Source: U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, “Finding of Appropriateness — Beach Use (Sports, Kite Flying, Grilling, and Shade Tents)”
URL: https://www.fws.gov/sites/default/files/documents/16w_Appendix_Entire_Document%2810351KB%29.pdf
Accessed: 2026-09-04

Supports:

- Kite string left on the ground or tangled in trees and shrubs can snag birds and tangle their feathers.
- A flying kite line may also be difficult for birds to see and can catch a bird in flight.

Supports:

- Qingtuan are green glutinous-rice treats associated especially with Qingming in Jiangnan and other parts of eastern China.
- Plant juices such as mugwort color and scent the dough.
- Modern fillings may be sweet or savory, and earlier forms were also used as offerings.
