# Ink Wash Painting: Magic Without Colored Paint

## Only Black, Yet It Can Paint Everything

Open a Chinese ink wash painting, and at first you may wonder:

Why is it all black and white?

No blue sky.

No green trees.

No red flowers.

Only dark and light black ink, plus the white of the paper.

But if you look longer, you begin to see that those shades of black have already said all the colors.

Thick ink can be storm clouds gathering over a mountain.

Pale ink can be a soft ripple in shallow spring water.

Dry ink can be a leafless branch shaking in the wind.

Clear, watery ink can be a faraway mountain so thin you cannot tell whether it is mist or a dream.

---

## Ink Has Five Colors: Burnt, Thick, Heavy, Pale, Clear

Chinese painters have an important saying:

"Ink has five colors."

Ink is more than one color. A good painter can use one ink stick to make at least five effects: burnt, thick, heavy, pale, and clear.

Burnt ink is very dark and thick. A painter may let ground ink sit for a while, pour off the extra water, and use the black paste at the bottom. Dot an eagle's eye with it, and the eye looks black, bright, and hard, as if a nerve is hidden behind it.

Thick ink can paint nearby pine needles, wet and full.

Heavy ink is thick ink with a little water added. It can paint the rocks of the main mountain in front.

Pale ink can be pulled out with a clean wet brush before the ink fully dries, making a far mountain fade away.

Clear ink is almost all water, with just a breath of black. It can paint a bird at the edge of the sky, barely there.

Put all five kinds on one sheet of paper:

a black tree trunk in front,
dark bamboo leaves behind it,
heavy ink moss dots nearby,
pale mountains pushed into the distance,
and a clear-ink bird disappearing into white space.

You have not seen any bright color.

But you have already seen trees, leaves, stones, mountains, and sky.

---

## Leaving Blank: The Best Part Is Not Painted

One of the most important ideas in Chinese painting is about more than what is painted.

It is what is left unpainted.

A large white area in the middle of a landscape painting may be a lake, a river, mist, or sky.

You do not need to paint water. Put a tiny boat and the back of an old fisherman in one corner, and the empty white paper under the boat becomes water.

You do not need to paint the whole sky. Draw the outline of a mountaintop, and the blank space above it becomes the wide heavens.

In Ma Yuan's Song dynasty painting *Fishing Alone on a Cold River*, almost the whole picture is empty except for one small boat and an old fisherman.

That emptiness does not mean, "I did not finish."

It means, "I painted up to the edge where you can see. The rest of the water is inside your own mind."

Many Western oil paintings try to fill the whole canvas.

Chinese painting often knows when to stop.

The painter does not leave white space because he has nothing to paint.

Every bit of blank space is left for your imagination.

## Bada Shanren Tore Up the Rules

In the late Ming and early Qing period, there was a strange, wild painter named Bada Shanren.

He was a member of the Ming imperial family. After the Ming dynasty fell, he did not dare openly use his family name Zhu. He broke up and changed his name into Bada Shanren.

His birds, fish, flowers, and insects are not gentle.

Fish roll their eyes.

An eagle stands on one leg on a dry vine and glares at the sky.

Rocks seem ready to fall.

You cannot learn realistic drawing from his paintings. He did not care how many scales a fish truly had.

He cared about something hidden in the blank water:

the pain of a lost country.

Later, when people walk into museums and see his paintings, they often think:

"This person feels so lonely."

Long after he died, people came to understand something else:

Art does not always have to fill everything in.

Sometimes the thing that moves you most is the blank space, the feeling stuck inside the heart that cannot be said aloud.

---

## Summary

Ink has five colors: burnt, thick, heavy, pale, and clear, all made from one color.

Thick ink can paint pine needles. Clear ink can fade into far mountains. Blank space can become water.

Ma Yuan used one small boat and a field of white to paint a whole cold river. Bada Shanren's fish rolled their eyes and broke open the quiet rules.

The greatest power of ink wash painting is often its quietness.

It waits for you to speak first.
