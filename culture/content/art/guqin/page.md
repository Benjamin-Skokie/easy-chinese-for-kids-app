[section]
page: 1
layout: text-wrap-image
heading_zh: 这是古琴，不是古筝
heading_en: Meet the Guqin—not the Guzheng
image: 2-guqin-closeup-strings.webp
caption_zh: 古琴有七根弦，琴身细长，直接放在桌上弹奏。
caption_en: The guqin has seven strings and rests flat on a table.
[zh]
古琴（*gǔqín*）和古筝（*gǔzhēng*）常常被认错。古筝比较大，弦很多，弦下面还有一排小琴码。古琴比较安静、细长，只有七根弦，直接平放在桌上。

它的声音不大。演奏者常常坐得很近，听手指拨弦、滑动和停下。古琴不像是在对一整个广场喊话，更像在一间安静的屋子里讲故事。
[/zh]
[en]
The guqin (*gǔqín*) and guzheng (*gǔzhēng*) are often mixed up. A guzheng is larger, with many strings and a row of small bridges. A guqin is quiet and narrow. It has only seven strings and lies flat on a table.

Its sound is not loud. Players and listeners often sit close enough to hear fingers pluck, slide, and pause. The guqin does not shout across a town square. It tells its story in a quiet room.
[/en]
[/section]

[section]
page: 2
layout: text-only
heading_zh: 拨、按、轻轻一点
heading_en: Pluck, Press, or Touch Lightly
[zh]
同一根弦可以发出三种不同的声音。直接拨空弦，声音清楚而饱满。把弦按在琴面上再拨，音高会改变；手指继续滑动，声音也跟着弯过去。

第三种声音最轻。手指只在弦上的一个特别位置轻轻一点，拨弦后马上离开，便会听见明亮的泛音，像一颗小星星突然闪了一下。七根弦配上这三种方法，就有了很多种声音。
[/zh]
[en]
One string can make three different kinds of sound. Pluck an open string and the note is clear and full. Press the string against the wooden surface and pluck again; the pitch changes. Slide the finger, and the note bends with it.

The third sound is the lightest. Touch a special spot on the string very gently, pluck, and lift the finger away. A bright harmonic rings out, like a tiny star flashing. Seven strings and these three methods give the instrument a surprising number of sounds.
[/en]
[/section]

[section]
page: 3
layout: text-wrap-image
heading_zh: 一张琴会慢慢变老
heading_en: An Instrument Grows Old Slowly
image: 1-guqin-study-room.webp
caption_zh: 古琴常在安静的室内弹奏，也会陪伴主人很多年。
caption_en: A guqin is often played in a quiet room and may stay with one owner for many years.
[zh]
古琴在中国已有很长的历史。早期的琴和今天的琴并不完全一样，弦的材料、琴的形状和弹法都曾慢慢改变。可是，人们一直喜欢它安静而有余韵的声音。

一张琴可以陪伴主人很多年。木头会变旧，漆面可能出现细细的裂纹，手常碰的地方也会变得光滑。这些痕迹记录着琴和人一起度过的日子。
[/zh]
[en]
The guqin has a very long history in China. Early instruments were not exactly the same as today's. String materials, shapes, and playing methods changed slowly over time. Yet people continued to love its quiet voice and notes that seem to hang in the air.

One guqin may stay with its owner for many years. The wood ages. Fine lines may appear in the lacquer. Places touched by hands grow smooth. These marks record the days that the qin and its player have shared.
[/en]
[/section]

[section]
page: 4
layout: text-wrap-image
heading_zh: 伯牙找到了听懂琴声的人
heading_en: Boya Found Someone Who Truly Heard Him
image: 3-boya-zhongziqi.webp
caption_zh: 传说钟子期能从伯牙的琴声中听见高山和流水。
caption_en: In the legend, Zhong Ziqi hears mountains and rushing water in Boya's music.
[zh]
古代传说中，伯牙（*Bó Yá*）很会弹琴。一次，他弹出心中高山的样子。听琴的钟子期（*Zhōng Zǐqī*）马上说：“多么高大的山！”伯牙又想着奔流的水弹奏，钟子期也听懂了。

伯牙终于遇见一个真正懂自己音乐的人。后来，钟子期去世，伯牙悲伤地认为再也找不到这样的听众。这个故事让“知音”（*zhīyīn*）成了一个特别的词：它原本是懂得声音的人，后来也指真正懂你的朋友。
[/zh]
[en]
In an old legend, Boya (*Bó Yá*) was a wonderful qin player. One day he played while thinking of a great mountain. His listener, Zhong Ziqi (*Zhōng Zǐqī*), cried, “What a towering mountain!” Boya then played with rushing water in mind, and Zhong understood that too.

Boya had found someone who truly heard his music. When Zhong later died, Boya grieved that he would never find another listener like him. The story gave Chinese a special word, *zhīyīn*. It first meant someone who understands your music. Now it can also mean a friend who truly understands you.
[/en]
[/section]
