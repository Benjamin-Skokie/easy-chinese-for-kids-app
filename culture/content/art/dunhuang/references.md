# 沙漠山崖里，为什么藏着满墙故事？— References and Editorial Notes

This file is for editorial research and does not render on the website.

## Site chronology, scale, and changing periods

Source: UNESCO World Heritage Centre, “Mogao Caves”
URL: https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/440/
Accessed: 2026-08-21

Supports:

- Buddhist cave art at Mogao developed from the fourth to the fourteenth century.
- The preserved caves contain large bodies of murals and painted sculpture and reflect exchange among China, Central Asia, and India.
- Different caves preserve different periods and subjects; a Tang cave does not represent the whole site.

Editorial boundary:

- Published counts vary by institutional counting method and date. The child-facing article avoids making a precise total the center of the story.
- “About a thousand years” describes the long tradition of construction and decoration, not continuous daily work.

## The 366 date and the Lezun tradition

Source: Dunhuang Academy, “莫高窟简介”
URL: https://www.dha.ac.cn/info/1425/3659.htm
Accessed: 2026-08-21

Supports:

- The cliff is made of relatively loose gravel conglomerate and was unsuitable for detailed stone carving, helping explain the importance of painted clay sculpture.
- A 698 inscription records that the monk Lezun began a cave in 366.
- Other later records give different starting dates, including 353 and a general late Western Jin date.

Editorial boundary:

- The 366 story is a later recorded tradition, not an eyewitness account from 366.
- The article does not invent Lezun's vision, dialogue, weather, or private thoughts.

## Patrons, migrants, and unnamed craftspeople

Source: Dunhuang Academy, “挖掘敦煌文化遗产中蕴含的中华文明精神标识（学苑论衡）”
URL: https://www.dha.ac.cn/info/1018/5661.htm
Accessed: 2026-08-21

Supports:

- The 698 inscription is the basis for the Lezun and Faliang account.
- Monks, travelers, merchants, farmers, craftspeople, painters, and performers contributed to Dunhuang's cultural setting.
- Generations of often unnamed cave cutters, painters, and sculptors worked across the site's long history.

Editorial boundary:

- The article names types of workers rather than inventing a particular workshop scene or claiming that every cave used the same team structure.
- Donor identities and levels of power varied widely by cave and period.

## Mural and sculpture materials

Source: Dunhuang Academy journal, Zhao Jinli et al., “莫高窟第55窟壁画制作材料和工艺分析”
URL: https://skytyz.dha.ac.cn/CN/Y2023/V2/I2/68
Accessed: 2026-08-21

Supports:

- Cave 55 contains layered plaster and paint, with straw in a coarse layer and hemp in a finer layer.
- Identified materials include mineral pigments, animal glue, and surface gilding.

Editorial boundary:

- This is a study of Cave 55, including Song and Western Xia layers. Its exact material recipe must not be presented as universal for every Mogao cave.

Source: Dunhuang Academy, “莫高窟第259窟”
URL: https://www.dha.ac.cn/info/1425/3556.htm
Accessed: 2026-08-21

Supports:

- Damage to figures in Cave 259 reveals bundles of reeds and wooden supports.
- A documented method for small and medium painted sculptures used a wooden frame, plant material, layered fiber-reinforced mud, and color.

Editorial boundary:

- Sculptures differ by size, date, and repair history. The article says “many figures,” not “every statue.”

## The Deer King story as visual narrative

Source: Dunhuang Academy, “莫高窟第257窟”
URL: https://www.dha.ac.cn/info/1425/3548.htm
Accessed: 2026-08-21

Supports:

- Cave 257 contains the Northern Wei Deer King Jataka, the story behind the familiar nine-colored deer.
- Its scenes begin at both ends and conclude in the center rather than following a simple left-to-right order.

Editorial boundary:

- The mural is evidence for Buddhist storytelling and pictorial design, not evidence that a magical deer was a historical animal.
- The article summarizes only enough plot to explain how the wall tells a story.

## Feitian and the pipa pose

Source: Dunhuang Academy, “莫高窟第314窟”
URL: https://www.dha.ac.cn/info/1425/3552.htm
Accessed: 2026-08-21

Supports:

- Flying beings are Buddhist artistic figures transmitted from India through the Western Regions and changed over many centuries at Dunhuang.
- Their forms and painting styles varied over time.

Editorial boundary:

- “Feitian” is not translated as “Chinese angels,” because that imports a different religious category.
- The article describes movement from India through Central Asia and local development, not a pure one-way copy or a claim that the figures became “completely Chinese.”

Source: Dunhuang Academy, “莫高窟第237窟”
URL: https://www.dha.ac.cn/info/1425/3641.htm
Accessed: 2026-08-21

Supports:

- Dunhuang murals contain more than one reverse-pipa dance pose.
- The Cave 237 description treats it as an image of a dancer holding the instrument behind the back amid a larger musical ensemble.

Editorial boundary:

- A painted pose does not by itself establish the exact continuous choreography, sound, or frequency of a real performance.
- The article does not claim the pose was impossible; modern performers can attempt related movements.

## Cave 17 contents, discovery, and dispersal

Source: International Dunhuang Programme, “Cave 17: Discovering Cave 17”
URL: https://idp.bl.uk/discover/learning/dunhuang/dunhuang-articles/cave-17-the-library-cave/discovering-cave-17/
Accessed: 2026-08-21

Supports:

- Wang Yuanlu found the sealed entrance in 1900 while caring for and restoring the site.
- Cave 17 held more than 50,000 manuscripts, paintings, printed documents, textiles, and other objects dating from the fourth to eleventh centuries.
- Stein obtained large groups in 1907 and Pelliot obtained another group in 1908; further dispersal followed.
- The original arrangement cannot now be reconstructed because the contents had already been moved before the earliest detailed observations and photographs.

Editorial boundary:

- Wang is neither excused as an innocent who understood nothing nor reduced to a cartoon villain. His reports, caretaking, negotiations, and sales all belong in the account.
- “Discovery” refers to the modern reopening of the sealed chamber, not the creation of its contents.

Source: International Dunhuang Programme, “Cave 17: What was stored in Cave 17?”
URL: https://idp.bl.uk/discover/learning/dunhuang/dunhuang-articles/cave-17-the-library-cave/what-was-stored-in-cave-17/
Accessed: 2026-08-21

Supports:

- Most items were handwritten documents, in about twenty languages, with Chinese and Tibetan especially numerous.
- The collection includes sutras but also contracts, monastery inventories, shopping lists, pilgrims' records, almanacs, and texts from several religious traditions.

Editorial boundary:

- The collection must not be described as only “Buddhist scriptures” or only “Chinese-language books.”

Source: British Museum, “Exploring the Silk Roads: The Stein Collection”
URL: https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/china/exploring-silk-roads
Accessed: 2026-08-21

Supports:

- Stein's 1907 expedition was substantially funded by the Government of India and the British Museum.
- Stein obtained manuscripts and objects from Wang for payment, and the resulting collection was divided among institutions in Britain and India.
- The British Museum itself calls the expedition “controversial.”

Editorial boundary:

- “Purchase” describes the transaction but does not settle the ethical question of consent, unequal institutional power, or cultural ownership.
- The article uses “obtained” and explains the funding and low payment rather than softening the episode into neutral collecting.

## Pigment change and physical conservation

Source: Dunhuang Academy, “李最雄的岩壁丹心”
URL: https://www.dha.ac.cn/info/1021/7486.htm
Accessed: 2026-08-21

Supports:

- Mineral pigments have different stabilities; light can alter cinnabar, while humidity and alkaline conditions can cause red lead to turn brown-black.
- Changes in salt and humidity can loosen and powder mural plaster.
- Conservation relies on testing, monitoring, and treatments chosen for particular problems.

Editorial boundary:

- Not every color simply “fades,” and not every dark face was originally painted dark. Different pigments and damage processes behave differently.
- Conservation slows and manages change; it cannot promise that original material will last forever.

## Visitors, cave climate, and digital access

Source: UNESCO World Heritage Centre, “Submission Form: Mogao Caves visitor management and digital interpretation”
URL: https://whc.unesco.org/document/116508
Accessed: 2026-08-21

Supports:

- Mogao monitors temperature, humidity, carbon dioxide, and visitor numbers in open caves.
- Caves may be closed when environmental readings exceed acceptable levels.
- Digital interpretation and managed visitor routes reduce time and pressure inside original caves.

Editorial boundary:

- Digital recording expands access and preserves information, but it is not the same thing as permanently preserving the physical caves.
- The article avoids a promise that scanning can make the site “immortal.”
