## Symbolic performance, movement, and music

Source: UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage, “Peking Opera”
URL: https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/peking-opera-00418
Accessed: 2026-09-04

Supports:

- Peking opera combines singing, recitation, acting, and martial arts.
- Its traditional staging uses symbolic, stylized movement and relatively few sets and props.
- Music helps establish pace and atmosphere, shape characters, and guide the story.
- Performers use carefully practiced movements of the hands, eyes, body, and feet.

## A small prop can create a larger world

Source: State Council Information Office / China.org.cn, “Peking Opera”
URL: https://www.china.org.cn/englishscio/chinafacts/2017-04/13/content_40612142.htm
Accessed: 2026-09-04

Supports:

- Traditional Peking opera can use one table and two chairs in different arrangements to suggest different settings.
- Symbolic stage props include riding whips and oars.
- A performer’s movements and a few conventional clues allow audiences to imagine horses, boats, journeys, palaces, and battlefields without realistic scenery.

Editorial boundary:

- Minimal staging is presented as a flexible performance language, not a rule that every production must use exactly the same furniture or props.

## Formation, costumes, makeup, and instruments

Source: Beijing Municipal Government, “Peking Opera: A Synthesis of Chinese Operas Combining Music, Vocal Performance, Mime, and Acrobatics”
URL: https://english.beijing.gov.cn/beijinginfo/culture/202005/t20200511_1894129.html
Accessed: 2026-09-04

Supports:

- Anhui troupes came to Beijing in 1790.
- From about 1840 to 1860, the developing form combined elements of Huiju, Qinqiang, Hanju, Kunqu, and local Beijing music before taking recognizable shape.
- Costume, headwear, facial features, and makeup can help audiences recognize information about roles and characters.
- Gongs, drums, and other instruments help establish rhythm and mark dramatic action.

Editorial boundary:

- The article treats 1790 as an important point in a gradual formation process, not the single birthday of a form invented by one person.

Supports:

- Peking opera facial makeup changes according to roles and follows established artistic conventions.
