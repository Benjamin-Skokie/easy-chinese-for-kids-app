# Peking Opera Face Paint Can Talk

## A Face Walks Out from Backstage

The lights go dim.

The gongs and drums explode with a loud *guang!*

A great red painted face walks out from backstage.

All the children in the audience go quiet.

Not because he is holding a weapon.

Because of his face.

It is not an ordinary face. The whole face is painted bright red. The eyebrows sweep upward almost to the top of the forehead. The corners of the eyes fly toward the temples.

He has not spoken. He has not even moved much.

But the moment that face stands on stage, you know:

this person is a hero.

In Chinese opera, this is called *lianpu*, or face paint.

A Peking opera actor does not always need to introduce himself with words. His face does it first.

A painted face can tell the audience a whole story about the person onstage.

---

## Colors Are Speaking

A red face often means loyalty and bravery. Guan Yu's face is usually painted red because people remember him as loyal and honorable.

A white face often suggests a tricky or dangerous person. Cao Cao's face may be painted pale white, with narrow eyes that make you feel careful right away.

A black face often means someone honest and firm. Bao Zheng's face is black like iron, with a small crescent moon on his forehead. It shows that he is fair and strict.

Gold and silver faces often belong to gods and spirits.

Blue and green faces may show bold heroes from the wild world, people with hot tempers who dare to act.

You do not have to watch the whole story. You do not have to wait for subtitles.

Very often, just by seeing the color, you can guess a lot:

red may be loyal,
white may be crafty,
black may be fair and strong.

It is as if the face begins singing before the actor opens his mouth.

---

## A Face Passed Down for More Than a Hundred Years

Face paint is not random.

Guan Yu's face is passed from teacher to student. If an opera group performs the same role for decades, the red color cannot be changed carelessly. The curve of the eyebrows matters too.

On Guan Yu's forehead, the three black rising lines have to feel right. If one line is missing, people who know opera may feel something is wrong before the actor says a word.

Students often learn to paint faces early in their training. They sit before a mirror and practice.

Paint the face wrong, and the role already feels wrong.

Different opera groups can paint the same person with tiny differences. A Guan Yu from Beijing and a Guan Yu from Shanghai may both have a red face and long, proud eyes. But the Beijing red may be deeper. The Shanghai red may be brighter. One eyebrow tail may stretch longer than the other.

Experienced opera fans can sometimes tell which opera group a performance comes from just by looking at the face.

Inside those tiny differences are generations of care.

---

## Summary

Peking opera actors tell stories with their faces.

A red face often makes people think of loyalty and bravery. A white face makes people cautious. A black face often suggests someone honest and firm.

One painted face, one craft, one crescent moon on Bao Zheng's forehead, and three rising lines on Guan Yu's brow: these details pass from teacher to student, generation after generation.

Learning Peking opera means learning to sing, move, and act.

But one important step is learning how to paint the face correctly.
