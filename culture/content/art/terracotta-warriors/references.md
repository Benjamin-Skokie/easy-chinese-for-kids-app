# 兵马俑：八千张脸，八千幅肖像吗？— References and Editorial Notes

This file is for editorial research and does not render on the website.

## 1. UNESCO World Heritage Centre and Emperor Qinshihuang’s Mausoleum Site Museum — discovery, setting, and scale

Source: UNESCO World Heritage Centre, “Mausoleum of the First Qin Emperor”
URL: https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/441/
Accessed: 2026-09-04

Source: Emperor Qinshihuang’s Mausoleum Site Museum, archaeological work exhibition
URL: https://www.bmy.com.cn/2014archaeology/contents/146/12633.html
Accessed: 2026-09-04

Supports:

- Local farmers found clay fragments while digging a well in 1974, leading archaeologists to the warrior pits near Xi’an.
- The pits form part of the much larger mausoleum complex of Qin Shi Huang, the first emperor of a unified China.
- The three principal warrior pits contain life-size figures, horses, chariots, and weapons; their estimated total is commonly rounded to about 8,000.
- The project preserves evidence of large-scale pottery, bronze working, building, and organized labor.

Editorial boundary:

- About 8,000 is an estimate for the pits, not a count of 8,000 complete, excavated, restored figures.
- The warriors stand in pits within the mausoleum complex, not inside the emperor’s unopened main burial chamber.

## 2. The Metropolitan Museum of Art — “Making the Warrior: The Qin Terracotta Soldiers in Age of Empires”

URL: https://www.metmuseum.org/perspectives/terracotta-warriors-age-of-empires
Accessed: 2026-09-04

Supports:

- Most scholars do not treat the warriors as 8,000 portraits copied directly from living soldiers.
- Scholars disagree about parts of the exact construction sequence, including how extensively separate modules, molds, coiling, and one-piece building were used.
- Across different explanations, repeated basic processes were combined with fine clay and hand finishing to form hair, beards, eyes, mouths, clothing, belts, and armor.
- The figures were fired after shaping and surface work.

Editorial boundary:

- The story preserves the scholarly disagreement instead of presenting one contested reconstruction as settled fact.
- “Repeated forms” does not mean one complete body mold produced thousands of identical statues.

## 3. UCL Institute of Archaeology / Emperor Qinshihuang’s Mausoleum Site Museum — Imperial Logistics Project

Source: “Project Introduction”
URL: https://www.ucl.ac.uk/terracotta-army/
Accessed: 2026-09-04

Source: “3d models and the faces of Qin?”
URL: https://www.ucl.ac.uk/terracotta-army/news/2014/jun/3d-models-and-faces-qin
Accessed: 2026-09-04

Supports:

- The collaborative project studies crafting methods, workshops, administration, makers’ marks, spatial patterns, figures, and weapons.
- Researchers used three-dimensional models to compare fine shape differences, including the ears of warriors from Pit 1.
- The ear variation may reflect modeled individuality, workshop practice, or both; it cannot by itself prove that each warrior depicts one real person.
- Names, marks, material patterns, and small stylistic differences help researchers investigate production groups and organized manufacture.

Editorial boundary:

- Three-dimensional comparison provides evidence about variation and production, not a list linking clay faces to named Qin soldiers.
- The article does not invent identities, ranks, biographies, or ethnic backgrounds for individual figures.

## 4. Getty Conservation Institute — conservation of the original painted surfaces

Source: Catharina Blaensdorf et al., “The Polychrome Terra-cotta Army of the First Emperor Qin Shi Huang”
URL: https://www.getty.edu/publications/resources/virtuallibrary/0892364165.pdf
Accessed: 2026-09-04

Source: Daniela Bathelt and Heinz Langhals, “Two Methods for the Conservation of the Polychromy of the Terracotta Army of Qin Shihuang”
URL: https://www.getty.edu/publications/resources/virtuallibrary/9781606060131.pdf
Accessed: 2026-09-04

Supports:

- The figures originally carried vivid painted surfaces over layers that included East Asian lacquer.
- After roughly 2,200 years in damp soil, excavation exposes the lacquer to a drier environment; it can rapidly lose water, shrink, crack, deform, lose adhesion, and pull away from the terracotta.
- Conservators may keep newly excavated material at very high humidity and stabilize it gradually, showing why excavation has to proceed cautiously.

Editorial boundary:

- The colors listed in the article are based on surviving pigment evidence, but the complete color scheme of every warrior is not known.
- The page-three image is labeled as a modern illustration, not an exact reconstruction of one excavated warrior.
