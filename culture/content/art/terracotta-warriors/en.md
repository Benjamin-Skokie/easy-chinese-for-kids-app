# Terracotta Warriors: An Army That Stood Underground for Two Thousand Years

## One Hoe Dug Up a Whole Army

In March 1974, in Xiyang Village near Xi'an, several farmers were digging a well.

On the fourth day, a hoe hit something hard with a *dang*.

The lead farmer, Yang Zhifa, thought it was a stone. He brushed away the dirt.

A clay human face stared back at him, inch by inch, from the earth.

He was so frightened that he threw down his hoe.

Some younger men gathered their courage and kept digging. Soon they found more clay heads, broken clay horse legs, and bronze arrowheads.

When the news reached the county cultural office, the farmers did not know what they had just found.

They had opened one of the most important ancient discoveries of the 20th century.

The first moment the Terracotta Warriors returned to daylight was the moment Yang Zhifa looked straight into that clay face.

## Eight Thousand Faces, None Exactly the Same

When archaeologists began digging carefully, they were stunned.

This was not one row or two rows.

An entire underground clay army stood below: more than 8,000 soldiers, standing in rows like an army ready to move.

At the front stood three rows of crossbow soldiers without armor. Behind them were horse carts and soldiers in armor. Horse riders guarded the two sides. Farther back were carts for leaders, plus painted bronze chariots.

The whole pit was like a careful mini version of Qin Shihuang's real army.

But the most shocking thing was not the number.

It was the faces.

Among more than 8,000 faces, almost no two look exactly the same.

Some have thick eyebrows, long eyes, and high noses.

Some have full lips, short chins, and wide cheekbones.

Some have narrow eyes and thin cheeks.

The warriors were not made by pressing one whole body from one mold again and again. Qin craftspeople used molds for parts of the body and limbs, then carefully finished the heads and faces by hand.

That is why each face has its own expression.

When you walk slowly along Pit 1, it feels as if you are looking at the shadows of real people from an ancient army.

The original Chinese text imagines a life behind every face: a soldier from Shaanxi, a crossbow team leader, a man who had fought for years, built walls after harvest, and carried scars from war and hard work.

We do not know those personal stories for each figure.

But the faces make us want to imagine them.

---

## They Were Once All Painted

Today, most Terracotta Warriors look grayish yellow.

That was not their original color.

When they first came out of the earth, many still had bright paint:

pink skin,
cinnabar-red armor,
green details,
dark blue trim around collars and sleeves.

Then air touched them.

The shiny coating changed. The paint dried, cracked, and lifted. Color disappeared from the figures like a thin breath of smoke.

At the time, people did not yet have today's ways to protect old color. For many warriors, the first few minutes after meeting the air were the last few minutes of their painted color.

So the warriors you see today are mostly the faded version.

Those gray clay faces are not how they first looked.

They are what remained after the colors left.

---

## Curiosity Sealed in the Pit

So far, only a small part of the tomb pits around Qin Shihuang's great tomb has been dug out.

The main underground palace under the burial mound has not been opened.

Tests show that under the mound there is a large amount of mercury, which matches old stories about rivers of mercury in the tomb.

But what is really inside?

No one today can say for sure.

You can imagine what ancient books described: a bronze ceiling, stars made of gems, and rivers of mercury.

Or you can honestly admit:

the real answer is still underground.

Today, the Terracotta Warriors Museum welcomes many visitors every year. People lean over the railings and look down at the faded clay soldiers.

On the back of a ticket, you might see a picture showing how the colors may once have looked.

But in front of your eyes are gray-yellow faces.

That difference is powerful by itself.

---

## Summary

One swing of a hoe uncovered 8,000 clay soldiers for Qin Shihuang.

Their faces are different from one another, as if each holds the shadow of a real person.

For a few minutes after they were dug out, some warriors still showed bright colors: pink skin, red armor, green and blue details. Then air touched them, and much of the color vanished.

Under the main tomb mound, mercury still waits in the dark.

For more than 2,000 years, no one has gone down to see the full answer.
