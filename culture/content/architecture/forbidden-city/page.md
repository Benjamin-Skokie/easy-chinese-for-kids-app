[section]
page: 1
layout: text-wrap-image
heading_zh: 像一座给皇帝住的小城市
heading_en: Like a Small City for One Emperor
image: no-trees-outer-court.webp
caption_zh: 红墙、黄瓦、大广场，故宫看起来像一座专门给皇帝准备的小城市。
caption_en: With red walls, yellow roofs, and huge open courtyards, the Forbidden City feels like a small city made for an emperor.
[zh]
故宫在北京。它不是一座小宫殿，而是一大片宫殿。

里面有很多很多房子、院子和大门。远远看去，红墙和黄瓦一层接一层，好像没有尽头。

皇帝住在这里，也在这里办公、见大臣、办大典。

有些大广场上，一棵树都没有。这样大家一走进去，就会马上觉得这里又大又庄严。
[/zh]
[en]
The Forbidden City is in Beijing. It is not one small palace. It is a huge group of palaces.

Inside are many buildings, courtyards, and gates. From far away, the red walls and yellow roofs seem to go on and on.

The emperor lived here. He also worked here, met officials here, and held great ceremonies here.

Some of the big courtyards have no trees at all. That way, when people walked in, the place felt wide, grand, and serious right away.
[/en]
[/section]

[section]
page: 2
layout: text-wrap-image
heading_zh: 没有卡车，怎么把大石头运来？
heading_en: No Trucks, So How Did They Move a Giant Stone?
image: giant-stone-on-ice.webp
caption_zh: 冬天路面结冰后，工匠把巨石放在滑板上，一点一点拖向故宫。
caption_en: In winter, workers pulled giant stones on sleds over ice, little by little, toward the palace.
[zh]
很久以前，没有卡车，也没有起重机。

可是故宫要用到很大的木头和很重的石头。工匠只好想办法，把它们从很远很远的地方运来。

大木头先顺着河流漂，再慢慢运到北京。

最重的大石头更难。工匠等到冬天，把路面浇上水，让它结冰，再把石头放到大滑板上，让很多人一起拉。

今天我们在故宫看到的大殿和石阶，背后都藏着许多人的力气和耐心。
[/zh]
[en]
Long ago, there were no trucks and no cranes.

But the Forbidden City needed huge logs and very heavy stones. So the builders had to think of clever ways to bring them from far away.

Big logs could float along rivers and canals until they reached Beijing.

The heaviest stones were even harder. Workers waited for winter, poured water on the road to make ice, put the stone on a giant sled, and had many people pull together.

When we see the great halls and carved stone ramps today, we are also seeing the strength and patience of many workers from long ago.
[/en]
[/section]

[section]
page: 3
layout: text-wrap-image
heading_zh: 皇帝也不是想去哪里就去哪里
heading_en: An Emperor Could Not Go Anywhere He Wanted
image: imperial-garden.webp
caption_zh: 故宫里也有花园，可它更像宫墙里的小小绿地。
caption_en: The palace had a garden too, but it was still only a small patch of green inside the walls.
[zh]
故宫里也有花园、亭子和古树。

可是对皇帝来说，这些地方还是在高高的宫墙里面。

他不能像普通人那样，想上街就上街，想去面馆就去面馆。

所以故宫一方面很华丽，另一方面也像一个很大的金色笼子。

房子最大的人，不一定最自由。
[/zh]
[en]
The Forbidden City also had gardens, pavilions, and old trees.

But for the emperor, all of that was still inside high palace walls.

He could not do what ordinary people could do, like walk into the street whenever he wanted or go out for a bowl of noodles.

So the Forbidden City was grand and beautiful, but in another way it was also like a giant golden cage.

The person living in the biggest house was not always the freest.
[/en]
[/section]

[section]
page: 4
layout: text-only
heading_zh: 后来，大家都能走进来
heading_en: Later, Everyone Could Walk In
[zh]
后来，故宫不再只是皇帝的家。

它变成了故宫博物院。今天，很多很多人都会从午门走进去，看看这些古老宫殿里发生过什么故事。

最有意思的是，最后一位住在这里的皇帝溥仪，长大以后再回故宫，已经要像别的游客一样买票了。

这听起来有点奇怪，也有点像历史在轻轻转了一个圈。
[/zh]
[en]
Later, the Forbidden City stopped being only the emperor's home.

It became the Palace Museum. Today, many visitors walk in through the Meridian Gate and imagine the stories that once happened there.

The strangest part is this: Puyi, the last emperor who lived there, had to buy a ticket when he came back as an adult.

That feels a little surprising, and a little like history quietly turned in a circle.
[/en]
[/section]

[ending]
heading_zh: 小结
heading_en: In Summary
[zh]
故宫像一座给皇帝住的小城市。它有大广场、大宫殿，也有小花园。修它的时候，人们想出了很多聪明办法来运木头和石头。

今天，故宫已经不是只属于皇帝的地方了。它成了大家都能走进去看的博物馆。
[/zh]
[en]
The Forbidden City was like a small city built for an emperor. It had huge courtyards, great halls, and even a small garden. The builders used many clever ways to move the wood and stone needed to make it.

Today, the Forbidden City no longer belongs only to emperors. It has become a museum that everyone can visit.
[/en]
[/ending]
