# Tulou: A Village Inside One Circle

## One Building Holds a Whole Village

Deep in the mountains of Fujian, there is a kind of building you do not forget once you see it.

It is called a *tulou*, or earth building.

From the sky, many tulou look like giant doughnuts, one circle inside another, scattered through the valleys.

Many people from the same family clan could live inside one tulou. They spent their days around one great circle.

Look up, and the sky is round.

Look down, and the courtyard is round too.

Home feels as if it is being held inside a giant ring.

---

## A Castle Made from Earth and Sticky Rice

Tulou walls can be amazingly thick. In some places, they are about 1.5 meters thick, wider than your arms can stretch.

What were they made from?

Not modern cement.

Builders mainly used local earth, wood, and stone. They packed earth down hard, layer after layer, until it became strong. Traditional stories also often mention sticky rice paste, egg white, and other materials used to help bind things together.

Each tulou was a little different. But builders really could make walls that were thick, strong, and long-lasting.

Many tulou have stood through hundreds of years of wind and rain.

That is why a tulou was more than a home. It was also a fortress.

In dangerous times, if bandits came, people could close the main gate. Then the tulou became a small castle.

The first floor had no windows. Windows opened only from the second floor up, and they were very small, just big enough for an arrow to shoot out.

Inside the central courtyard, there could be wells, grain storage, and pens for animals.

Even if the gate stayed closed for months, people inside could keep living.

---

## Chengqi Lou: The King of Tulou

Chengqi Lou in Yongding, Fujian, is often called the "King of Tulou."

It has several rings. The tall outer ring surrounds the building, while more layers sit closer and closer to the center.

At its busiest, people say many families lived there.

Open a door, and you might see relatives.

Push open a window, and you might hear a neighbor talking, cooking, or calling a child.

The whole building was like a small village that could breathe.

---

## Why Is It Round?

The round shape was not just for beauty.

It helped with defense. It also helped the building stand strongly, because force could spread more evenly around the circle.

The view was connected too. From inside, people could see around the courtyard more easily.

Why did so many tulou use circles?

Partly because circles worked well. Partly because local ideas of beauty, family life, and community all mattered too.

Architecture is rarely about just one reason.

---

## Summary

A tulou is like a home and a fortress at the same time.

Many people could live inside one building. Once the gate closed, the inside became a small world that could take care of itself.

The most fascinating thing about tulou may be more than how thick the walls are.

It may be that so many people could share daily life inside one great circle.
