[section]

heading_zh: 皇帝不是每天都穿同一件衣服
heading_en: An Emperor Did Not Wear the Same Clothes Every Day
image: mianfu-formal-robe.webp
caption_zh: 冕服是最庄重的礼服，只有最重要的大典才会穿。
caption_en: Mianfu was the most formal robe, worn only for the most important ceremonies.
[zh]
很多电视剧里的皇帝，好像天天都穿着亮闪闪的龙袍。

其实不是这样。皇帝有不同的衣服，要看他去做什么事。

最重要的大典，要穿最庄重的礼服，叫冕服。

冕服不只是衣服，还包括帽子、腰带、鞋子和很多配件。穿上以后，皇帝看起来就像把“皇帝这个身份”整个穿到了身上。
[/zh]
[en]
In many TV dramas, emperors seem to wear the same shining dragon robe all day.

Real history was not like that. Emperors had different clothes for different jobs.

For the biggest ceremonies, they wore the most formal kind of dress, called *mianfu*.

Mianfu was not only one robe. It also included the crown, belt, shoes, and other parts. When the emperor put it on, it was almost like he had dressed himself in the whole role of emperor.
[/en]
[/section]

[section]
page: 2
layout: text-wrap-image
heading_zh: 衣服上也会“讲道理”
heading_en: The Robe Could Teach a Lesson Too
image: twelve-symbols-closeup.webp
caption_zh: 太阳、月亮、山、龙、火和谷粒这些图案，不只是装饰。
caption_en: Symbols such as the sun, moon, mountains, dragon, fire, and grain were more than decoration.
[zh]
还有一种礼服，叫衮服。

衮服上常常会绣很多特别的图案，比如太阳、月亮、山、龙、火和谷粒。

古代人觉得，这些图案不是随便好看就行，它们还在提醒皇帝应该成为什么样的人。

所以有时候，一件衣服也像一本不会说话的规则书。
[/zh]
[en]
There was another formal robe called *gunfu*.

Gunfu often carried special designs such as the sun, moon, mountains, dragon, fire, and grain.

Long ago, people did not think these patterns were there only to look beautiful. They also reminded the emperor what kind of ruler he was supposed to be.

That means a robe could sometimes work like a rulebook that never spoke out loud.
[/en]
[/section]

[section]
page: 3
layout: text-wrap-image
heading_zh: 去打猎时，衣服就要利落得多
heading_en: Hunting Clothes Had to Be Easier to Move In
image: hunting-dress-on-horseback.webp
caption_zh: 到了猎场，皇帝会换上更适合骑马和射箭的衣服。
caption_en: At the hunting ground, the emperor changed into clothes better for riding and archery.
[zh]
要是皇帝去骑马、射箭或打猎，就不能再穿又重又长的礼服了。

这时候，他会换上窄袖、短一些的袍子，再配上靴子、弓袋和箭囊。

这样动作会方便很多。

可是就算换了更轻便的衣服，大家还是一看就知道：这不是普通猎人，这是皇帝。
[/zh]
[en]
If the emperor went riding, shooting, or hunting, he could not stay in a heavy long ceremony robe.

Then he changed into narrower sleeves, a shorter robe, boots, a bow case, and a quiver.

That made movement much easier.

But even after changing into practical clothes, everyone could still tell right away: this was not an ordinary hunter. This was the emperor.
[/en]
[/section]

[section]
page: 4
layout: text-only
heading_zh: 换了衣服，气氛也变了
heading_en: When the Clothes Changed, the Feeling Changed
[zh]
冕服让人觉得安静、庄重。

衮服让人想到规矩和身份。

猎装看起来最有动作感，也最像要立刻出门做事。

同一个人，只是换一套衣服，给人的感觉就会很不一样。
[/zh]
[en]
Mianfu feels quiet and solemn.

Gunfu makes people think of rules and rank.

Hunting dress feels the most active, as if the wearer is ready to ride out at once.

It is the same person, yet changing clothes can completely change the feeling people get from him.
[/en]
[/section]

[ending]
heading_zh: 小结
heading_en: In Summary
[zh]
皇帝的衣服分很多种。最正式的时候穿冕服，讲规矩的时候会穿有章纹的礼服，去骑马打猎时又要换上更利落的衣服。衣服一换，气氛就跟着变了。
[/zh]
[en]
Emperors had many kinds of clothes. They wore *mianfu* for the most formal moments, symbol-filled robes for ceremony and rank, and practical dress for riding and hunting. When the clothes changed, the whole feeling changed too.
[/en]
[/ending]
