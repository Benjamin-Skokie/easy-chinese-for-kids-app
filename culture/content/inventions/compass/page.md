heading_en: Clouds Have Hidden Both Sun and Stars

caption_zh: 这是现代绘制的海上想象图，不是某一次宋代航行的记录。图中的船、人物、风浪和天气都不能当作历史证据。
caption_en: This is a modern imagined scene at sea, not a record of a particular Song voyage. Its ship, people, waves, and weather are not historical evidence.
[zh]
想象一艘古代海船正在离开熟悉的海岸。白天，船员可以观察太阳；晚上，他们可以看星星；靠近陆地时，山峰、岛屿和港口也能帮忙认路。可是乌云压下来以后，太阳不见了，夜里连星星也看不到。船还在被风推着走，方向却越来越难判断。

这时，一根能大致保持南北方向的磁针会很有用。不过，指南针不是一件突然出现的神奇礼物，也不是会告诉船员“下一站在哪里”的地图。它经历了很长的变化：人们先认识天然磁石，再尝试会转动的指向器，后来制作磁化铁针、指南鱼和航海罗盘。每一步留下的证据都不一样，有些很清楚，有些仍在争论。

所以真正的问题不只是“谁发明了指南针”，而是：一块能吸铁的石头，怎样一步步变成船上可以使用的方向工具？
[/zh]
[en]
Imagine an early seagoing ship leaving a familiar coast. During the day, sailors could watch the sun. At night, they could use stars. Near land, mountains, islands, and harbors also helped them recognize a route. Then heavy clouds rolled in. The sun vanished, and no stars appeared after dark. The wind kept pushing the ship, but its direction became harder to judge.

A magnetized needle that kept roughly to a north–south line could help. Yet the compass did not appear all at once as a magical gift, and it was not a map that could announce the next port. It changed over a long time. People learned about naturally magnetic stone, tested pointers that could turn, and later made magnetized iron needles, south-pointing fish, and mariners' compasses. Different steps left different kinds of evidence. Some are clear; others remain disputed.

So the most interesting question is not simply, “Who invented the compass?” It is this: How did a stone that attracted iron gradually become a direction-finding tool aboard ships?
[/en]
[/section]

[section]
page: 2
layout: text-wrap-image
heading_zh: 磁石会吸铁，但那把“勺子”是真的吗？
heading_en: Lodestone Attracts Iron—but Was There Really a “Spoon”?
image: lodestone-spoon.webp
caption_zh: 这是现代人根据古文制作的司南勺想象图，不是出土的汉代指南器。古文中的“司南”究竟是什么装置、勺形复原能否代表实物，学者仍有争论。
caption_en: This is a modern imagined lodestone-spoon reconstruction based on old texts, not an excavated Han direction finder. Scholars still debate what the ancient *sinan* was and whether the spoon model represents a real device.
[zh]
天然磁石是一种本身带有磁性的矿石。古人很早就知道它能吸引铁，也注意到某些磁性物体自由转动以后会朝向一定方向。东汉学者王充在《论衡》中写过一句著名的话，大意是“司南”的柄会指向南方。这句话证明当时有“司南”这个指向概念，却没有留下尺寸、材料和完整操作说明。

今天博物馆和课本里常出现一把放在光滑盘面上的磁石勺：勺身旋转，勺柄停在南方。这种形象主要来自二十世纪的复原试验。到目前为止，没有一件被普遍确认的汉代“司南勺”实物出土；古书中有关字形、器形和使用方式的解释也有分歧。它可能帮助我们想象一种早期方案，却不能当成已经找到的发明原件。

这一区别很重要。古文可以证明人们谈论过指南现象，现代复原可以检验某种做法是否可能，但“书里有一句话”和“考古发现了这件器物”不是同一种证据。
[/zh]
[en]
Lodestone is a naturally magnetized mineral. People in ancient China knew early that it could attract iron, and they noticed that some magnetic objects turned toward a regular direction when free to move. In the Eastern Han period, the scholar Wang Chong wrote a famous line saying, in effect, that the handle of a *sinan* pointed south. The sentence shows that a south-pointing idea called *sinan* existed, but it gives no complete set of dimensions, materials, or operating instructions.

Modern museums and textbooks often show a lodestone spoon resting on a smooth plate. The bowl of the spoon turns, and its handle stops toward the south. That familiar image comes mainly from twentieth-century reconstruction experiments. No excavated Han object has been widely accepted as an actual “south-pointing spoon,” and scholars disagree about the old characters, the shape of the device, and the way it worked. The model can help us imagine one possible design, but it is not a recovered original invention.

That distinction matters. An old sentence can show that people discussed directional behavior. A modern reconstruction can test whether one design might work. Neither is the same as archaeologists finding the device itself.
[/en]
[/section]

[section]
page: 3
layout: text-wrap-image
heading_zh: 从指南鱼到被磁石“教过”的针
heading_en: From a South-Pointing Fish to a Needle “Taught” by Lodestone
image: floating-needle.webp
caption_zh: 这是现代绘制的漂浮磁针想象图，不是《武经总要》或《梦溪笔谈》的精确器物复原。原书描述了方向器和使用方法，却没有留下这样的彩色现场图。
caption_en: This is a modern imagined floating magnetic needle, not an exact reconstruction from the *Wujing zongyao* or *Dream Pool Essays*. The texts describe direction finders and methods, but they did not preserve a scene like this.
[zh]
到了北宋，证据变得具体得多。1044年编成的官修兵书《武经总要》描述“指南鱼”：把薄铁片剪成鱼形，加热后按特定方向冷却，再让它浮在水面上，用来辨别方向。它不是活鱼，也不是现代转轴罗盘，而是一种能自由转动的铁制指向器。书中把它和机械式的指南车一同列为行军迷路时的工具。

指南车需要特别说明。传说把它追溯到黄帝，后来文献也记载工程师制造过这种车；它靠齿轮等机械结构，让车上的人像在转弯后仍朝原来的方向。它不靠磁性，也不会自动寻找磁北或磁南。早期故事的年代和真实性有争议，因此不能写成“黄帝发明了磁罗盘”。

大约1088年，沈括在《梦溪笔谈》中记录了另一种做法：用磁石摩擦针尖，使针带上磁性。他比较了让针浮在水上、放在指甲或碗沿上、用丝线悬挂等办法，认为丝线悬针比较好。这里已经不是一句模糊的“它能指南”，而是有人在比较材料、支撑方式和指向结果。
[/zh]
[en]
By the Northern Song period, the evidence becomes much more specific. The government military manual *Wujing zongyao*, completed in 1044, describes a “south-pointing fish.” A thin piece of iron was cut into a fish shape, heated, cooled while held in a set direction, and then floated on water to show direction. It was not a living fish or a modern pivoted compass. It was an iron pointer free to turn. The book lists it with the mechanical south-pointing carriage as a tool for troops that had lost their way.

The south-pointing carriage needs a careful explanation. Legends trace it back to the Yellow Emperor, and later texts report that engineers built such vehicles. Through gears and other mechanical parts, a figure on the carriage could keep facing its starting direction even after the vehicle turned. The carriage did not use magnetism, and it did not automatically seek magnetic north or south. Its earliest stories and dates are disputed, so it should never be presented as “the Yellow Emperor's magnetic compass.”

Around 1088, Shen Kuo recorded a different method in his *Dream Pool Essays*. He wrote that rubbing the point of a needle with lodestone could magnetize it. He compared a needle floating on water, balanced on a fingernail or bowl rim, and hanging from a silk thread. He thought the thread suspension worked best. This is no longer a vague statement that “something points south.” It is a record of someone comparing materials, supports, and results.
[/en]
[/section]

[section]
page: 4
layout: text-only
heading_zh: 沈括发现：它指得很稳，却不是正南
heading_en: Shen Kuo Noticed: It Was Steady, but Not Due South
[zh]
沈括还写下一个很容易被“指南针永远准确”的故事漏掉的观察：磁针通常微微偏东，并不完全指向正南。今天我们把磁针方向和地理正北之间的夹角叫作磁偏角。指南针顺着所在地的地球磁场转动，并不是像手指那样直接瞄准地图顶端的北极点。

磁偏角会随着地点不同而变化，也会随时间改变。针附近的铁器、磁铁或其他磁场还可能干扰读数。换句话说，指南针提供的是一个重要方向基准，不是一条永远零误差的答案。现代海图会标出当地磁偏角，导航者要进行修正。

沈括没有现代地球磁场理论，却认真记录了“不是正南”这个不方便的小偏差。科学史中，这样的细节常比一句“古人早就什么都知道”更珍贵：好的观察者不会把不合预期的结果擦掉。
[/zh]
[en]
Shen Kuo also recorded an observation that disappears from the simple tale “a compass is always exact.” He wrote that the needle usually pointed slightly east, not perfectly south. Today, the angle between a compass direction and geographic true north is called magnetic declination. A compass aligns with the horizontal part of Earth's magnetic field where the instrument is located. It does not point like a finger aimed straight at the North Pole at the top of a map.

Declination varies from place to place and changes over time. Iron objects, magnets, and other nearby magnetic fields can also disturb a reading. In other words, a compass supplies an important directional reference, not a guaranteed answer with zero error. Modern nautical charts show local magnetic variation so navigators can correct for it.

Shen did not have a modern theory of Earth's magnetic field, but he carefully kept the inconvenient detail that the needle was “not exactly south.” In the history of science, such a detail is often more valuable than claiming that ancient people already knew everything. A good observer does not erase a result simply because it is unexpected.
[/en]
[/section]

[section]
page: 5
layout: text-only
heading_zh: 什么时候，磁针真正上了海船？
heading_en: When Did the Magnetic Needle Go to Sea?
[zh]
沈括写磁针时，主要在讨论方向现象和装置方法，并没有清楚描写一艘船怎样靠它航行。稍后的朱彧在《萍洲可谈》中留下了更直接的航海记录。这部书谈到十一世纪末至十二世纪初的广州海船，并说舟师“夜则观星，昼则观日，阴晦观指南针”：夜里看星，白天看太阳，天色阴暗时看指南针。

这句话让我们看见指南针在船上的真实位置。它不是把所有旧知识赶下船，而是在看不见天体时补上一条重要线索。原文接着还提到用长绳和钩子探测海底泥沙。成熟的舟师还要认识海岸和岛屿，了解季风、海流与水深，记住航路，并根据船速和经过的时间估计位置。

因此，一根磁针能帮助船只在坏天气中守住大致航向，却不能告诉人前方有没有暗礁、风会不会改变，或船已经离港多远。真正的航海依靠工具、环境线索和长期经验一起工作。
[/zh]
[en]
When Shen Kuo wrote about magnetic needles, he mainly discussed directional behavior and ways to support the needle. He did not clearly describe a ship navigating by one. A somewhat later writer, Zhu Yu, left more direct maritime evidence in *Pingzhou Table Talks*. Discussing Guangzhou seagoing ships of the late eleventh and early twelfth centuries, he explained that pilots watched the stars at night, the sun by day, and the compass when the sky was overcast.

That sentence shows the compass's real place aboard ship. It did not throw every older kind of knowledge overboard. It added an important clue when sailors could not see the sky. The passage then mentions lowering a long line and hook to examine mud from the seafloor. Skilled pilots also recognized coasts and islands, understood monsoon winds, currents, and depth, remembered routes, and estimated position from speed and time.

A magnetic needle could therefore help a ship hold a rough course in bad weather. It could not report a reef ahead, predict a change of wind, or tell the crew exactly how far they had traveled from port. Real navigation joined instruments, environmental clues, and long experience.
[/en]
[/section]

[section]
page: 6
layout: text-only
heading_zh: “中国发明”不等于传播路线已经画清楚
heading_en: “Developed in China” Does Not Mean Every Route Is Known
[zh]
现存中国文献把天然磁石、指南鱼、磁化针和海船使用指南针连成了一条很重要的早期发展线。欧洲关于航海磁针的明确文字记录出现在十二世纪后期，伊斯兰世界的相关记录出现在十三世纪。时间先后让许多研究者认为，中国的磁指南知识可能通过海上贸易或欧亚交流向西传播。

可是，中间没有保存一份完整的“交接记录”。我们不知道是哪位水手把哪一种装置带到哪里，也难以证明每一种转轴、刻度盘和船用改进都只沿一条路线传递。欧洲和伊斯兰世界的人也继续试验、改变装置，并把它适应自己的航海、天文和宗教定向需要。研究者对传播路径、独立发现和各地改进所占的分量仍有讨论。

比较可靠的说法是：中国的早期磁性研究和指南工具在世界罗盘史上非常重要，宋代文本尤其提供了清楚而早的证据；后来许多地区共同改进了罗盘和航海方法。世界技术史很少像接力棒只交一次那样整齐。
[/zh]
[en]
Surviving Chinese texts form an important early line of development from lodestone to south-pointing fish, magnetized needles, and compass use aboard ships. Clear European writing about a magnetic direction finder at sea appears in the later twelfth century, while related records from the Islamic world appear in the thirteenth. This order has led many researchers to think that magnetic direction-finding knowledge may have moved west through maritime trade or wider Eurasian exchange.

But no complete “handover record” survives. We do not know which sailor carried which design to which port, and it is difficult to prove that every pivot, compass card, and maritime improvement traveled by only one route. People in Europe and the Islamic world also experimented, altered instruments, and adapted them for their own navigation, astronomy, and religious orientation. Scholars still discuss how much came through transmission, independent discovery, and local improvement.

The careful conclusion is that early Chinese work on magnetism and direction finders is extremely important to the global history of the compass, with especially clear early evidence from Song texts. Many regions later helped reshape compasses and navigation. The history of technology is rarely as tidy as passing one relay baton once.
[/en]
[/section]

[section]
page: 7
layout: text-only
heading_zh: 一根针改变的，是人怎样组合证据
heading_en: The Needle Changed How Navigators Combined Clues
[zh]
回到那艘被云遮住天空的船。舟师不会问指南针：“我家在哪里？”他会先读出大致航向，再把它和出发地点、航行时间、风、海流、水深以及记忆中的岛屿位置放在一起。磁针给出的只是一条线，但在别的线索暂时消失时，这条线可能极其重要。

从磁石到航海罗盘，真正的突破也不只是一件器物变小了。人们学会了让铁获得磁性，减少转动时的阻力，标出方向，比较误差，并把读数放进一套航海知识里。后来更稳定的支点、罗盘盘面和保护外壳继续发展，才让它成为更可靠的日常工具。

一个好工具不需要无所不知。它只要在正确的时候，提供一种人能理解、检查并与其他证据合用的信息。指南针的力量，正来自这根“不完全准确，却可以反复观察”的小针。
[/zh]
[en]
Return to the ship beneath the clouded sky. Its pilot would not ask the compass, “Where is my home?” He would read a rough heading and combine it with the departure point, time traveled, wind, current, water depth, and remembered positions of islands. The needle supplied only one line of information. When other clues disappeared, that line could matter enormously.

The change from lodestone to mariner's compass involved more than making one object smaller. People learned to magnetize iron, reduce friction as a pointer turned, mark directions, compare errors, and place the reading inside a larger body of navigational knowledge. More stable pivots, compass cards, and protective cases continued to develop, making the instrument a more dependable everyday tool.

A good tool does not have to know everything. At the right moment, it needs to provide information that people can understand, check, and combine with other evidence. The compass gained its power from a tiny needle that was not perfectly exact but could be observed again and again.
[/en]

heading_zh: 小结：不是一把神奇勺子突然带船远航
heading_en: In Summary: One Magic Spoon Did Not Suddenly Launch Ocean Voyages
[zh]
古代中国人很早认识磁石，汉代文本提到司南，但流行的磁石勺形象是有争议的现代复原。北宋《武经总要》留下指南鱼做法，沈括记录磁石磨针和磁偏现象，朱彧则清楚写到海船在阴晦天气使用指南针。

这条历史由许多观察、材料和用途慢慢连成。指南车不是磁罗盘，磁针不永远指向地理正北，航海也从未只靠一根针。真正把船带远的，是指南针与星象、海岸、水深、风、海流和人的经验共同组成的导航系统。
[/zh]
[en]
People in ancient China recognized lodestone early, and a Han text mentions the *sinan*, but the familiar lodestone spoon is a disputed modern reconstruction. The Northern Song *Wujing zongyao* preserves instructions for a south-pointing fish. Shen Kuo described magnetizing a needle and noticing its directional offset. Zhu Yu clearly recorded seagoing ships using the compass under overcast skies.

Many observations, materials, and uses gradually formed this history. The south-pointing carriage was not a magnetic compass. A magnetic needle does not always indicate geographic true north, and sailors never relied on the needle alone. What carried ships farther was a navigational system joining the compass with stars, coastlines, depth, wind, current, and human experience.
[/en]
[/ending]
