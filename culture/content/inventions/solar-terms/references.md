# Twenty-Four Solar Terms — References and Editorial Notes

This file is for editorial research and does not render on the website.

## Structure, origin, and living tradition

Source: UNESCO, “The Twenty-Four Solar Terms, Knowledge in China of Time and Practices Developed through Observation of the Sun’s Annual Motion”
URL: https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/the-twenty-four-solar-terms-knowledge-in-china-of-time-and-practices-developed-through-observation-of-the-suns-annual-motion-00647
Accessed: 2026-09-05

Supports:

- The system divides the sun's annual motion into 24 segments.
- It originated in the Yellow River reaches and developed through observation of seasons, astronomy, and other natural phenomena.
- The solar terms have guided agricultural work and daily routines and became part of rituals, festivities, rhymes, ballads, and proverbs.
- UNESCO inscribed the tradition on its Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2016.

Editorial boundary:

- The article presents the solar terms as seasonal markers, not as weather forecasts that come true everywhere on one date.
- It does not attempt to explain all 24 terms or treat the Yellow River region's observations as identical to conditions across all of China.

## Historical development

Source: China Agricultural Museum, “Origin of the Solar Terms”
URL: https://www.ciae.com.cn/detail/en/36075.html
Accessed: 2026-09-05

Supports:

- Early Chinese observers recognized the solstices and equinoxes before the complete system was assembled.
- The full set of 24 solar terms was established during the Qin and Han dynasties and recorded systematically in the *Huainanzi*.
- The system originated in the Yellow River reaches and was progressively used more widely.

Editorial boundary:

- “More than two thousand years ago” is used as a broad child-friendly chronology rather than assigning the whole system to one inventor or one day.
- The article does not turn a gradual historical development into a single invention event.

## Seasonal and agricultural meanings

Source: China Agricultural Museum, “Social Functions and Cultural Meanings”
URL: https://www.ciae.com.cn/detail/en/36077.html
Accessed: 2026-09-05

Supports:

- *Xiǎomǎn* or Lesser Fullness refers to summer grain seeds beginning to fill but not yet being ripe.
- White Dew, Cold Dew, and Frost's Descent mark successive movement toward colder conditions.
- The solar terms provided a time framework for agricultural production and daily life.

Editorial boundary:

- The crop explanation is tied to the northern or Yellow River-region context described by the source.
- The article avoids suggesting that dew or frost must appear everywhere in China on the named date.

## The sun, equinoxes, and solstices

Source: Hong Kong Observatory, “The 24 Solar Terms”
URL: https://www.hko.gov.hk/en/gts/time/24solarterms.htm
Accessed: 2026-09-05

Supports:

- The solar terms divide the sun's apparent annual path into 24 positions.
- At the equinoxes, day and night are nearly equal in length.
- In the Northern Hemisphere, daylight is longest at the Summer Solstice and shortest at the Winter Solstice.

Editorial boundary:

- The article keeps the astronomical explanation at a child-friendly level and does not introduce ecliptic longitude calculations.
- Seasonal statements are framed for the Northern Hemisphere where necessary.
