[section]
page: 1
layout: text-wrap-image
heading_zh: 最早留下的不是欢呼，而是警告
heading_en: The Earliest Clue Sounds More Like a Warning
image: 3-1-alchemy-workshop-sparks.webp
caption_zh: 这是现代绘制的炼丹室想象图，不是火药“发明现场”。人物、器皿、材料摆放和猛烈火花都没有史料依据；真实粉末可能有毒或爆燃，绝不能模仿。
caption_en: This is a modern imagined alchemy workshop, not the moment gunpowder was “invented.” The people, vessels, arranged materials, and shower of sparks are undocumented. Real powders may be toxic or ignite violently; never imitate this scene.
[zh]
古代一些炼丹者想把矿物、药材和火结合，寻找能延年益寿甚至长生不老的丹药。他们没有找到不死药，却积累了关于燃烧、烟、颜色和物质变化的经验。火药正是从这种漫长的试验传统中逐渐出现的，不是某一个人忽然喊出“我发明了火药”。

一部成书年代有讨论、通常被放在九至十世纪的炼丹文献《真元妙道要略》，记下一次危险结果：有人把硫黄、雄黄、硝石和蜜一同加热，火焰烧伤手脸，甚至烧毁房屋。雄黄还含有有毒的砷。这里没有一位可确认的“火药发明家”，也没有值得重做的实验；留下来的是一条非常清楚的安全警告。

所以这篇故事不会提供比例、研磨方法、点火方式或制作步骤。不要触摸、品尝、混合、加热或点燃来历不明的粉末，也不要拆开烟花爆竹。看到可疑材料时，离开现场并告诉可信任的成年人；遇到火情则按当地紧急规则求助。
[/zh]
[en]
Some early Chinese alchemists combined minerals, medicinal substances, and heat while searching for drugs that might extend life—or even defeat death. They never found an immortality medicine, but they gathered experience with burning, smoke, color, and material change. Gunpowder gradually emerged from this long tradition of experimentation. It was not created when one person suddenly shouted, “I have invented gunpowder!”

An alchemical text called the *Zhenyuan miaodao yaolüe*, whose date is debated but is often placed in the ninth or tenth century, records a dangerous result. It says that people heated sulfur, realgar, saltpeter, and honey together; flames burned their hands and faces and even destroyed a building. Realgar also contains poisonous arsenic. The passage gives us no identifiable “inventor” and no experiment worth repeating. What survives is a very clear safety warning.

For that reason, this story gives no ratios, grinding methods, ignition methods, or construction steps. Never touch, taste, combine, heat, or ignite unknown powders, and never take apart fireworks or firecrackers. If you see suspicious material, move away and tell a trusted adult. In a fire emergency, follow local emergency instructions.
[/en]
[/section]

page: 2
layout: text-wrap-image
heading_zh: 三类材料，不等于一个固定配方
heading_en: Three Kinds of Material Did Not Make One Fixed Formula
image: 3-2-fire-medicine-mixture.webp
caption_zh: 这是现代想象图，不是历史配方记录，更不是儿童实验示范。图中粉末无法凭颜色辨认，孩子不应靠近、研磨或加热任何未知材料。
caption_en: This is a modern imagined scene, not a historical recipe and certainly not a children's experiment. Powders cannot be identified safely by color; children should never approach, grind, or heat unknown materials.
[zh]
后来成熟的黑火药通常包含三类作用不同的材料：提供氧的硝石、硫黄，以及木炭等含碳燃料。可是“含有这三类东西”不等于已经得到性能固定的火药。材料是否纯净、颗粒状态和各种成分所占比例都会改变燃烧速度；早期混合物常更像猛烈的燃烧剂，后来提高硝石比例、改进加工，才逐步获得更强的推进或爆炸效果。

这也是为什么旧书中的危险混合物、火攻燃烧剂、推进药和能炸裂外壳的火药不能全画上等号。技术不是一个配方从天而降，而是炼丹者、药工、军器工匠和官府作坊在许多代人中试验、记录和改进的结果。具体是谁第一次跨过哪一道界线，现有证据无法回答。

在中文里，“火药”可以理解为“着火的药物或配料”。这个名称保存了它与药材和炼丹的历史联系，却不表示它曾是安全有效的药。古代有人因吞服含汞、砷等炼丹材料而中毒；“古人当药用”绝不是今天可以入口的证明。
[/zh]
[en]
Mature black powder usually contains three kinds of material with different jobs: saltpeter supplies oxygen, sulfur plays another part in the reaction, and charcoal or another carbon-rich substance serves as fuel. But simply having those three categories does not produce powder with one fixed behavior. Purity, particle form, and the share of each ingredient all affect how quickly it burns. Early mixtures often acted more like fierce incendiaries. Over time, mixtures with more saltpeter and improved processing gained stronger propelling or explosive effects.

That is why a dangerous mixture in an old book, an incendiary, a propellant, and powder able to burst a casing should not all be treated as identical. The technology did not fall from the sky as one perfect recipe. It developed as alchemists, medicine workers, military artisans, and government workshops tested, recorded, and changed materials over many generations. The evidence cannot name exactly who first crossed each technical boundary.

The Chinese name *huoyao* can be understood as “fire medicine” or “fire compound.” It preserves the technology's connection to medicinal materials and alchemy, but it does not mean gunpowder was ever a safe or effective medicine. Some people in the past were poisoned after swallowing alchemical substances containing mercury, arsenic, and other hazards. “Ancient people used it as medicine” is never proof that something is safe to eat today.
[/en]
[/section]

page: 3
layout: text-only
heading_zh: 1044年，配方进入官方兵书
heading_en: In 1044, Formulas Entered an Official Military Manual
[zh]
北宋官修兵书《武经总要》在1044年编成，保存了现存最早的一组明确火药配方。书中谈到火球、烟球和火药箭等武器。我们知道配方含硝石、硫黄和含碳材料，也混入其他物质；但这篇儿童文章不会抄出可操作的数量。重要的不是复现它，而是看见火药知识已经从炼丹警告进入有组织的军器生产。

这些早期配方的硝石比例通常低于后来最有爆炸力的黑火药，更适合燃烧、放烟或助燃。火药箭也要仔细解释：早期例子是把燃烧物附在箭上，再用弓或弩射出；“火箭”这个词当时可能只是“带火的箭”，并不一定指靠喷气自行推进的现代意义火箭。

名称很容易让人看错技术。“火炮”在旧文献中有时指由抛石机投出的火球或爆炸物，并不一定是金属大炮；“枪”原本也可以指长矛。读古代兵器时，不能看到现代熟悉的字，就自动想成今天的枪炮和火箭。
[/zh]
[en]
The Northern Song government completed the military manual *Wujing zongyao* in 1044. It preserves the earliest surviving set of clear gunpowder formulas. The book discusses fireballs, smoke balls, and gunpowder arrows. We know that its mixtures included saltpeter, sulfur, carbon-rich material, and other substances, but this children's article will not reproduce usable quantities. The important point is not to recreate them. It is that gunpowder knowledge had moved from alchemical warnings into organized military production.

These early formulas generally contained less saltpeter than later, more explosive black powder and were better suited to burning, smoking, or helping fires spread. “Fire arrows” also need careful explanation. Early examples attached an incendiary package to an arrow and then launched it with a bow or crossbow. In an old source, the words “fire arrow” may simply mean an arrow carrying fire, not a modern-style rocket moving by its own jet.

Names can make old technology look deceptively familiar. In some texts, a “fire cannon” meant a fireball or explosive thrown by a siege engine, not a metal cannon. The Chinese word later used in “fire lance” could also mean an ordinary spear. When reading about early weapons, we cannot see a familiar modern word and automatically imagine today's guns, cannons, or rockets.
[/en]
[/section]

[section]

layout: text-wrap-image
heading_zh: 火枪不是现代枪，变化发生了几百年
heading_en: A Fire Lance Was Not a Modern Gun
image: 3-3-fire-lance-and-hand-cannon.webp
caption_zh: 这是现代合成式想象图，把管状喷火器、后来的金属炮和儿童放在同一场景中；它们并非同一年代的教学器具。切勿模仿、装填或点燃任何类似装置。
caption_en: This modern composite places a tube-shaped flame weapon, a later metal cannon, and a child in one imagined scene. They were not classroom objects from one period. Never imitate, load, or ignite any similar device.
[zh]
1132年的德安守城记录提到二十多条“长竹竿火枪”，由两人共同拿着，对付靠近城墙的攻城器械。它大致是在长杆前装管状火药装置，喷出火焰；有些后来的火枪还喷出碎片。它很危险，却不是有扳机、枪管和子弹的现代步枪，也不能因为名字相同就把两者画成一步到位。

接下来的变化包括更结实的管体、更有推进力的火药，以及从管口射出的弹丸。十三世纪的中国出现爆炸性更强的炸弹记载；现存早期金属火铳也来自这一技术世界。火枪、火铳和大炮之间没有一张工匠留下的完整“进化图”，年代和个别器物身份仍会有争论，但大方向很清楚：人们逐渐从利用火焰，走向利用迅速产生的气体推动弹丸。

这种变化重新塑造了攻城与守城。石墙并没有立刻失效，弓弩、长矛和投石机也没有一夜消失；早期火器常和旧兵器一起使用。后来火炮变得更大、更可靠，城墙设计、军队训练、金属铸造和国家军费才不断跟着改变。技术革命往往是漫长的调整，不是一声爆响之后世界立刻翻页。
[/zh]
[en]
An account of the defense of De'an in 1132 mentions more than twenty “long-bamboo-shaft fire lances,” each handled by two people against siege equipment near the wall. A tube-like gunpowder device was fixed near the end of a long pole and sent out flame; some later fire lances also scattered fragments. It was dangerous, but it was not a modern rifle with a trigger, barrel, and bullet. A shared name must not make us draw the two as one instant invention.

Later changes included stronger tubes, powder with greater propelling force, and projectiles fired from the mouth of the tube. Thirteenth-century Chinese texts describe more powerful explosive bombs, and surviving early metal hand cannons belong to this changing technical world. No artisan left us one complete evolutionary chart linking fire lance, hand cannon, and large cannon, and scholars still debate some dates and objects. The broad direction is clear: people gradually moved from using flame to using rapidly produced gas to drive a projectile.

That transition reshaped attacks on and defenses of cities. Stone walls did not instantly become useless, and bows, spears, and siege engines did not disappear overnight. Early gunpowder weapons often served beside older weapons. As cannon became larger and more dependable, wall design, army training, metal casting, and government military spending kept changing. A technological revolution is often a long adjustment, not a world turning a page after one bang.
[/en]
[/section]

heading_zh: 带火的箭，还是自己飞的火箭？
heading_en: An Arrow Carrying Fire—or a Self-Propelled Rocket?

caption_zh: 这是现代对比图，不是《武经总要》器物复原。左边像弓射火药箭，右边像自行推进火箭，但早期名称常含糊，图中的尺寸、支架和飞行状态没有史料保证。
caption_en: This is a modern comparison, not a reconstruction from the *Wujing zongyao*. The left resembles a bow-launched fire arrow and the right a self-propelled rocket, but early names are often ambiguous; the sizes, stand, and flight behavior are undocumented.
[zh]
自行推进的火箭需要燃气从一个方向喷出，把装置推向相反方向。这个原理与“箭上绑一包燃烧物，再由弓射出”不同。可是古书记载常很短，“飞火枪”“火箭”等名称又可能随时代改变意思。研究者因此会问：文字有没有明确说它自己向前运动？还是仍由人投、弓射或机器抛出？

十三世纪的材料让自行运动的烟火和火箭线索越来越清楚，但“第一枚真正火箭”的时间仍不好钉死。把所有带火的箭都称为火药推进火箭，会让发展过程看起来太整齐。更准确的故事是：燃烧包、引线、管状容器、喷火装置和箭矢有许多组合，其中一部分才逐步发展出可靠的自推进效果。

同一种火药知识也走向战场以外。它可以制造烟火和爆竹，用声音与光庆祝或表达驱邪愿望；可以用于军事和民用信号。更晚时，火药爆破也帮助采矿、开路和隧道施工。可是节庆烟火和工程炸药都由受训练、获许可的专业人员管理；“用于庆祝”绝不等于“孩子可以自己做”。
[/zh]
[en]
A self-propelled rocket works when gas rushes in one direction and pushes the device in the opposite direction. That is different from tying a burning package to an arrow and launching it with a bow. Yet old records are often brief, and terms such as “flying fire lance” and “fire arrow” changed meaning over time. Historians therefore ask: Does the text clearly say that the object moved by itself? Or was it still thrown by a person, shot from a bow, or hurled by a machine?

By the thirteenth century, evidence for self-moving fireworks and rockets becomes clearer, but it is still difficult to pin down one uncontested “first true rocket.” Calling every arrow that carried fire a gunpowder-propelled rocket makes development look too tidy. A better history follows many combinations of incendiary packages, fuses, tubes, flame devices, and arrows. Only some of them gradually produced dependable self-propulsion.

The same body of gunpowder knowledge also moved beyond battlefields. It could make fireworks and firecrackers, using sound and light for celebration or hopes of driving away misfortune. It could support military and civilian signaling. In later centuries, blasting powder also helped with mining, roads, and tunnels. Festival pyrotechnics and engineering explosives, however, belong in the hands of trained and licensed professionals. “Used for celebration” never means “safe for children to make.”
[/en]
[/section]

[section]
page: 6
layout: text-only
heading_zh: 它怎样走向西亚和欧洲？没有一条画好的路线
heading_en: How Did It Reach West Asia and Europe? No Single Route Is Certain
[zh]
十三世纪，阿拉伯语和拉丁语文献开始出现硝石、火药或相关燃烧配方；十四世纪初，欧洲已有更明确的火炮图像和制造记录。这些时间晚于中国的炼丹警告、《武经总要》和宋金火器记录，说明知识大体从东向西传播，但中间的每一步并没有留下完整旅行日记。

蒙古帝国的征服把东亚、中亚、西亚和欧洲连进巨大的战争与交流网络，常被认为是重要推动力量。工匠迁移、俘虏、商贸、使节、翻译和各地自己的试验都可能参与其中。不同学者对蒙古军具体使用了什么、某段文字是否真指火药，以及配方与金属火器是否沿同一路线传播，仍有不同判断。

因此，“一个中国人把秘方沿丝绸之路送到阿拉伯，再交给欧洲人”只是过分整齐的故事。技术传播更像许多人接力：知识经过翻译会改变，各地材料和战争需要也会改变武器。西亚和欧洲工匠不是被动抄写者，中国后来也从海外重新吸收改进的火器。发明和传播都不是一个民族独自跑完全程。
[/zh]
[en]
During the thirteenth century, Arabic and Latin texts began to record saltpeter, gunpowder, or related incendiary formulas. By the early fourteenth century, Europe had clearer images and production records for cannon. These dates come after Chinese alchemical warnings, the *Wujing zongyao*, and Song-Jin records of gunpowder weapons. They support a broad movement of knowledge from east to west, but no complete travel diary preserves every step.

The Mongol conquests connected East Asia, Central Asia, West Asia, and Europe through enormous networks of war and exchange, and many historians see them as an important force in transmission. Moving artisans, captives, merchants, envoys, translators, and local experiments may all have played parts. Scholars still disagree about exactly what Mongol armies used, whether a particular passage truly describes gunpowder, and whether formulas and metal firearms traveled along the same routes.

So the tale of “one Chinese traveler carrying a secret formula down the Silk Road to the Arabs, who then handed it to Europeans” is far too neat. Technology moved more like a relay involving many people. Translation changed knowledge, while local materials and military needs changed weapons. West Asian and European artisans were not passive copyists, and China later absorbed improved firearms from overseas. No one person or culture ran the entire course alone.
[/en]
[/section]

[section]
page: 7
layout: text-only
heading_zh: 同一种力量，可以照亮夜空，也可以打碎城墙
heading_en: One Force Could Light the Sky or Break a Wall
[zh]
火药不会自己选择用途。烟花把快速燃烧变成光、声和节日记忆；信号帮助远处的人传递信息；采矿和道路工程利用爆破移动岩石。与此同时，炸弹与火炮扩大了杀伤距离和力量，让城里的平民也可能受到围攻火力。更强的城防又推动更大的炮，形成持续竞争。

火器没有单独“结束骑士时代”或决定每一场战争。训练、补给、天气、地形、疾病、政治和旧兵器仍然重要。不过，能制造大量火药与金属炮的国家往往要建立矿产、运输、作坊、仓库和税收体系。火药改变的不只是战场，也包括政府怎样组织金钱、材料和人的劳动。

寻找长生不老的人，最终没有逃过死亡；他们探索的一部分却进入了此后上千年的生活。这个反差很有故事性，但不能被简化成一次幸运爆炸。更真实的答案是：危险观察被记录，配方经过多代改变，用途由许多工匠、军人和普通人扩展，社会又不断决定如何使用并承受这种力量。
[/zh]
[en]
Gunpowder does not choose its own purpose. Fireworks turn rapid burning into light, sound, and festival memory. Signals help people communicate over distance. Mining and road building use blasting to move rock. At the same time, bombs and cannon increased the distance and force of killing, putting civilians inside cities at risk from siege fire. Stronger defenses encouraged still larger cannon in an ongoing competition.

Firearms did not single-handedly “end the age of knights” or decide every war. Training, supply, weather, terrain, disease, politics, and older weapons still mattered. Yet states that wanted large supplies of powder and metal cannon often needed networks of mines, transport, workshops, storehouses, and taxation. Gunpowder changed not only battlefields but also the ways governments organized money, materials, and human labor.

The people seeking immortality did not escape death, but part of their experimentation entered the next thousand years of human life. That irony makes a good story, yet it should not become a tale about one lucky explosion. The fuller answer is that dangerous observations were recorded, formulas changed across generations, many artisans, soldiers, and ordinary people expanded the uses, and societies kept deciding how to use—and endure—this power.
[/en]
[/section]

heading_zh: 最重要的结论不是配方，而是证据和责任
heading_en: The Most Important Lesson Is Evidence and Responsibility
[zh]
唐末到五代前后的炼丹警告、《武经总要》的兵器配方、宋金战场记录、考古器物和各地后来的文献，是不同种类的证据。它们拼出一条逐渐变化的历史，而不是一个人的发明日。

真正值得带走的不是任何配方，而是两个问题：这项技术怎样一步步形成？人们怎样为它的用途负责？任何未知粉末、烟花、弹药或爆炸装置都不应触摸、拆解、混合或点燃。
[/zh]
[en]
An alchemical warning from around the late Tang or Five Dynasties era, weapon formulas in the *Wujing zongyao*, Song and Jin battle records, archaeological objects, and later texts from many regions are different kinds of evidence. Together they show a changing history, not one person's invention day.

The lesson worth carrying away is not a recipe but two questions: How did the technology take shape step by step, and how are people responsible for its uses? Never touch, take apart, combine, or ignite any unknown powder, firework, ammunition, or explosive device.
[/en]
