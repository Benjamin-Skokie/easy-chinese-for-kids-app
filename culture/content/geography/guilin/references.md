# Editorial References: Guilin

Accessed: 2026-08-21

This file is for editorial research and does not render on the website.

Editorial boundary:

- These notes support fact-checking and image decisions; they are not learner-facing copy.

## 1. UNESCO World Heritage Centre — South China Karst

URL: https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1248

Supports:

- Guilin Karst is one component of the UNESCO-listed South China Karst serial property.
- The Guilin component includes both *fengcong* (clustered cone karst) and *fenglin* (tower karst) landscapes.
- Karst includes landforms such as towers, cones, caves, and underground drainage created in soluble rock.

Editorial boundaries:

- The article says that Guilin is part of the South China Karst World Heritage property. It does not call all of Guilin a UNESCO Global Geopark.
- UNESCO's broad geologic descriptions do not justify assigning one exact age to every visible peak or cave formation.

## 2. Sweeting, M. M. et al. — “Surface sediment characteristics and tower karst dissolution, Guilin, southern China” (*Geomorphology*)

URL: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0169555X02001885

Supports:

- Guilin's carbonate rock is relatively resistant to physical breakup but susceptible to chemical dissolution.
- Uplift, acidic water and sediments, dissolution, and erosion all contribute to the development and persistence of residual tower karst.

Editorial boundaries:

- “Water carved the mountains” is retained only as a metaphor and immediately corrected. Water did not act alone: rock structure, uplift, fractures, chemical dissolution, erosion, and collapse are different parts of the process.
- No unsupported “millions” or “tens of millions of years” countdown is attached to the present shape of a particular peak.

## 3. Guilin Lijiang Scenic Area official site — Overview of the Li River

URL: https://www.lijiangriver.com.cn/page/article/zjlj.ljjj

Supports:

- The current full sightseeing route starts at Mopan Mountain Passenger Port or Zhujiang Passenger Port outside central Guilin and ends in Yangshuo.
- The official overview gives the current route as approximately 61 kilometers.
- The official attraction list names Huangbu Reflection and Yuanbao Mountain (identified there with the 20-yuan background) separately.

Editorial boundaries:

- Older sources use other distances, often because they measure different endpoints or a broader section of the river. The article therefore identifies the current official cruise route rather than calling every “Guilin to Yangshuo” journey 80-plus kilometers.
- The site's separate attraction labels are one reason not to promise children an undisputed, exact camera point for the banknote.

## 4. People's Bank of China — Guide to the Fifth Series of Renminbi

URL: https://guiyang.pbc.gov.cn/guiyang/113337/114084/3540759/2018051811593716305.pdf

Supports:

- The official description identifies the reverse design of the fifth-series 20-yuan note as “Guilin scenery” (桂林山水).

Editorial boundaries:

- The central bank guide names the subject but does not supply an exact latitude, camera position, or claim that the engraving is a mechanically exact photograph of one view.
- Tourism materials commonly associate the picture with the Xingping/Huangbu Reflection reach, so the article presents that as a widely used association rather than an undisputed one-point match.

## 5. U.S. National Park Service — *Cave Geology* (Carlsbad Caverns)

URL: https://www.nps.gov/cave/planyourvisit/upload/cave_geology.pdf

Supports:

- Weakly acidic water dissolves limestone; after water enters an air-filled cave and loses carbon dioxide, calcite can be deposited as cave formations.
- Speleothems do not all grow at the same rate. Growth varies with rainfall, carbon dioxide, water supply, rock, fractures, and other local conditions.
- Size alone is not a reliable measure of age.

Editorial boundaries:

- The old fixed claim “one centimeter always takes more than 100 years” and the calculation of age directly from height were removed.
- The article distinguishes cave enlargement by dissolution from the later deposition that builds stalactites and stalagmites.

## 6. Princeton University Art Museum — Chinese Landscape Painting teaching resource

URL: https://static.artmuseum.princeton.edu/asian-art/china/resources/landscape-painting

Supports:

- *Shanshui* literally means “mountain-water,” and handscroll viewing creates a journey that unfolds section by section.
- Unpainted or negative space can be as important as painted forms, while ink tones and shifting viewpoints suggest texture, depth, and distance.

Editorial boundaries:

- Blank paper is described as capable of suggesting water, sky, mist, or distance depending on context—not as an automatic symbol that always means water.
- Guilin is used as a helpful comparison, not as the single origin or cause of Chinese landscape aesthetics.
- “Freehand expression” is not treated as a timeless rule for every Chinese landscape painter; styles and artistic goals varied across periods.
