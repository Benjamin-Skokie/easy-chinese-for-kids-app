## Current Story and Editorial Boundaries

- The final story begins with the Guilin scene on the 20-yuan note, follows a boat toward Yangshuo, connects the changing view to a Chinese landscape scroll, and then explains how limestone and water create towers and caves.
- Chinese place names and key art words receive tone-marked pinyin on first use: *Guìlín*, *Lí Jiāng*, *Yángshuò*, and *shānshuǐ*.
- Exact banknote-viewpoint debates, fixed cave-growth rates, sightseeing-route mileage, and specialist geology terms are intentionally omitted.

## 1. The 20-Yuan Banknote

People's Bank of China, *Guide to the Fifth Series of Renminbi*

URL: https://guiyang.pbc.gov.cn/guiyang/113337/114084/3540759/2018051811593716305.pdf

Supports:

- The official description identifies the main reverse design of the fifth-series 20-yuan note as “Guilin scenery” (桂林山水).
- The object shown to children is a designed and engraved banknote image based on a real landscape, not a photograph printed directly on the money.

Editorial boundary:

- The official guide does not document one photographer, one source photograph, or one exact camera position. The article therefore avoids claims about a single matching viewpoint.

## 2. A Boat from Guilin toward Yangshuo

Guilin Lijiang Scenic Area official site, “Overview of the Li River”

URL: https://www.lijiangriver.com.cn/page/article/zjlj.ljjj

Supports:

- Sightseeing boats travel along the Li River from outside central Guilin toward Yangshuo.
- Yangshuo is a riverside destination south of Guilin, surrounded by the same karst landscape.

Editorial boundary:

- The child-facing story follows the changing view from a moving boat. It does not ask children to remember ports, route lengths, or lists of named attractions.

## 3. Why Guilin Has Stone Towers

UNESCO World Heritage Centre, “South China Karst”

URL: https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1248

Sweeting, M. M. et al., “Surface Sediment Characteristics and Tower Karst Dissolution, Guilin, Southern China,” *Geomorphology*

URL: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0169555X02001885

Supports:

- Guilin is a major example of tower and cone karst formed in soluble rock.
- Limestone, cracks, uplift, mildly acidic water, dissolution, and erosion work together to create caves and steep remaining peaks.

Editorial boundary:

- The explanation uses the child-sized sequence “ancient sea → limestone → cracks → water → caves and towers.” It does not assign one exact age or growth rate to a particular peak.

## 4. The American Karst Comparison and Cave Formations

U.S. National Park Service, “Mammoth Cave Map & Guide”

URL: https://home.nps.gov/maca/planyourvisit/mammoth-cave-map-guide.htm

U.S. National Park Service, “How Mammoth Cave Formed”

URL: https://home.nps.gov/maca/learn/nature/how-mammoth-cave-formed.htm

U.S. National Park Service, “Stalactites, Stalagmites, and Cave Formations”

URL: https://www.nps.gov/maca/learn/nature/stalactites-stalagmites-and-cave-formations.htm

Supports:

- Mammoth Cave National Park in Kentucky is a U.S. karst landscape containing the world's longest known cave system.
- Mildly acidic groundwater slowly dissolves limestone and enlarges cracks into cave passages.
- Stalactites hang from ceilings, stalagmites grow from floors, and both are mineral deposits left by water.

Editorial boundary:

- Mammoth Cave gives American children a familiar example of the same kind of rock-and-water process. Its surface is not presented as looking like Guilin's forest of stone towers.

## 5. Why the Boat Ride Feels Like a Landscape Scroll

Princeton University Art Museum, Chinese Landscape Painting teaching resource

URL: https://static.artmuseum.princeton.edu/asian-art/china/resources/landscape-painting

Supports:

- *Shānshuǐ* literally joins the words “mountain” and “water.”
- A handscroll opens section by section and gives the viewer the feeling of traveling through changing scenery.
- Blank paper, different ink tones, and changing viewpoints can suggest mist, water, depth, and distance.

Editorial boundary:

- The article compares the rhythm of the Li River boat ride with opening a landscape scroll. It does not claim that Guilin was the single source of Chinese landscape painting.
