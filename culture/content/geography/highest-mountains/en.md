# Where Are the Highest Mountains?

## Qomolangma: One of the Closest Places to the Sky

Where is the highest mountain on Earth? It is not in Europe, Africa, or the Americas. It is in Asia, on the border between China and Nepal.

The mountain is more than 8,800 meters high. In Chinese, it is called Qomolangma. Many English speakers call it Mount Everest.

Try imagining a stack of very tall skyscrapers. Even then, Everest would still feel shockingly high.

And the mountain is still changing, very slowly. A giant piece of Earth's crust under India keeps pushing into a giant piece under Asia. That push helps lift the Himalayas. Your fingernails may grow about 30 millimeters in a year. A mountain may rise only a tiny bit in that time. But give the Earth millions of years, and that slow push can lift an ancient sea floor into high mountains.

---

## A Snow Line That Seems to Move

The top of Everest is covered with ice and snow all year. The air is freezing cold, often far below zero. Strong winds blow again and again.

But near the foot of the mountain, on the southern side of the Himalayas, the world can look completely different. At around 3,000 or 4,000 meters high, there can be green forests and flowers.

That is one amazing thing about the world's highest mountains. From snowy peaks down to warm valleys, the change in height can be more than 6,000 or 7,000 meters. It is almost like traveling from the icy far north toward hot lands near the middle of the Earth, but going up and down instead of north and south.

---

## Who Does Everest Belong To?

The north side of Everest is in Tibet, China. The south side is in Nepal. The very top sits on the border, so it is shared by two countries.

The first people most historians accept as reaching the top were Edmund Hillary from New Zealand and Tenzing Norgay, a Nepali Sherpa guide. They climbed from the south side in 1953.

Chinese climbers also made an important climb from the north side. In 1960, a Chinese team reached the top from Tibet. That was a very difficult and important moment in China's climbing history.

---

## More Than One High Peak

Everest is not the only great mountain on the Qinghai-Tibet Plateau. Around the world, there are 14 mountains higher than 8,000 meters. Quite a few are fully or partly inside China's borders. Most are in the Himalayas and nearby mountain areas.

Farther east, China also has the Kunlun Mountains, the Tianshan Mountains, and Changbai Mountain.

The poet Li Bai once wrote about seeing snow on faraway western mountains from a window. That line gives a feeling many people in Sichuan know well: you may be in a city, then look up and see snowy mountains far away.

---

## Summary

Qomolangma, or Mount Everest, is more than 8,800 meters high and stands on the border between China and Nepal. It is not a lonely mountain by itself. It is the most famous peak in the huge world of the Himalayas and the Qinghai-Tibet Plateau. Snow peaks, deep valleys, forests, wind, and ice stack up layer by layer. This is one of Asia's most powerful mountain worlds.
