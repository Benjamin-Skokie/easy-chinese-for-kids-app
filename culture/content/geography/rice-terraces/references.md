## 1. UNESCO World Heritage Centre — “Cultural Landscape of Honghe Hani Rice Terraces”

URL: https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1111/
Accessed: 2026-09-05

Supports:

- The Honghe Hani Rice Terraces are in southern Yunnan and have developed over roughly 1,300 years.
- In some places, as many as 3,000 terraces descend between the lower edge of the forest and the valley floor.
- The landscape forms an integrated system of mountaintop forests, water supply, villages, and terraces.
- Rainwater is held and released through the mountain landscape, while an extensive network of channels and branch ditches carries water around the slopes and into the fields.
- The wider farming system has included red rice, water buffalo, cattle, ducks, fish, and eels.

Editorial boundary:

- The phrase “real-life Minecraft” is a modern comparison for children. It does not imply any association with or endorsement by Minecraft or its owner.
- The terraces were developed and maintained across many generations, not built as one construction project 1,300 years ago.

## 2. Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations — “Hani Rice Terraces, China”

URL: https://www.fao.org/giahs/giahs-around-the-world/china-hani-rice-terraces/en
Accessed: 2026-09-05

Supports:

- The Hani landscape places forests above villages and terraces below the villages.
- Forests help accumulate and release water into streams and creeks.
- Fish and ducks are raised or harvested from paddies, streams, and ponds, while livestock form part of the larger agricultural system.

Editorial boundary:

- The article presents the terraces as a living agricultural and water-management system, not only as a scenic view.

## 3. Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations — “Rice Fish Culture, China”

URL: https://www.fao.org/giahs/giahs-around-the-world/china-rice-fish-culture/en
Accessed: 2026-09-05

Supports:

- Evidence for rice-fish culture in China reaches back about 2,000 years; a Han-period clay object shows a fish moving from a pond toward a rice field.
- Fish can eat larvae and weeds, stir water, soften soil, and add fertilizer, while rice provides shade and food for fish.

Editorial boundary:

- This source documents the long history of rice-fish farming in China generally. It does not prove that the Honghe Hani terraces have used precisely the same rice-fish practices continuously for 2,000 years.

## Editorial synthesis

- The article follows one central question: how people transformed a steep mountain into a working system for water, rice, and other living things.
- The Chinese text uses 云南 without repeating its pinyin. The English text uses the tone-marked forms *Yúnnán* and *Hāní*.
- The English conclusion uses the present tense—“Here, people reshape the real world”—to emphasize that the landscape is still living and maintained.
