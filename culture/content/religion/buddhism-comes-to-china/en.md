# The White Horse That Carried Buddhism to China

## The Emperor Had a Dream

The most famous opening to the story of Buddhism coming to China is a dream.

Old books say that around the year 64 CE, Emperor Ming of the Eastern Han dynasty dreamed of a shining golden person. A ring of white light glowed around the figure’s head. The figure flew past the palace and drifted toward the west.

The next morning at court, the emperor told his ministers about the dream. Someone said, “People say there is a holy teacher in the West called the Buddha. Maybe Your Majesty dreamed of him.”

Later, the court sent people west to look for Buddhist teachings.

In the story, the messengers traveled toward what is now Central Asia. There they met two monks, She Moteng and Zhu Falan. The monks brought Buddhist images and scriptures and slowly traveled east with the messengers.

When they reached Luoyang, people later called the temple there White Horse Temple. The name remembered the white horse said to have carried the scriptures. In traditional Chinese stories, White Horse Temple is often seen as one of the earliest Buddhist temples in China.

---

## When Buddhism Met China

Some parts of the White Horse Temple story may be legend, but the story still points to something real.

After Buddhism entered China, it did not stay exactly the same. Little by little, it changed and grew in a Chinese way.

In early Indian art, Guanyin was often shown as male. In China, Guanyin’s image slowly became gentler and closer to the kind, motherly figure many people know today.

Chan Buddhism, later known in Japan as Zen, also grew in China. It took in many Chinese ideas. Chan teachers often said that wisdom was not found only in books and written words. A person also had to look clearly into their own mind.

The flying figures in Dunhuang murals show this mixing too. Their roots were tied to Indian art, but in China their ribbons became lighter, and their bodies seemed to float more and more like clouds in a Chinese painting.

Buddhism also brought many new words and ideas into Chinese. Words such as “world,” “cause and effect,” “fate or connection,” and “moment” became part of everyday speech. Today, when someone says, “We have yuanfen,” meaning a special connection or fate, they are hearing an echo from cultural exchange long ago.

## White Horse Temple Is Still There Today

Today, if you walk into White Horse Temple in Luoyang, you can still see halls, old trees, and stone steps.

There is a place in the temple called Qingliang Terrace. In traditional stories, it is often tied to early translation work. The monks She Moteng and Zhu Falan, and the text called the Sutra in Forty-Two Sections, became famous parts of the story of early Buddhist translation into Chinese.

On holidays, worshippers still come and go. The sound of bells, the smell of incense, the stone steps, and the old trees make it easy to imagine a long road from far away.

Along that road, Buddhist scriptures came step by step into China.

---

## Summary

The most famous image in the story of Buddhism coming east is the white horse. Legend, old records, and later imagination all came together and made White Horse Temple a symbol of Buddhism’s arrival in China.

But the more important story is what happened next. Buddhism did not stay still. It mixed with Chinese language, art, and thought, and slowly grew into something new.
