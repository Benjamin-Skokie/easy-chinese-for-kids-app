# The Smile in the Temple: Maitreya Buddha's Big Belly

## A Big Buddha Who Laughs

Walk into many Buddhist temples in China, and one of the first figures you may see is a laughing Buddha with a big round belly.

Other Buddhist figures often look more serious. Shakyamuni Buddha may sit with quiet eyes. Fierce guardian figures may stare with wide, angry eyes.

But Maitreya grins. His round belly pushes out from his robe. Many children want to touch it as they pass by. Adults often do not stop them.

"Touch it," they may say. "May you keep smiling."

This image is very different from older pictures of Maitreya from India. In Indian Buddhism, Maitreya was the future Buddha, often shown as a tall and graceful holy figure. His old Indian name means something like "the kind one." He is said to be waiting in a Buddhist heaven called Tushita until the time comes for him to become a Buddha.

Later in China, people slowly connected this big-bellied, laughing image with a real monk who once lived.

---

## The Cloth-Bag Monk

In the late Tang time and the years after it, a strange monk wandered around the Fenghua area of Zhejiang.

No one knew where he came from.

He was short and round. He carried a large cloth bag everywhere. He walked through streets, alleys, and temples. When he was hungry, he took a bowl from his bag and begged for food. When he was sleepy, he hugged his bag and slept under the edge of a roof.

No one gave him a proper home. He did not seem to care.

He did not spend much time talking about Buddhist lessons. He mostly did one thing:

he laughed.

Children followed him, and he laughed.

Dogs barked at him, and he laughed.

Someone splashed dirty water on his face. He did not even wipe it off. He still laughed.

Once, someone asked him, "Monk, you are always laughing. What is so funny?"

He reached into his cloth bag, took out his food bowl, turned it upside down on his head, and kept walking.

A monk nearby saw this and seemed to understand something at once. Some things, the Cloth-Bag Monk did not plan to explain directly.

He almost never gave serious talks about Buddhist lessons, but he left a deep impression on many people.

Before he died, he sat on a flat rock at Yuelin Temple in Fenghua and spoke a short poem:

"Maitreya, true Maitreya,
with hundreds and thousands of forms.
He shows himself to people all the time,
but people do not recognize him."

The meaning is something like this: Maitreya may appear in many different forms in the world, but people may not know who they are seeing.

After that, the monk died.

Later, many temple statues of Maitreya slowly began to look like him: big belly, big ears, and a smile always on the face.

## Why People Like to Touch Maitreya's Belly

There is a famous two-line saying about Maitreya Buddha in China. It is carved at the entrances of many temples:

"A big belly can hold what the world finds hard to hold.
An open mouth laughs at the people and things worth laughing at."

The words are not just about the size of his belly.

They are about a heart large enough to hold trouble, anger, and silly human problems without breaking.

His smile feels as if he has looked at the messy world, shaken his head a little, and laughed anyway.

That is why many people stop in front of him, whether or not they know much about Buddhism.

His belly and smile feel like comfort.

---

## Summary

The Maitreya Buddha many Chinese people know is often a fat, smiling monk with a big belly. People connect this image with the Cloth-Bag Monk, who wandered around in the late Tang time carrying a bag and laughing at almost everything.

He did not explain long teachings. But with one bag and one smiling face, he made people remember him.

Later, the big-bellied, laughing Maitreya became common in Chinese temples. As the two-line saying tells us, his big belly can hold what the world finds hard to hold.
