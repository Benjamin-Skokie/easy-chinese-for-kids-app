# Tea — References and Editorial Notes

This file is for editorial research and does not render on the website.

## Watching tea leaves in a glass

Source: Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Affairs of China, “2022 International Tea Day — New Ways of Drinking Tea” (《2022“国际茶日”茶故事｜爱茶新生代 饮茶新方式》)
URL: https://gjcr.moa.gov.cn/czx/202205/t20220517_6399546.htm
Accessed: 2026-09-05

Supports:

- Fine green teas such as Xihu Longjing are often brewed in a transparent glass so the leaves can be watched as they open and move.
- This movement is sometimes called a “tea dance.”

Editorial boundary:

- The story presents the glass as a common brewing choice, not the only correct way to prepare green tea.

## Xihu Longjing processing and shape

Source: Zhejiang Administration for Market Regulation, *Technical Specification for Hand Processing of Xihu Longjing Tea* (《西湖龙井茶手工炒制技术规程》)
URL: https://zlzx.zjamr.zj.gov.cn/bzzx/rest/redirect/files/localFile/2023-07-03/62bfc1f2e5653c150985b14bdbc96b70.pdf
Accessed: 2026-09-05

Supports:

- Traditional hand processing uses a series of wok-frying motions to heat, move, press, and shape the leaves.
- Finished Xihu Longjing leaves are characteristically flat, smooth, slender, and green.

Editorial boundary:

- The child-facing account names only a few representative hand movements and does not turn the technical standard into a memorization list.

## Biluochun processing and curled leaves

Source: Suzhou Municipal Government, “Bi Luo Chun Tea in Suzhou”
URL: https://english.suzhou.gov.cn/szsenglish/sgy/201911/a43de9d366404c0bafd31bfc105c95f2.shtml
Accessed: 2026-09-05

Supports:

- Traditional Biluochun processing curls the warm leaves and brings out their fine pale hairs.
- Finished Biluochun leaves are described as curled like snails and green in color.

Editorial boundary:

- The name is explained through the tea's familiar appearance without presenting one naming legend as proven history.

## Biluochun tea-and-fruit gardens

Source: Suzhou Municipal Government, *Measures for the Protection and Management of Dongting Mountain Biluochun Tea Bases* (《苏州市洞庭山碧螺春茶基地保护管理办法》)
URL: https://www.suzhou.gov.cn/szsrmzf/szfgfxwjk/202507/3ff398f1f51243b38806d8328f9b085b.shtml
Accessed: 2026-09-05

Supports:

- Dongting Mountain Biluochun gardens use a tea-and-fruit intercropping system.
- Fruit trees grown with the tea include loquat, citrus, Chinese bayberry, chestnut, and plum.

Editorial boundary:

- The story gives several examples of companion fruit trees and does not claim that every garden contains the same mixture.

## Green tea in China's domestic market

Source: China Tea Marketing Association, *2023 China Tea Production and Sales Report* (《2023年度中国茶叶产销形势报告》)
URL: https://www.ctma.com.cn/zhuanyebaogao/77663.html
Accessed: 2026-09-05

Supports:

- Green tea accounted for 53.6 percent of China's domestic tea sales volume in 2023, making it the largest tea category by volume.

Editorial boundary:

- The story says green tea is the largest category sold inside China today without asking children to remember the percentage.

## Black tea developed later

Source: Jianghua County Government, “Black Tea: China's Gift to the World” (《红茶：中国送给世界的礼物》)
URL: https://www.jh.gov.cn/jh/kpjh/202403/c253f5508bb0470981127abd80577afd.shtml
Accessed: 2026-09-05

Supports:

- China is identified as the birthplace of black tea, whose development is commonly placed about four centuries ago.
- Black-tea processing allows the leaves to change farther than green-tea processing before drying.

Editorial boundary:

- The story does not include the unverified Lapsang Souchong accident legend or ask children to memorize its place of origin.

## Different processing paths from tea leaves

Source: UNESCO, “Traditional Tea Processing Techniques and Associated Social Practices in China”
URL: https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/traditional-tea-processing-techniques-and-associated-social-practices-in-china-01884
Accessed: 2026-09-05

Supports:

- Chinese tea traditions include knowledge and skills for growing, picking, processing, drinking, and sharing tea.
- Different processing choices produce teas with varied shapes, colors, aromas, and flavors.

Editorial boundary:

- The article explains only the basic contrast between early heating for green tea and allowing greater change for black tea; it does not introduce a six-category chart.

## Loose-leaf brewing and tea vessels

Source: Beijing Party Member Education, “How to Brew Fine Teas” (《名贵绿茶最好用瓷杯泡饮》)
URL: https://www.bjcc.gov.cn/article/50178643.html
Accessed: 2026-09-05

Supports:

- Fine green teas are commonly brewed loose in transparent glasses so their leaves can be seen.
- High-quality Chinese black teas may be brewed loose in porcelain or glass gaiwan vessels.

Editorial boundary:

- A tea bag is described only as a packaging method, not as a defining feature of black tea.

## Tea Horse Roads and porters

Source: Ya'an Municipal Government, “The Tea Horse Road: A Tea Leaf's Journey” (《茶马古道，一片茶叶的旅程》)
URL: https://www.yaan.gov.cn/xinwen/show/d86618b9-f9f9-4607-b56c-6fbcbe935404.html
Accessed: 2026-09-05

Supports:

- The Tea Horse Roads formed a network of routes through difficult mountain terrain rather than one single road.
- On steep sections where animals could not pass, porters carried tea and used wooden staffs to support their loads while resting.

Editorial boundary:

- The illustration represents a mountain caravan generally and is not presented as one documented journey or a complete picture of the route network.
