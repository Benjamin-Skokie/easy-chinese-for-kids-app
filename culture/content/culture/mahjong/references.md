# Editorial References: Mahjong: Before the Tiles, There Were Cards

This file is for editorial research and does not render on the website.

## 1. Hsi-yüan Chen, Institute of History and Philology, Academia Sinica — “Madiao and Mahjong in Popular Culture and Elite Discourse”

URL: https://www1.ihp.sinica.edu.tw/en/Publications/Bulletin/59/Article/207
Accessed: 2026-09-05

Supports:

- The paper identifies *mǎdiào* as a card game popular in the late Ming and mahjong as its successor emerging in the late Qing.
- It traces mahjong’s movement across urban and social settings, including its spread to elite circles and the imperial court.
- It records mahjong’s rapid popularity in China and its overseas craze in the 1920s.

Editorial boundary:

- The article presents a family connection rather than claiming that modern mahjong was invented directly from one fixed pack of *mǎdiào* cards.
- The child-facing explanation compresses a complex, gradual history of borrowed rules, changing formats, and regional variants.

## 2. The British Museum — Mahjong set, As1908,0416.1.b

URL: https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/A_As1908-0416-1-b
Accessed: 2026-09-05

Supports:

- The museum describes a nineteenth-century Chinese mahjong set made from bone, bamboo, and pigment.
- The object record supports the description of a carved and colored bone face joined to a bamboo back.

Editorial boundary:

- This surviving set demonstrates one documented construction method; it is not treated as the design of every nineteenth-century mahjong set.

## 3. The British Museum — *China’s Hidden Century*

URL: https://www.britishmuseum.org/sites/default/files/2023-09/Chinas_hidden_century_large_print_guide.pdf
Accessed: 2026-09-05

Supports:

- The guide describes mahjong as developing in China in the 1800s.
- It documents a surviving nineteenth-century set made from bone, wood, and bamboo.

Editorial boundary:

- The article uses the broad nineteenth-century date and does not claim a single inventor, birthplace, or exact year.

## 4. The British Museum — Mahjong set, OA+.7013

URL: https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/A_OA-7013
Accessed: 2026-09-05

Supports:

- The museum records a Qing-dynasty mahjong set made from ivory and bamboo.

Editorial boundary:

- Ivory is mentioned as one historical material among several, not as the standard material for all Qing mahjong sets.

## 5. The British Museum — “Top 10 Historical Board Games: Mahjong”

URL: https://www.britishmuseum.org/blog/top-10-historical-board-games
Accessed: 2026-09-05

Supports:

- The overview places mahjong in Qing-era China and describes its spread during the nineteenth century.
- It provides visual and historical context for tile suits, materials, and the recognizable form of mahjong.

Editorial boundary:

- The article does not use the museum overview to impose one regional ruleset on the many forms of mahjong played today.
