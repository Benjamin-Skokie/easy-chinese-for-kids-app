## 1. China Culture — “Chinese Knot”

URL: https://en.chinaculture.org/focus/focus/2010spring/2009-01/20/content_367236.htm
Accessed: 2026-09-05

Supports:

- A defining feature of Chinese decorative knots is that each knot can be made from a single cord.
- Knots are named for forms and meanings and can be combined into ornaments carrying wishes and auspicious ideas.

Editorial boundary:

- “Many traditional Chinese knots” is used in the story rather than claiming that every complex hanging ornament consists of only one uninterrupted cord.

## 2. China Culture — “Macrame / Chinese Knotting”

URL: https://en.chinaculture.org/chineseway/2014-04/29/content_523804.htm
Accessed: 2026-09-05

Supports:

- Chinese knotting commonly uses symmetrical structures.
- Making a finished knot involves tying, gradually tightening, adjusting loop size and tension, and adding finishing touches.
- The *páncháng* knot is formed from continuous loops and is associated with continuity.

Editorial boundary:

- The story explains the handwork in child-facing language and does not present the illustrations as step-by-step technical instructions.

## 3. 《周易·系辞下》 and 国家统计局 — 《结绳记事》

URL: https://www.stats.gov.cn/zs/tjlshi/gd/202301/t20230101_1903803.html
Accessed: 2026-09-05

Supports:

- 《周易·系辞下》保存了“上古结绳而治”的文本传统。
- 后世郑玄注释有“大事，大结其绳；事小，小结其绳”的说法。

Editorial boundary:

- These texts document an ancient textual tradition about tied-cord record keeping. They do not prove a direct, uninterrupted line from those record-keeping knots to the decorative Chinese knots used today.
- The supplied story carefully states that the two belonged to different periods and uses.

## 4. 《悠悠古韵 千千情结》

URL: https://www.nydi.gov.cn/sitesources/nysjwjw/page_pc/xcjd/s/article9bc0e1521e6a4d2eaa08faf0830d5c92.html
Accessed: 2026-09-05

Supports:

- Decorative knot types include 同心结、如意结、团锦结、蝴蝶结 and 盘长结.
- 如意结 is associated with wishes that things go as desired; 同心结 is associated with closeness and lasting union.

Editorial boundary:

- Knot names and meanings can vary by maker, context, and arrangement. The story presents common associations rather than an exhaustive fixed dictionary.

## 5. The Metropolitan Museum of Art — “Woman’s Informal Robe with Garden Roundels”

URL: https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/70383
Accessed: 2026-09-05

Supports:

- An early-eighteenth-century Chinese robe uses the endless knot as an auspicious element.

Editorial boundary:

- The museum object documents the endless knot as an auspicious motif in Qing art; it is not evidence that every modern *páncháng* decoration has one exclusive meaning.

## 6. Smithsonian’s National Museum of Asian Art — “Chinese Red”

URL: https://asia-archive.si.edu/red/
Accessed: 2026-09-05

Supports:

- Red has long been associated in China with auspiciousness, happiness, celebration, and prosperity.

Editorial boundary:

- Red has carried many meanings in different settings. The story discusses its familiar celebratory and auspicious use in decorative knots.

## Editorial synthesis

- The supplied Chinese and English manuscripts remain the final writing base. Excess line breaks were merged into complete thought-based paragraphs; wording and claims were not rewritten.
- Ancient tied-cord record keeping is presented as a textual tradition and later explanation, not as the proven direct ancestor of modern decorative Chinese knotting.
- The story distinguishes craft technique from symbolism: looping and tightening describe the handwork, while lasting happiness, things going as wished, and united hearts are common cultural associations.
- Scholarly and institutional sources were used to verify boundaries, not added automatically as story material.
