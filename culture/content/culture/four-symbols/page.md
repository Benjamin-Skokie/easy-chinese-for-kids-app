[section]
page: 1
layout: text-only
heading_zh: 夜空里，出现了四种动物
heading_en: Four Creatures in the Night Sky
[zh]
想象一片星空：东方，一条青绿色的长龙舒展开身体，它叫青龙。南方，一只红色大鸟张开双翼，那是朱雀。西方的白虎昂着头，仿佛正要迈出一步。到了北方，画面更特别了：一条蛇盘绕着一只龟，它们合在一起，叫作玄武。

这四种形象合称“四象”。在中国古代，它们各自代表天空的一个方向。它们不只出现在神话和绘画里，还和人们观察星星、认识季节有关。四种动物，怎么会和那么大的一片天空连在一起呢？
[/zh]
[en]
Picture a blue-green dragon coiling across the eastern sky. Its name is Qīnglóng. To the south, a bright red bird called Zhūquè spreads its wings. Báihǔ, a white tiger, prowls through the west. The northern creature is a surprise: a snake curls around a tortoise. Together, they make Xuánwǔ.

These are the Four Symbols. In ancient China, each one stood for a different part of the sky. They look ready for an adventure, but they also helped people picture the stars and the changing seasons. How could a dragon, a bird, a tiger, and a tortoise with a snake do all that?
[/en]
[/section]

[section]
page: 2
layout: two-column
heading_zh: 给满天星星分分组
heading_en: How Do You Map a Sky Full of Stars?
image: 2-four-symbols-night-sky.webp
caption_zh: 四象在星光中现身，每一个都守着自己的方向。
caption_en: The Four Symbols glow among the stars, each watching over a different direction.
[zh]
很久以前，没有望远镜，也没有手机里的星空软件。人们一晚又一晚地仰望夜空，认出熟悉的星星，留意月亮和行星走到了哪里。满天星星，要怎样才能说清它们的位置呢？

中国古人用一些星星组成了二十八组，叫作“二十八宿”。这里的“宿”读作 *xiù*。可以把它们想成天空路上的一个个站点，帮助人们记录日月和行星经过的位置。

二十八宿又分成四大组，每组七宿，分别对应东、南、西、北。四象就和这四组星宿连在一起：青龙代表东方七宿，朱雀代表南方七宿，白虎代表西方七宿，玄武代表北方七宿。一条龙的形象，代表的可是好大一片星空。
[/zh]
[en]
Long before telescopes or stargazing apps, people went outside and looked up. Night after night, they recognized familiar stars and watched the Moon and planets move among them. But with so many stars overhead, how could they describe where to look?

Chinese sky-watchers used twenty-eight groups of stars called *xiù*. Think of them as stops along a path across the sky. They helped people keep track of the paths of the Sun, Moon, and planets.

The twenty-eight groups belonged to four bigger sets of seven, one for each direction. Each set had an animal symbol: the dragon, the red bird, the tiger, or the tortoise and snake. So Qīnglóng was more than a dragon in a picture. Its long, curling shape stood for a whole stretch of the eastern sky, with seven star groups of its own.
[/en]
[/section]

[section]
page: 3
layout: text-only
heading_zh: 四个方向，四个季节
heading_en: Around the Sky, Around the Year
[zh]
四象不只和方向有关，还各自对应一个季节。青龙在东方，对应春天；朱雀在南方，对应夏天；白虎在西方，对应秋天；玄武在北方，对应冬天。

想想春天枝头冒出的新叶，再看看青龙的颜色。青绿色和春天放在一起，是不是很容易记住？朱雀红红的翅膀，又让人想到火和夏日的炎热。古人还把青龙和木、朱雀和火、白虎和金、玄武和水联系起来。在他们眼里，四象既代表四方的天空，也和一年中的变化连在一起。
[/zh]
[en]
The Four Symbols also had a place in the year. The eastern dragon went with spring, and the southern bird with summer. The western tiger went with fall, and the northern tortoise and snake with winter.

Some of those connections are easy to picture. The dragon's blue-green color can bring to mind fresh spring leaves. The bird's red wings can remind us of fire and summer heat. People linked Qīnglóng with wood, Zhūquè with fire, Báihǔ with metal, and Xuánwǔ with water. These connections were part of how people in ancient China thought about nature. The four animals brought the sky and the changing year into one picture.
[/en]
[/section]

[section]
page: 4
layout: two-column
heading_zh: 屋檐上，也有四象
heading_en: Look Up: Guardians at the Roof's Edge
image: 3-four-symbols-wadang.webp
image_aspect: wide
caption_zh: 白虎纹样排在屋檐边，圆圆的瓦当连着后面弯曲的筒瓦。
caption_en: A row of White Tigers lines the roof's edge. Each round face is attached to a curved roof tile.
[zh]
再把目光从星空移到屋檐边。图里一排圆圆的瓦片上，怎么都有一只老虎？它们正是白虎纹瓦当。

屋顶上一条条弯曲的筒瓦，伸到屋檐前端时，朝外露出一个圆形的瓦头，这就是瓦当。它们用泥土烧制，既保护屋檐，也让屋顶变得好看。白虎的身体和尾巴顺着圆圆的瓦面展开，图案和瓦片是同一种陶土的颜色。

两千多年前的汉代，人们已经把四象做成瓦当纹样。除了白虎，还有盘旋的青龙、展翅的朱雀和龟蛇相缠的玄武。工匠把图案压进泥坯，再烧成坚硬的陶瓦，装到屋檐上。

铜镜、砖块上也能找到四象。人们把它们看作守护者，寄托着平安的愿望。在这里，代表大片星空的白虎，变成了屋檐边一个个小小的守护者。
[/zh]
[en]
Now look along the edge of the roof in the picture. There is a row of round clay faces, each with a tiger on it. The White Tiger has turned up again, this time much closer to the ground.

Each round face is called a *wǎdāng*. It sits at the end of a curved roof tile, facing outward. These decorated ends helped protect the roof's edge as well as make it beautiful. Here, the tiger's body and tail fit inside the circle. The tiger and its tile are the same clay color.

More than 2,000 years ago, during the Han dynasty, people made roof tiles with all four designs. There were coiling dragons, birds with open wings, and tortoises wrapped in snakes, as well as tigers. Tile makers pressed the designs into clay and baked it hard before the tiles went onto a roof.

The Four Symbols appeared on mirrors and bricks, too. People saw them as guardians and hoped they would bring protection. On this roof, the great tiger of the western sky has become a row of little guardians watching over the building.
[/en]
[/section]
