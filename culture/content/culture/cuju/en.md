# Cuju: Football from Two Thousand Years Ago

## A Very, Very Old Kicking Game

Today, people all over the world play football.

But if you turn time back more than 2,000 years, you will find that people in ancient China were already playing a game that looked a lot like football.

It was called *cuju*.

*Cu* means to kick with the foot.

*Ju* means a leather ball.

Put together, the name means something like "kick ball."

Cuju was already popular in the [[Warring States period]]. At first, soldiers also used it for training. Kicking the ball helped them build strength, quick feet, and teamwork.

By the [[Han dynasty]], cuju had clearer rules.

Two teams each had 12 players. At each end of the field stood a goal.

But the goal was not a big rectangle on the ground like a modern football goal.

It was a net hanging between two tall bamboo poles, with a small hole in the net. The hole was called the "wind-flowing eye."

The ball had to fly through that little hole to score.

That was much harder than kicking into a modern goal.

You had to send the ball into a small opening in the air.

---

## The Song Dynasty: Cuju's Golden Age

The [[Song dynasty]] was the high point of cuju.

From emperors to ordinary people, many enjoyed kicking the ball.

In *Water Margin*, there is a man named Gao Qiu who gets a high government job because he is good at cuju. He was a real person in history. Song Emperor Huizong noticed him because of his ball skills, and Gao Qiu rose all the way to a top army job.

Getting a high government job because you were good at ball games sounds strange today.

It would be a little like Messi becoming a general.

The [[Song dynasty]] also had groups that looked a bit like sports clubs. One was called Qiyun Society, also known as Round Society.

To join, you had to be tested. If you passed, a teacher would take you in. There were regular practices and matches.

Song cuju had many tricks.

Players could control the ball with shoulders, backs, knees, and feet. If the ball touched the ground, you lost.

These moves feel a little like street football today. When you see someone juggle a ball with the shoulder, remember:

Song dynasty players were already very good at that kind of thing.

---

## How Did Cuju Disappear?

After the [[Ming dynasty]] began, cuju slowly became less popular.

Zhu Yuanzhang, the first Ming emperor, banned soldiers from playing it. He worried that soldiers would love the game too much and forget their army training.

By the [[Qing dynasty]], cuju had mostly disappeared from everyday life.

A game people had played for 2,000 years quietly left the field.

But the fun of it did not disappear.

Modern football rules were organized in Britain in the 1800s. But the joy of kicking a ball with your feet, playing in two teams, and trying to send the ball to the other side is much older.

Ancient Chinese people were already playing with that joy long ago.

---

## Summary

Cuju was an ancient Chinese kicking game that looked a lot like football.

By the Han dynasty, it already had fairly complete rules, and the goal was a small hole hanging in the air.

In the Song dynasty, cuju became even livelier, with groups that looked a little like clubs.

After the Ming dynasty, cuju slowly disappeared. But people still love chasing a ball, competing, and cheering.

That part has never changed.
