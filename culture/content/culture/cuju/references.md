# Editorial References: Cùjū: A Ball Game from Two Thousand Years Ago

This file is for editorial research and does not render on the website.

## 1. FIFA Museum — “Origins: Cuju in China”

URL: https://editorial.fifamuseum.com/en/read/origins-cuju-in-china/
Accessed: 2026-09-05

Supports:

- *Cùjū* has a documented history extending more than two thousand years, and its name is commonly translated as “kick-ball.”
- The game existed in several competitive and cooperative forms, including a version with a raised goal between two poles and a central opening.
- Players practiced keeping the ball off the ground with multiple parts of the body.
- Early leather balls were stuffed with feathers; later balls used an inflated animal bladder inside a sewn leather covering.

Editorial boundary:

- The article describes selected well-documented forms of *cùjū* and does not present one ruleset as universal across its long history.
- The imagined modern goalkeeper is a child-facing joke, not a historical quotation.

## 2. National Museum of China — “Bronze Mirror with Figures Playing Cu-ju”

URL: https://en.chnmuseum.cn/collections_577/collection_highlights_608/artifacts_handed___down_from_ancient_times_612/202008/t20200827_247516.html
Accessed: 2026-09-05

Supports:

- The museum’s Song-dynasty mirror depicts people playing and watching *cùjū*.
- The object description discusses the development of a rounder leather ball around an inflated animal bladder, skill-based play, public performances, and organized *cùjū* clubs.

Editorial boundary:

- The bronze mirror provides evidence for a Song-period playing scene; it is not treated as a complete record of every rule or technique.

## 3. FIFA Museum — “Origins: Pre-Histories of Football”

URL: https://fifamuseum.com/fr/explore/fifamuseumplus/digital-exhibitions/origins-pre-histories-of-football
Accessed: 2026-09-05

Supports:

- The exhibition places *cùjū* among several pre-histories of football and emphasizes its many forms across different eras.
- It provides the comparative boundary needed to discuss similarities without claiming that modern association football directly developed from *cùjū*.

Editorial boundary:

- The article explicitly says that modern soccer did not simply grow out of *cùjū*.
- Familiar feelings and visual parallels are interpretive connections, not claims of direct institutional descent.
