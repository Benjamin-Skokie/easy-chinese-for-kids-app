heading_en: Long Ago, People in China Were Already Kicking a Ball

caption_zh: 蹴鞠最特别的地方，是球门不在地上，而是在半空中。
caption_en: One of cuju's most surprising features was a goal hanging high in the air.
[zh]
今天，全世界很多孩子都爱踢足球。可如果把时间往前拨两千多年，你会发现古代中国人也很爱踢球。这个游戏叫蹴鞠。

“蹴”就是用脚踢，“鞠”就是球。名字连起来，意思差不多就是“踢球”。它在[[战国时期|Warring States period]]就已经流行，后来到了[[汉朝|Han dynasty]]，规则也越来越完整。

最让人吃惊的是它的球门。现代足球的球门在地上，可蹴鞠有一种踢法，是在两根高竹竿之间挂一张网，网上留一个小洞。球要从那个小洞穿过去，才算得分。
[/zh]
[en]
Today, children all over the world love football. But if you turn time back more than two thousand years, you will find that people in ancient China loved kicking a ball too. Their game was called cuju.

The name is simple. *Cu* means kick. *Ju* means ball. Put together, it means something like "kick ball." The game was already popular in the [[Warring States period]], and by the [[Han dynasty]], its rules had become much clearer.

The most surprising part was the goal. In modern football, the goal sits on the ground. In one famous kind of cuju, a net hung between two tall bamboo poles, with a small hole in the middle. To score, the ball had to fly through that little hole.
[/en]
[/section]

caption_zh: 到了宋朝，蹴鞠不只是比赛，还是很多人爱看的表演和本事。
caption_en: By the Song dynasty, cuju was not only a match. It was also a skill people loved to watch.
[zh]
到了[[宋朝|Song dynasty]]，蹴鞠更热闹了。皇帝喜欢看，老百姓也喜欢看。有人认真比赛，也有人专门练控球的本事。

肩膀、膝盖、脚背，都可以把球稳稳接住，不让它掉下来。你今天在街头看到有人把足球一下一下颠起来，古代踢蹴鞠的人，也会练这种本事。

那时候还有点像球社一样的组织，大家一起练，一起比。蹴鞠已经不只是训练身体，也成了一种让人围着看、看了会叫好的运动。
[/zh]
[en]
By the [[Song dynasty]], cuju had become even more lively. Emperors enjoyed it, and ordinary people enjoyed it too. Some players took part in serious matches, and some became famous for control and skill.

They could keep the ball up with shoulders, knees, and feet without letting it fall. When you see someone juggling a football today, remember that cuju players long ago were practicing that kind of control too.

There were even groups a little like sports clubs. People trained together and played together. Cuju was no longer only about exercise. It had become a sport that other people gathered around to watch and cheer for.
[/en]
[/section]

[section]
page: 3
layout: text-only
heading_zh: 后来它慢慢退场了，可踢球的快乐没有退场
heading_en: The Game Faded, but the Joy of Kicking a Ball Did Not
[zh]
后来，蹴鞠慢慢没有以前那么常见了。到了[[明朝|Ming dynasty]]，有人担心士兵太贪玩，会把练兵放在一边。再后来，到了[[清朝|Qing dynasty]]，蹴鞠已经很少出现在日常生活里了。

可它带来的快乐没有真的消失。分成两队，追着一个球跑，想办法把球送到对面去，这种高兴的感觉，今天的小朋友还是一下子就能懂。
[/zh]
[en]
Later, cuju slowly became less common. In the [[Ming dynasty]], some rulers worried that soldiers might spend too much time playing and not enough time training. By the [[Qing dynasty]], cuju had mostly slipped out of everyday life.

But the joy inside it did not truly disappear. Two sides, one ball, everyone running and trying to send it to the other end: children today can still understand that excitement right away.
[/en]
[/section]

[ending]
heading_zh: 小结
heading_en: In Summary
[zh]
蹴鞠是中国很早很早以前的踢球游戏。它有会挂在半空中的球门，也有讲究控球的本事。到了宋朝，它已经热闹得像一种全民运动。

虽然这个名字后来慢慢淡出了日常生活，可人类喜欢追着球跑、喜欢比赛、喜欢欢呼，这件事一直没有变。
[/zh]
[en]
Cuju was an early Chinese ball game from long ago. It had unusual goals hanging in the air, and it asked players to control the ball with real skill. By the Song dynasty, it had become lively enough to feel almost like a sport for everyone.

The name slowly faded from daily life, but the joy behind it never did. People still love chasing a ball, competing, and cheering.
[/en]
[/ending]
