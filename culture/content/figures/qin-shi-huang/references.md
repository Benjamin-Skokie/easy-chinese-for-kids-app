# Editorial References: Qin Shi Huang

This file is for editorial research and does not render on the website.

## 1. Sima Qian, *Records of the Grand Historian*, “Basic Annals of the First Emperor of Qin”

URLs:

- https://ctext.org/shiji/qin-shi-huang-ben-ji/zh
- https://ctext.org/shiji/qin-shi-huang-ben-ji/ens

Accessed: 2026-08-21

Supports:

- Ying Zheng becoming king at thirteen, Qin's conquests, the new title of emperor, efforts to make rules and measures match, construction projects, the search for long-life medicines, and his death while traveling.

Editorial boundary:

- Sima Qian wrote later, during the Han dynasty. The story therefore does not present its dramatic details as an eyewitness record, and it does not claim to know Qin Shi Huang's exact cause of death.

## 2. Sima Qian, *Records of the Grand Historian*, “Biographies of the Assassins”

URLs:

- https://ctext.org/shiji/ci-ke-lie-zhuan/zh
- https://ctext.org/shiji/ci-ke-lie-zhuan/zhs

Accessed: 2026-08-21

Supports:

- Jing Ke entering the palace with a hidden dagger, grabbing the king's sleeve, and the king struggling to draw his long sword.

Editorial boundary:

- The main event and people are historically grounded, but the moment-by-moment scene comes from a literary account written later.

## 3. The Metropolitan Museum of Art, “Qin Dynasty (221–206 B.C.)” and “Age of Empires”

URLs:

- https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/qin-dynasty-221-206-b-c
- https://www.metmuseum.org/exhibitions/listings/2017/age-of-empires/exhibition-galleries

Accessed: 2026-08-21

Supports:

- Qin's unification in 221 BCE, the imperial title, shared rules for writing, money, weights, and measures, and the short life of the Qin dynasty.

Editorial boundary:

- The story says Qin made these systems more alike; it does not say Qin invented them or changed every local practice in a single day.

## 4. Research on Qin frontier walls and forced labor

URLs:

- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4522747/
- https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-chinese-history/article/making-use-of-the-land-the-political-ecology-of-chinas-first-empire/5C80E8CC40548B2953F72246C49A6CF7
- https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10216532

Accessed: 2026-08-21

Supports:

- Earlier kingdoms building northern wall sections, Qin repairing and joining some of them, and the heavy demands for labor, grain, taxes, and military service.

Editorial boundary:

- The story does not give an exact death toll and does not describe the Qin walls as the later brick Great Wall seen by many visitors today.

## 5. UNESCO World Heritage Centre, “Mausoleum of the First Qin Emperor”

URL: https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/441/

Accessed: 2026-08-21

Supports:

- The mausoleum complex, the nearby pits containing clay soldiers, horses, and chariots, and the fact that the central burial room has not been opened.

Editorial boundary:

- The Terracotta Army is near the main burial room, not inside it. The story does not claim to know what the unopened room contains.
