Accessed: 2026-09-01

Supports:

- Li Bai's wide travels, love of books and swordsmanship, palace appointment in 742, hope of helping the country, brief court career, involvement with Prince Li Lin, imprisonment, exile, pardon, illness, and death in 762.

Editorial boundary:

- The story omits disputed details about Li Bai's birthplace and family because they do not help the child-facing narrative.
- It does not claim certainty about why he joined Prince Li Lin.

## 3. The three poems and the Mount Lu setting

URLs:

- *Quiet Night Thought*: https://ctext.org/wiki.pl?chapter=810697&if=gb
- *Viewing the Waterfall at Mount Lu*: https://ctext.org/text.pl?if=gb&node=139483
- *Leaving Baidi City in the Morning*: https://ctext.org/text.pl?if=gb&node=139621
- Hong Kong Education Bureau teaching guide: https://www.edb.gov.hk/attachment/tc/curriculum-development/kla/chi-edu/resources/primary/lang/pdp/20250110_PDF.pdf
- UNESCO World Heritage Centre, “Lushan National Park”: https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/778/

Accessed: 2026-09-01

Supports:

- The moonlight and homesickness in *Quiet Night Thought*; the Milky Way image in *Viewing the Waterfall at Mount Lu*; and the calling animals, light boat, and mountains in *Leaving Baidi City in the Morning*.
- The common reading that links *Leaving Baidi City in the Morning* with Li Bai's release and its feeling of speed and freedom.
- Mount Lu's peaks, valleys, streams, waterfalls, and long importance to artists and writers.

Editorial boundary:

- The date and exact setting of *Leaving Baidi City in the Morning* remain debated. The story follows a widely taught interpretation without presenting the date as proven.
- The English lines are original child-facing paraphrases, not copied from a modern copyrighted translation.
