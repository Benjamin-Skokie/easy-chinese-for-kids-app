## 1. Chen Shou, *Records of the Three Kingdoms*, “Annals of Emperor Wu,” with Pei Songzhi's annotations

URL: https://ctext.org/text.pl?if=gb&node=601875&remap=gb
- https://zh.wikisource.org/zh/%E4%B8%89%E5%9B%BD%E5%BF%97%EF%BC%88%E9%99%88%E5%AF%BF%E6%92%B0_%E8%A3%B4%E6%9D%BE%E4%B9%8B%E6%B3%A8_%E5%BB%BA%E9%98%B3%E5%88%8A%E6%9C%AC%EF%BC%89/%E9%AD%8F%E6%9B%B8/%E6%AD%A6%E5%B8%9D%E7%BA%AA%E7%AC%AC%E4%B8%80
- https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/b1/SSID-12983472_%E4%B8%89%E5%9C%8B%E5%BF%97_%E5%8D%B71-23.pdf

Accessed: 2026-08-21

Supports:

- Cao Cao's career, offices, campaigns, move of Emperor Xian's court to Xu, farming colonies, Guandu campaign, Duke and King of Wei titles, death in 220, and later posthumous imperial title.
- Chen Shou's main text and the diverse earlier works quoted by Pei Songzhi are visibly different textual layers.
- Pei's annotations sometimes preserve conflicting stories and sometimes criticize figures in the main narrative.

Editorial boundary:

- *Records of the Three Kingdoms* was compiled decades after Cao Cao's death and is not treated as a stenographic transcript.
- Material quoted by Pei is labeled as an annotation or earlier report rather than silently merged into Chen Shou's main text.
- The annal's title “Emperor Wu” is posthumous and is not evidence that Cao Cao reigned as emperor while alive.

## 2. *Book of the Later Han* and *Records of the Three Kingdoms* — Emperor Xian and political authority

URL: https://ctext.org/hou-han-shu/ens
- https://ctext.org/text.pl?if=gb&node=601875&remap=gb
- https://gateway-ipfs.st/ipfs/bafykbzacecojov5y4uw5wahvv625hh6j75nxuepd5jkioxh2d4ivcbidtnhi4?filename=%E4%B8%AD%E5%9B%BD%E9%80%9A%E5%8F%B2+%E7%AC%AC%E4%BA%94%E5%8D%B7+%E4%B8%89%E5%9B%BD%E4%B8%A4%E6%99%8B%E5%8D%97%E5%8C%97%E6%9C%9D%E6%97%B6%E6%9C%9F+%EF%BC%88%E4%B8%8A%EF%BC%89_%E7%99%BD%E5%AF%BF%E5%BD%9D.pdf

Accessed: 2026-08-21

Supports:

- Emperor Xian remained the formal Han sovereign while several military strongmen controlled the court.
- Cao Cao brought the court to Xu in 196, restored institutions and supply, and used Han offices and imperial commands while concentrating power.
- “Supporting/honoring the emperor” and “holding the emperor to command the lords” express rival political interpretations of the same unequal relationship.
- Cao Cao became Duke of Wei in 213 and King of Wei in 216; Cao Pi founded Wei after Cao Cao's death and then honored him posthumously as emperor.

Editorial boundary:

- Emperor Xian is not described as having no agency at all, but neither is the court presented as independent of Cao Cao.
- “Held hostage” is explained as political shorthand, not a claim that the emperor was literally tied up each day.
- Cao Cao is never called a reigning emperor during his lifetime.

## 3. Guandu: *Records of the Three Kingdoms*, biographies of Cao Cao and Yuan Shao, with Pei Songzhi's numerical critique

URL: https://ctext.org/text.pl?if=gb&node=601875&remap=gb
- https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/b1/SSID-12983472_%E4%B8%89%E5%9B%BD%E5%BF%97_%E5%8D%B71-23.pdf
- https://www.zzrvtc.edu.cn/_upload/article/files/65/a4/f7e77904478ea6afd1e4fa3e13db/379fe01f-3d80-4385-8a5f-cc862f429147.pdf

Accessed: 2026-08-21

Supports:

- The sources describe Yuan Shao selecting 100,000 infantry and 10,000 horses and elsewhere give “more than 100,000.”
- Cao Cao's annal reports “fewer than 10,000” at one point at Guandu and significant casualties.
- Pei Songzhi explicitly doubts that Cao Cao's whole relevant force could have been so small, based on earlier recruitment, annexations, dispersed operations, and the ability to leave camp.
- Cao Cao suffered a supply crisis, Xu You defected, and Cao Cao personally led the raid that destroyed Yuan Shao's grain at Wuchao.

Editorial boundary:

- The article states that Yuan Shao had a clear numerical advantage but does not convert the literary figures into an exact 100,000-versus-10,000 scoreboard.
- No speculative modern estimate is substituted as if it were a surviving army register.
- Wuchao is treated as a major turning point involving intelligence, logistics, and command decisions, not a single flash of genius.

## 4. Farming colonies and recruitment edicts

URL: https://ctext.org/text.pl?if=gb&node=601875&remap=gb
- https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hans/%E6%B1%82%E8%B3%A2%E4%BB%A4
- https://mzj.xuchang.gov.cn/decqgdmpc/20230511/51edb2c4-ea74-4bd6-b741-fdf10b41bf2f.html
- https://journal.scu.edu.cn/info/1109/12295.htm
- https://www.cirs.tsinghua.edu.cn/sannjcyc/424.jhtml

Accessed: 2026-08-21

Supports:

- Cao Cao adopted proposals associated with Zao Zhi and Han Hao and began farming colonies near Xu in 196.
- The administration recruited displaced people, organized land and equipment, created agricultural offices, and took a share of harvests; military colonies directly supported armies.
- The system restored cultivation and stabilized supply while also increasing state organization and control of land and labor.
- Cao Cao's 210 edict uses the phrase “唯才是举” and asks officials to seek capable people beyond conventional reputation and elite visibility.

Editorial boundary:

- Farming colonies are not described as free land grants or purely humanitarian relief.
- Exact assessments and management varied, and scholarly debates about administration are not concealed.
- “Promote talent” is not translated into modern universal equality or a claim that birth, gender, status, and patronage no longer mattered.

## 5. Violence in Xu Province: *Book of the Later Han* and *Records of the Three Kingdoms*

URL: https://ctext.org/wiki.pl?chapter=274601&if=en
- https://ctext.org/text.pl?if=gb&node=601875&remap=gb
- https://www.zzrvtc.edu.cn/_upload/article/files/65/a4/f7e77904478ea6afd1e4fa3e13db/379fe01f-3d80-4385-8a5f-cc862f429147.pdf

Accessed: 2026-08-21

Supports:

- Early histories report the capture and slaughter of cities during Cao Cao's campaigns against Tao Qian in Xu Province.
- They describe mass civilian death and devastated communities, although their enormous totals and literary river-blocking image cannot be independently counted.
- Cao Cao's political and military career also involved executions and coercion against perceived opponents.

Editorial boundary:

- Doubt about an exact casualty number is not used to erase well-attested mass violence.
- The article does not invent individual victims, speeches, or Cao Cao's private emotions.
- Administrative and literary achievements are discussed without offsetting or excusing atrocities.

## 6. National Museum of China and Cao Cao's surviving writings — Jian'an literature

URL: https://en.chnmuseum.cn/Portals/0/web/exhibition/exhibitions/161120Chines-Epic/
- https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/Author:%E6%9B%B9%E6%93%8D
- https://clajc.ouc.edu.cn/ctwhyjzx/2019/1129/c16711a277391/page.psp

Accessed: 2026-08-21

Supports:

- Cao Cao is a major figure in Jian'an literature; more than twenty poems are preserved under his name.
- *Ballad of Haoli* addresses wartime devastation, while *Short Song* and *Viewing the Sea* became especially famous.
- Cao Cao, Cao Pi, Cao Zhi, the “Seven Masters,” and other writers formed a broader literary world rather than one man creating an age alone.

Editorial boundary:

- A poem is not used as transparent access to every private thought or as proof that a pictured event occurred.
- The popular “composing with a lance held crosswise before Red Cliffs” scene is marked as later literary imagination, not a documented moment of composition.
- Literary merit does not certify moral or political merit.

## 7. Lü Boshe accounts and the famous saying

URL: https://zh.wikisource.org/zh/%E4%B8%89%E5%9B%BD%E5%BF%97%EF%BC%88%E9%99%88%E5%AF%BF%E6%92%B0_%E8%A3%B4%E6%9D%BE%E4%B9%8B%E6%B3%A8_%E5%BB%BA%E9%98%B3%E5%88%8A%E6%9C%AC%EF%BC%89/%E9%AD%8F%E6%9B%B8/%E6%AD%A6%E5%B8%9D%E7%BA%AA%E7%AC%AC%E4%B8%80
- https://ctext.org/sanguo-yanyi/ch4/ens
- https://chiculture.org.hk/sc/china-five-thousand-years/4277

Accessed: 2026-08-21

Supports:

- Chen Shou's main text does not narrate the Lü Boshe killings.
- Pei Songzhi quotes three incompatible accounts: resistance to robbery, killing from suspicion, and Sun Sheng's version involving food-vessel sounds and “宁我负人，毋人负我.”
- The novel adds Lü Boshe's personal death, Chen Gong's witnessing role, a pig prepared for hospitality, and the expanded “宁教我负天下人，休教天下人负我.”

Editorial boundary:

- None of the quotations is treated as an audio recording or certain verbatim speech.
- The difference between Chen Shou's silence, Pei's conflicting sources, and the later novel is explicit.
- Literary reshaping is analyzed without calling fiction fraudulent history.

## 8. Historical kernels and novel episodes: Huarong, the dagger, and the cut beard

URL: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/b1/SSID-12983472_%E4%B8%89%E5%9B%BD%E5%BF%97_%E5%8D%B71-23.pdf
- https://ctext.org/sanguo-yanyi/ch4/ens
- https://ctext.org/sanguo-yanyi/ch50/zh
- https://ctext.org/sanguo-yanyi/ch58/ens

Accessed: 2026-08-21

Supports:

- An account quoted in Pei's annotation says Cao Cao's army retreated through a muddy Huarong route and that Liu Bei's fire arrived too late.
- The novel's chapter 50 makes Guan Yu block and release Cao Cao at Huarong.
- The novel's chapter 58 presents the “cutting off the beard and discarding the robe” escape from Ma Chao.
- The novel's chapter 4 combines the assassination-dagger episode, Lü Boshe story, and enlarged saying.

Editorial boundary:

- A historical place or conflict is not taken as proof of every later character, dialogue, trap, or escape detail.
- The article labels the full Guan Yu, beard, robe, and gift-dagger scenes as literary constructions.

## 9. National Centre for the Performing Arts — Peking opera face paint

URL: https://www.chncpa.org/cgyl_278/yszl/wqzl/201704/t20170408_168389.shtml
- https://m.chncpa.org/zxdt/rdjjx/202008/t20200814_218509.shtml

Accessed: 2026-08-21

Supports:

- Elaborate face paint belongs especially to the jing role tradition and uses color, line, and pattern to shape stage identity.
- White can contribute to the portrayal of suspicious or calculating figures in particular dramatic traditions.
- Face designs are performance conventions developed long after the historical person.

Editorial boundary:

- Cao Cao's stage-white face is not a historical portrait, biological feature, or moral diagnosis.
- The article does not turn all white face paint into one universal meaning.

## 10. Henan Provincial Institute of Cultural Heritage and Archaeology — Cao Cao's Gaoling mausoleum

URL: https://www.hnswwkgyjy.cn/NewsView.php?News_ID=113
- https://www.hnswwkgyjy.cn/NewsView.php?News_ID=1993
- https://www.hnswwkgyjy.cn/NewsView.php?News_ID=3
- https://www.chinamuseum.org.cn/detail.html?contentId=4258&id=13

Accessed: 2026-08-21

Supports:

- Tomb M2 at Xigaoxue was excavated after looting in 2008–2009 and identified by archaeological institutions as Cao Cao's Gaoling.
- The identification combines tomb date, location, layout, inscribed stone tags, artifacts, historical burial information, and partial remains of a man around sixty.
- Later investigation revealed more of the cemetery and its architectural history.

Editorial boundary:

- The identification is not reduced to one “魏武王” tag or one skull.
- Looting, disturbance, incomplete remains, and the interpretive nature of archaeological attribution are acknowledged.
- A facial reconstruction is not called a portrait; hairstyle, skin, beard, soft tissue, and expression require modern choices.
- DNA and genealogy claims are not treated as settled without secure samples, documented kinship, contamination control, and reproducible analysis.
