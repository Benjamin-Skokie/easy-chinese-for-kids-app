## 1. *Records of the Three Kingdoms* and *Book of the Later Han*

URLs:

- https://ctext.org/text.pl?if=gb&node=601875&remap=gb
- https://ctext.org/hou-han-shu/ens
- https://zh.wikisource.org/zh/%E4%B8%89%E5%9B%BD%E5%BF%97%EF%BC%88%E9%99%88%E5%AF%BF%E6%92%B0_%E8%A3%B4%E6%9D%BE%E4%B9%8B%E6%B3%A8_%E5%BB%BA%E9%98%B3%E5%88%8A%E6%9C%AC%EF%BC%89/%E9%AD%8F%E6%9B%B8/%E6%AD%A6%E5%B8%9D%E7%BA%AA%E7%AC%AC%E4%B8%80
- https://www.metmuseum.org/TOAH/hd/hand/hd_hand.htm

Accessed: 2026-08-21

Supports:

- The weakening of Han authority, Cao Cao's early city office, the punishment of a powerful palace official's relative, his military rise, and his control of Emperor Xian's court.
- Cao Cao becoming Duke and then King of Wei while Emperor Xian remained the formal emperor, followed by Cao Pi ending Han and becoming emperor of Wei after Cao Cao's death.

Editorial boundary:

- Cao Cao did not rule with the title of emperor while alive. The title “Emperor Wu” was given after his death.
- “The Han emperor lost control” does not mean the dynasty had already ended. Emperor Xian remained on the throne until Cao Pi replaced Han with Wei in 220.
- These histories were compiled later and are not treated as word-for-word records of private conversations.

## 2. The Battle of Guandu

URL: https://ctext.org/text.pl?if=gb&node=601875&remap=gb

Accessed: 2026-08-21

Supports:

- Yuan Shao's larger force, Cao Cao's supply trouble, the adviser who crossed sides, the weakly guarded grain at Wuchao, Cao Cao's night raid, and the battle turning after the grain was destroyed.

Editorial boundary:

- The story says Yuan Shao had a much larger army but does not present disputed ancient troop numbers as an exact scoreboard.
- Guandu is explained through food, information, timing, and soldiers' actions rather than as one man's magical trick.

## 3. Farming colonies, recruitment, and violence in Xu Province

URLs:

- https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hans/%E6%B1%82%E8%B3%A2%E4%BB%A4
- https://mzj.xuchang.gov.cn/decqgdmpc/20230511/51edb2c4-ea74-4bd6-b741-fdf10b41bf2f.html
- https://journal.scu.edu.cn/info/1109/12295.htm
- https://ctext.org/wiki.pl?chapter=274601&if=en

Accessed: 2026-08-21

Supports:

- Cao Cao organizing abandoned farmland to supply civilians and armies and seeking capable officials beyond the most powerful families.
- Early historical reports of Cao Cao's forces capturing and slaughtering towns in Xu Province, including many civilians, and his use of executions against suspected political enemies.

Editorial boundary:

- Farming colonies are not presented as free gifts without government control or demands.
- Uncertain casualty totals are omitted, but uncertainty about a number is not used to erase well-attested mass violence.

## 4. Cao Cao's surviving poetry

URLs:

- https://en.chnmuseum.cn/Portals/0/web/exhibition/exhibitions/161120Chines-Epic/
- https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/Author:%E6%9B%B9%E6%93%8D

Accessed: 2026-08-21

Supports:

- More than twenty poems surviving under Cao Cao's name, including poetry about the sea, the old horse that still longs to run, and the damage caused by war.
- Cao Cao's importance within the wider group of Jian'an writers.

Editorial boundary:

- Poems are used as literary works, not as perfect records of every private thought.
- Literary achievement does not excuse military or political violence.

## 5. Peking opera face paint

URL: https://www.chncpa.org/cgyl_278/yszl/wqzl/201704/t20170408_168389.shtml

Accessed: 2026-08-21

Supports:

- White face paint as a later stage convention used to present Cao Cao as suspicious, clever, and dangerous.

Editorial boundary:

- The white face belongs to performance. It is not a portrait of Cao Cao or proof that every historical action matched the later villain role.
