# Editorial References: Shang Yang

This file is for editorial research and does not render on the website.

## 1. Sima Qian, *Records of the Grand Historian*, “Biography of Lord Shang”

URL: https://ctext.org/shiji/shang-jun-lie-zhuan/zhs

Accessed: 2026-08-21

Supports:

- Shang Yang coming to Qin, the ruler's search for ways to strengthen the kingdom, and the log placed at the city gate to prove that promised rewards would be paid.
- Household registration, groups of neighboring families with shared responsibility, rewards for farming and military success, and punishment connected with the crown prince.
- The death of Shang Yang's protector, the accusation of rebellion, the inn refusing him without official papers, the neighboring kingdom refusing him, his armed resistance, and his death at the hands of Qin forces.

Editorial boundary:

- Sima Qian wrote more than two centuries after Shang Yang. The story keeps the main sequence but does not present the innkeeper's exact words as a recorded conversation.
- The story does not repeat the familiar but oversimplified claim that a living Shang Yang was necessarily torn apart by five horses.

## 2. Research on Shang Yang's reforms and Qin's growing power

URLs:

- https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/34296/chapter-abstract/290749611
- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4522747/

Accessed: 2026-08-21

Supports:

- Recording households and farmland, gathering taxes and soldiers, encouraging grain production, rewarding military success, and placing neighboring households under shared responsibility.
- The reforms helping Qin gather resources, build a stronger army, weaken the old nobility, and give some people new paths to land or rank.
- The reforms also increasing government control and pressure on ordinary households.

Editorial boundary:

- Qin's later conquest of the other kingdoms is not credited to Shang Yang alone.
- Rewards based on farming and warfare are not described as a fair modern system with equal opportunity for everyone.
