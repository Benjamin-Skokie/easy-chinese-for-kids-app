# 鲁班和长着小齿的草叶 — References

This file is for editorial research and does not render on the website.

## The complete saw legend

Source: Jining Municipal Bureau of Culture and Tourism, “济宁非遗记录之鲁班传说”
URL: https://whlyj.jining.gov.cn/art/2021/1/20/art_69979_2705967.html
Accessed: 2026-09-01

Supports:

- In this version of the legend, Lu Ban is asked to build a palace and needs more timber than workers can prepare quickly with axes.
- He goes up the mountain, slips, cuts his hands on toothed grass, studies the leaf, and copies its shape in iron.
- The new toothed tool cuts wood faster and solves the story's central problem.

Editorial boundary:

- The children's story openly introduces this account as a legend.
- Short sensory details and the exact sequence of workshop trials are narrative retellings, not documented words or a technical reconstruction.

## Why there are so many Lu Ban stories

Source: China Intangible Cultural Heritage Network and Digital Museum, “鲁班传说”
URL: https://www.ihchina.cn/art/detail/id/12270.html
Accessed: 2026-09-01

Supports:

- Lu Ban legends are recognized as a form of national intangible cultural heritage and include stories about tools, buildings, household objects, and magical feats.
- Over many generations, Lu Ban became a shared symbol of skilled builders and craft knowledge.

Editorial boundary:

- The page tells one complete saw legend instead of combining unrelated stories about siege ladders, wooden birds, bridges, and many other inventions.
- The existence of many legends does not make every invention claim a documented historical fact.

## Toothed tools before Lu Ban

Source: Richard Fullagar et al., “A functional study of denticulate sickles and knives, ground stone tools from the early Neolithic Peiligang culture, China,” *Archaeological Research in Asia*
URL: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S235222672100012X
Accessed: 2026-08-21

Supports:

- Archaeological sites in China contain toothed cutting tools from thousands of years before the period associated with Lu Ban.

Editorial boundary:

- The story is presented as the traditional explanation of Lu Ban's saw, not proof that he created the first toothed cutting tool in history.

## Image boundaries

All three images are newly generated story illustrations, not portraits, archaeological evidence, or reliable reconstructions.

- Lu Ban is shown as the same adult carpenter in every scene so the visual story does not change his age.
- The workshop scene contains only a few tools needed for the experiment; it does not claim that a complete later carpenter's toolbox already existed.
- The final scene shows Lu Ban teaching through hands-on work. It illustrates his later place as a great teacher of carpenters without turning the workshop into a temple or presenting a documented event.
