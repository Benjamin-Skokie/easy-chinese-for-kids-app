# Lu Ban: The Carpenter China Remembered for 2,000 Years

## An Idea from Cutting Firewood

About 2,500 years ago, a young man named Gongshu Ban went up a mountain to cut firewood.

In legend, he slipped. His hand grabbed some wild grass beside the path.

Ouch.

The grass cut his palm.

Gongshu Ban looked closely at the leaf. Along the edge were many tiny teeth.

He stared at it for a long time.

Then he went back to his workshop and made a tool with teeth along its edge.

People often tell this story to explain where the idea for the saw came from.

Was the saw truly invented by Lu Ban alone, all at once, in exactly this way? We cannot be that sure.

But Chinese people have long remembered Lu Ban as a master builder and woodworker. That part is clear.

---

## A Person Who Could Make Almost Anything

Lu Ban was more than a carpenter.

He was a problem solver.

Later people connected many woodworking tools and clever building ideas with his name.

In stories, saws, planes, and smart ways to build things all seem to come from him thinking while he worked.

In real history, tools usually improve slowly over a long time. We may not be able to point to one afternoon and one person's hand.

But people wanted to place these ideas under Lu Ban's name.

That shows what he became: a name for woodworking wisdom.

He is also often tied to the ink line tool.

A thin string is dipped in ink, pulled tight, and snapped.

Pop!

A straight black line appears on the wood.

Even today, woodworkers and builders still use similar ideas to mark straight lines.

The materials may change. The details may change.

But the idea still works.

---

## Cloud Ladders: Tools Made for War

Lu Ban was remembered for more than woodworking tools.

Old books and later legends also connect him with machines for attacking cities.

One famous name is the "cloud ladder."

Just hearing the name gives you a picture: a ladder rising so high it seems to reach the clouds.

Later siege machines changed from age to age. It would be too simple to say people used the same design for more than 2,000 years.

But Lu Ban's name slowly became more than one person.

It became a symbol of someone who could make things and think of ways to solve hard problems.

---

## Honored as the Old Master

After Lu Ban died, generations of carpenters honored him as their old master.

Even today, in some places in China, builders and woodworkers still have customs tied to Lu Ban.

He may not be a historical person we can rebuild clearly in every detail.

He is more like the ancestor a whole craft chose for itself.

Lu Ban's stories really hold one simple idea.

If grass cuts most people's hands, they may just complain and walk away.

Lu Ban stops.

He looks again.

He thinks one step farther.

Chinese carpenters honored him for many years not just because of one tool, but because of this skill:

when he saw a problem, he wanted to make an answer.
