# Tang Poems: One Sentence Can Hold a World

## Poems That Save Words

Tang poems are very good at using few words.

Twenty Chinese characters, in a four-line poem with five characters in each line, can paint a picture, tell a story, and leave a deep feeling.

Liu Zongyuan wrote a famous snow poem:

No birds fly over a thousand mountains.

No footprints cross ten thousand paths.

In a lone boat, an old man in a rain cape

fishes alone in the cold river snow.

You may read the poem in only a few seconds.

But the picture of the old man sitting by the snowy river can stay in your mind for a long time.

---

## Why Could So Many People in the Tang Dynasty Write Poems?

In the Tang dynasty, the big government exams cared a lot about poetry.

Many people who wanted to become government workers had to practice writing poems.

Generals wrote poems.

Monks wrote poems.

Emperors wrote poems.

Palace women wrote poems too.

If you went to a Tang dynasty drinking party, the host might raise a cup and say, "Everyone write a poem about this moment."

That would not be too strange.

The huge collection *Complete Tang Poems* includes nearly 50,000 poems by more than 2,000 poets.

And that is probably not all of them.

Many poems were once written on tavern walls, temple pillars, and windows at travel stations.

Later, wind made them old, rain blurred them, and other people painted over them.

## Li Bai and Du Fu: The Twin Stars of the Tang

Li Bai and Du Fu lived in the same age.

But their poetic eyes often looked in different directions.

Li Bai liked to look upward: the moon, waterfalls, the Milky Way.

Du Fu often looked deeply into human life: suffering people by the road, villages after war, and a hut torn by wind.

Li Bai wrote with huge confidence, saying Heaven gave him talent for a reason and money spent would come back.

Du Fu wrote with a wide heart, wishing for thousands of great houses to shelter poor people who studied from the cold.

The two men met and admired each other.

Later people loved placing them side by side, like the two brightest stars in Tang poetry.

## Borderland Poems: Beauty on the Battlefield

One of the most powerful kinds of Tang poetry is borderland poetry.

These poems write about war, deserts, snow mountains, and faraway borderlands.

Wang Changling wrote about the moon of the Qin age and the border pass of the Han age, while soldiers on long journeys still had not returned.

The moon is old.

The border is old.

But the people sent far away still do not come home.

Cen Shen wrote about snow beyond the borderlands.

He said it was like a spring wind arriving overnight, making thousands of pear trees bloom.

But this was not Jiangnan spring.

It was snow on dry branches.

A wild wind came, and countless branches were covered in white.

Not pear flowers.

Ice flowers.

Using the most beautiful spring words to write the coldest scene is one clever trick of borderland poetry.

---

## Summary

Tang poems can use twenty Chinese characters to paint a whole picture.

An old man in a rain cape fishes alone in cold river snow.

In the Tang dynasty, many people wrote poems because exams asked for them and social life enjoyed them.

Li Bai looked upward to moons and waterfalls.

Du Fu looked downward to people suffering on earth.

Borderland poets used pear blossoms to write about snow.

That is Tang poetry:

the fewest words making the largest world.
