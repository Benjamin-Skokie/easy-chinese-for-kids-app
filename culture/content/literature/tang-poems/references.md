# Tang Poems: Research Notes and Sources

This file is for editorial research and does not render on the website.

Accessed: 2026-09-01

Supports:

- The Chinese poem texts, the child-facing explanations of their central images, and the short account of poetry in Tang examinations and social life.

Editorial boundary:

- The English poem lines in the article are original, child-facing translations made for this project. They are not copied from published English translations.
- The article does not claim that everyone in Tang China wrote poetry or took the examinations.

## Poem texts and teaching notes

Source: Wikisource, Liu Zongyuan, “River Snow” (《江雪》)
URL: https://wikisource.org/wiki/%E6%B1%9F%E9%9B%AA

- Confirms the four-line Chinese text used in the article.

Source: Radio Television Hong Kong, “Classical Poem Appreciation: River Snow” (古诗诵赏：《江雪》)
URL: https://www.rthk.hk/chiculture/chipoem/poem22.htm

- Notes how the poem moves from the distant mountains and paths to the nearby boat and fisherman.

Source: Hong Kong Education Bureau, Wang Wei, “Farewell to Yuan Er on His Mission to Anxi” (《送元二使安西》)
URL: https://www.edb.gov.hk/attachment/tc/curriculum-development/kla/chi-edu/recommended-passages/KS2_08.pdf

- Confirms the poem text and explains that the poem was set to music and became a popular Tang song.

Source: Wikisource, Li Bai, “Drinking Alone under the Moon” (《月下独酌》)
URL: https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E6%9C%88%E4%B8%8B%E7%8D%A8%E9%85%8C

- Confirms the lines “举杯邀明月，对影成三人.”

Source: Hong Kong Education Bureau, teaching resource for Du Fu's “My Thatched Roof Is Ruined by the Autumn Wind” (《茅屋为秋风所破歌》)
URL: https://www.edb.gov.hk/attachment/tc/curriculum-development/kla/chi-edu/resources/secondary-edu/lang/chi_chapter/P027.pdf

- Confirms the damaged-roof setting and Du Fu's wish for shelter for people who were cold and poor.

Source: Hong Kong Education Bureau, Cen Shen, “A Song of White Snow in Farewell to Secretary Wu Going Home” (《白雪歌送武判官归京》)
URL: https://www.edb.gov.hk/attachment/tc/curriculum-development/kla/chi-edu/resources/secondary-edu/lang/chi_chapter/P030.pdf

- Confirms the poem text, Cen Shen's time on the Tang frontier, the snow-covered farewell setting, and the spring-wind and pear-blossom comparison.
- The child-facing English translation renders “胡天八月即飞雪” as “It is only fall, but snow is already coming down.” This carries the surprise of early snow without asking young readers to interpret the traditional Chinese calendar.

## Why poetry was so common in Tang educated life

Source: Asia for Educators, Columbia University, “Introduction to Tang Poetry: Poetry and the Civil Service Examination”
URL: https://video.afe.easia.columbia.edu/playlist/intro-to-tang-poetry/intro/

- Explains that poetry composition entered the civil service examination requirements in 680 and helped drive widespread technical training in poetry.

Source: Nathan Vedal, “Never Taking a Shortcut: Examination Poetry of the Tang Dynasty,” *Tang Studies* 33 (2015)
URL: https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1179/0737503415Z.00000000014

- Establishes that the *jinshi* examination required poetry composition from the late seventh century onward.

## Editorial boundaries

- The article does not claim that everyone in Tang China wrote poetry or took the examinations.
- The gathering image is a modern imagined scene, not a record of one identified historical event.
- The article presents only short selections from the longer poems by Li Bai, Du Fu, and Cen Shen.
- The translations favor clear images and natural English for ages six to ten while preserving the action and central comparison of each Chinese line. They are not intended as word-for-word translations.
