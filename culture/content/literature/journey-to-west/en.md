> Difficulty: Easy

# Journey to the West: The Adventures of the Monkey King

## The Monkey Who Jumped Out of a Stone

*Journey to the West* begins with a stone. On Flower-Fruit Mountain stood a great magic stone. It had been warmed by the sun, cooled by moonlight, and washed by wind and rain for longer than anyone could remember. Then one day, the stone cracked open. Out jumped a monkey.

The stone monkey ran through the mountain with the other monkeys, climbing trees and eating fruit. Before long, he proved that he was braver than the rest. When the monkeys found a roaring waterfall, they wondered what lay behind it. They made a promise: whoever could jump through the waterfall, find out what was on the other side, and come back safely would become their king.

The stone monkey jumped. Behind the falling water, he found a stone bridge and a large cave where the whole troop could live. He came back through the waterfall and reminded everyone of their promise. From that day on, he became the Handsome Monkey King.

---

For a while, being king was wonderful. Then one day he began to worry about something that even a king could not order away: growing old and dying. So the Monkey King left his mountain to search for a teacher who could show him the secrets of a longer life.

After years of traveling, he finally found one. The teacher gave him the name *Sūn Wùkōng* and taught him powerful magic. Wùkōng learned seventy-two transformations, which let him change his shape, and the Somersault Cloud, which could carry him an enormous distance in a single leap.

By the time he returned to Flower-Fruit Mountain, Wùkōng could turn into animals, fly through the sky, and perform tricks that no ordinary monkey could dream of. Now he needed a weapon.

---

## A Staff from the Bottom of the Sea

Wùkōng went beneath the sea to the palace of the Dragon King and asked for something heavy enough for him to use. The Dragon King brought out weapon after weapon. Wùkōng tried them, but nothing felt right.

Then he noticed a huge iron pillar that seemed to shine when he came near it. This was *Rúyì Jīngū Bàng*, the magic gold-banded staff. It was so heavy that almost no one could move it, but Wùkōng lifted it with ease. Even better, the staff could change size. When he wanted to fight, it could become thick and long. When he was finished, he could shrink it until it was as small as an embroidery needle and tuck it safely inside his ear.

Wùkōng was delighted. The Dragon King was less delighted to see his treasure leave.

---

Soon Wùkōng caused trouble somewhere else. One night, messengers from the Underworld came for him because the Book of Life and Death said his time was up. Wùkōng refused to accept that.

He stormed into the Underworld, found the book that recorded lifetimes, and crossed out his own name. Then he crossed out the names of many other monkeys too. Now both the Dragon King and the rulers of the Underworld had complaints about him. Heaven decided it was time to do something.

The Jade Emperor, ruler of Heaven, thought perhaps the easiest way to keep this powerful monkey out of trouble was to give him a job. Wùkōng was invited to Heaven and given the title *Bìmǎwēn*. It sounded important. His job was to help manage the heavenly horses.

And, surprisingly, he took the job seriously.

---

Wùkōng checked the records and counted the horses. Other workers prepared grass, brought water, cooked feed, and brushed the horses. Wùkōng watched over everything day and night. If a horse was sleeping instead of eating, he woke it up. If one wandered away from the feeding trough, he brought it back. After a little more than two weeks, the horses were fat and healthy. The other officials were so pleased that they prepared a feast for him.

During the meal, Wùkōng suddenly asked, “By the way, what rank is a *Bìmǎwēn*?” The others told him it had no official rank. Wùkōng misunderstood. “No rank? Then it must be so important that it is above all the ranks!”

Not exactly. They explained that the job was so low that it did not even count as a proper rank. Wùkōng exploded. He had been king of Flower-Fruit Mountain, and Heaven had brought him up here to be a stable keeper? He pushed over the table, pulled the staff from his ear, and stormed out of Heaven.

When he reached Flower-Fruit Mountain, the monkeys rushed to welcome him home. “You’ve been gone more than ten years!” they said. Wùkōng stared at them. “Ten years? I was only in Heaven for a little more than two weeks!” That was when he learned another strange rule of the story: one day in Heaven could equal a year below.

Wùkōng had had enough of heavenly job titles. He gave himself a title instead. *Qítiān Dàshèng*—Great Sage Equal to Heaven. This one sounded much better.

---

## How a Peach Garden Turned into Havoc in Heaven

Heaven first sent soldiers to deal with Wùkōng, but he fought them off. Finally, the Jade Emperor decided to invite him back and let him keep his grand new title. Perhaps, everyone hoped, Wùkōng would be happy if he felt important. For a while, he was.

Then Heaven gave him another job: watching over the Peach Garden. These were no ordinary peaches. Some took thousands of years to ripen, and the ripest ones were said to give extraordinary long life. Putting Sūn Wùkōng in charge of them was a risky choice.

At first, he walked around the garden and learned which trees had the best fruit. Then, whenever the biggest peaches ripened, he began quietly eating them himself. He did this more than once.

One day, seven fairy attendants arrived with baskets. The Queen Mother of Heaven was preparing a great Peach Banquet and had sent them to gather fruit. They filled baskets from the first trees and the middle trees. But when they reached the trees with the finest peaches, they found almost nothing ready to pick. Most of the ripe ones were gone. Wùkōng had eaten them.

The fairies finally found him sleeping high in a peach tree. When he woke, he asked why they had come. “The Peach Banquet,” they explained. Wùkōng immediately became interested. “Who has been invited?” The fairies began listing important gods and immortals. Wùkōng waited. Then he asked the question he really cared about. “Am I invited?” The fairies had not heard his name on the list.

Wùkōng was furious.

---

He used magic to freeze the fairies in place and hurried toward the banquet. Along the way, he tricked one invited guest into going somewhere else. Then he slipped into the banquet area before the celebration had properly begun.

There was food. There was heavenly wine. Wùkōng helped himself to both. Before long, he had drunk far too much and wandered away from the banquet. Somehow he ended up at the home of *Tàishàng Lǎojūn*, a powerful Daoist immortal famous for making magical pills.

No one was there. But the pills were. Wùkōng found containers filled with precious pills of immortality and began eating them like snacks.

Eventually even Wùkōng realized this was becoming a very large problem. He had eaten the peaches, crashed the banquet, drunk the wine, and eaten the magical pills. It seemed like a good time to leave. He fled back to Flower-Fruit Mountain.

---

Heaven sent an army. Wùkōng fought back with his staff and his seventy-two transformations. At last, *Èrláng Shén*, one of Heaven’s strongest warriors, came to fight him. Wùkōng changed shape; Èrláng changed shape too. They chased each other through one transformation after another.

While they were fighting, *Tàishàng Lǎojūn* threw down a magical metal ring. It struck Wùkōng and knocked him off balance. This time, Heaven finally captured him.

But capturing Wùkōng and punishing Wùkōng turned out to be two different things. Blades could not cut him. Fire could not burn him. Thunder did not finish the job either. Wùkōng had learned too much magic and eaten too many magical peaches and pills.

So *Tàishàng Lǎojūn* tried something else. He put Wùkōng inside the Eight Trigrams Furnace and ordered the fire to burn for forty-nine days.

Wùkōng found a place inside where the flames were weaker, but smoke kept blowing into his eyes. When the furnace was finally opened, he was still alive. His eyes had been changed by the smoke and heat. They became the famous “fiery golden eyes,” which later helped him see through many magical disguises.

Wùkōng jumped out of the furnace, kicked it over, pulled out his staff, and began making trouble in Heaven all over again.

---

This time, the Buddha came to stop him. Wùkōng boasted that he was so powerful he could rule Heaven himself. The Buddha held out one hand. “If you can jump out of my palm,” he said, “then you can prove how great you are.”

Wùkōng almost laughed. One somersault could carry him farther than most people could imagine. He leaped into the air and flew until he saw five enormous pillars far in the distance. Surely, he thought, this must be the end of the world.

To prove he had been there, he wrote, “Great Sage Equal to Heaven was here,” on one of the pillars. Then he flew back. “I made it out of your hand!” he announced.

The Buddha told him to look more carefully. Wùkōng looked. The words he had written were right there on one of the Buddha’s fingers. The five enormous pillars had been five fingers. Wùkōng had never left the Buddha’s palm.

He tried to escape again, but the Buddha turned his hand over. His five fingers became five connected mountains. Wùkōng was trapped beneath Five Elements Mountain. A magical seal was placed on top.

For the first time, Sūn Wùkōng could not simply fight, fly, or transform his way out of trouble.

---

## Five Hundred Years Under a Mountain

Wùkōng remained under the mountain for five hundred years. Later, *Guānyīn*, a bodhisattva helping to arrange a great journey to collect Buddhist scriptures, came past the mountain. Wùkōng promised that if someone freed him, he would protect the scripture seeker on the dangerous road west. So he waited.

At last, a monk named *Táng Sēng* came down the road. The monk in the novel was inspired by a real Chinese Buddhist monk named *Xuánzàng*, who traveled from China to India more than thirteen hundred years ago to study Buddhism and bring important texts home. *Journey to the West* takes that real journey and fills it with magic, monsters, talking animals, and one very troublesome monkey.

Wùkōng called out to Táng Sēng from beneath the mountain and begged to be freed. Táng Sēng climbed to the top, found the magical seal, and removed it. “Move away!” Wùkōng called. Táng Sēng and the others hurried back.

Then came a tremendous crash. Rocks shook and split as Wùkōng forced his way out from under the mountain. A moment later, the Monkey King was standing in front of Táng Sēng. After five hundred years, he was free.

---

Wùkōng became Táng Sēng’s first disciple and promised to protect him. But it did not take long for a new problem to appear. Wùkōng was powerful, quick-tempered, and used to doing exactly what he wanted. Táng Sēng was gentle and believed that even dangerous situations should be handled with care.

When robbers blocked the road, Wùkōng fought them much more fiercely than Táng Sēng thought was right. Táng Sēng scolded him. Wùkōng became angry and flew away.

While Wùkōng was gone, *Guānyīn*, disguised as an old woman, gave Táng Sēng a special cap and taught him a secret spell. Wùkōng eventually came back. He saw the cap and liked it. Táng Sēng told him that it was a wonderful cap. Wùkōng put it on. Then Táng Sēng began reciting the spell.

Suddenly, a golden band hidden inside the cap tightened around Wùkōng’s head. “My head! My head!” Wùkōng cried. When Táng Sēng stopped reciting, the pain stopped. Wùkōng tried to pull the band off. It would not move. He even took the tiny needle-sized staff from his ear and tried to pry underneath it. Still no luck.

From then on, if Wùkōng became completely impossible to control, Táng Sēng had one way to stop him. Wùkōng was not pleased. But he stayed.

Soon the traveling group grew. A dragon prince who had swallowed Táng Sēng’s original horse was turned into the White Dragon Horse and became his new mount.

Then came *Zhū Bājiè*, a strong, pig-faced traveler who loved food, disliked hard work, and complained whenever the road became especially miserable. Last came *Shā Wùjìng*, a quieter and steadier companion who joined them at a dangerous river.

Now the famous group was complete: Táng Sēng on the White Dragon Horse, Sūn Wùkōng with his gold-banded staff, Zhū Bājiè with his rake, and Shā Wùjìng helping carry the luggage. They headed west. It would be a very long trip.

---

## Three People—or One Demon?

One day the travelers entered a lonely mountain. A demon called *Báigǔ Jīng*, the White Bone Demon, saw Táng Sēng and wanted to capture him. She had heard that eating the monk’s flesh could bring immortality. But there was a problem. Sūn Wùkōng was nearby. So the demon decided not to attack as herself.

She changed into a young woman carrying food and walked toward the travelers. Táng Sēng saw a young woman alone in the mountains. Zhū Bājiè noticed the food. Wùkōng saw a demon.

His fiery golden eyes were not fooled. He raised his staff and struck. But the demon escaped from the false body she had created. Táng Sēng did not see the escape. He only saw a young woman lying on the ground. He was horrified.

The demon tried again. This time she changed into an old woman who seemed to be looking for her missing daughter. Again, Wùkōng saw through the disguise. Again, the demon escaped. And again, Táng Sēng thought Wùkōng had attacked an innocent person.

The third time, the demon returned as an old man searching for both his wife and daughter. Wùkōng knew exactly what was happening. This time he finally defeated the demon for good.

---

But by then Táng Sēng had seen what appeared to be three ordinary people attacked by his disciple. He had warned Wùkōng again and again, and now he could no longer believe him. He sent Wùkōng away.

Wùkōng returned to Flower-Fruit Mountain, angry and deeply hurt. He had been protecting his master, yet his master thought he was the danger. But Táng Sēng had a problem too. Wùkōng had fiery golden eyes. Táng Sēng did not. They had looked at the same three figures and seen completely different things.

Without Wùkōng, however, the travelers soon ran into even greater danger. Eventually Zhū Bājiè had to go all the way back to Flower-Fruit Mountain and ask him to help. Wùkōng was still angry. He came back anyway.

---

## The Monkey Who No Longer Needed the Golden Band

The White Bone Demon was only one adventure in a very long journey. The novel says the travelers faced eighty-one trials. There were demons, rivers, mountains, traps, magic, hunger, arguments, and plenty of moments when Zhū Bājiè thought going home sounded like an excellent idea.

But they kept moving west. Again and again, Wùkōng used his strength and cleverness to save the group. Again and again, Táng Sēng reminded him that having great power did not mean he could always do whatever he wanted.

At last, after years of traveling, the group reached its destination and received the Buddhist scriptures. Their journey was finally complete. At the very end of the story, Wùkōng remembered the golden band around his head. Now that the journey was finished and he had earned his reward, he asked Táng Sēng to remove it.

Táng Sēng told him something surprising. “You don’t need it anymore. Touch your head.” Wùkōng reached up. The band was gone.

The monkey who had once crossed his own name out of the Book of Life and Death, fought Heaven, eaten the peaches, stolen the pills, and declared himself equal to Heaven no longer needed a magical band to make him stay on the road.

He was still Sūn Wùkōng. He was still clever, proud, impatient, and very good with a staff. But he had learned something that none of his seventy-two transformations could teach him.

Having great power is one thing.

Learning what to do with it is another.
