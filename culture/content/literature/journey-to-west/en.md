> Difficulty: Easy

# Journey to the West: Adventure with the Monkey King

## The Stone Monkey Jumps Through a Waterfall

*Journey to the West* begins on Flower-Fruit Mountain. A magic stone sits at the top. One day, the stone cracks open, and out jumps a monkey.

Later, the monkeys find a great waterfall. They agree that whoever is brave enough to jump through it will become their king. The stone monkey leaps through the rushing water and finds a wide, dry cave behind it. The waterfall hangs across the entrance like a curtain, so the monkeys call their new home Water Curtain Cave.

The stone monkey leads everyone into the cave and becomes the Handsome Monkey King. After many happy years, however, he begins to worry. What will happen when he and his friends grow old and die?

Hoping to find a way to live forever, he crosses the sea on a wooden raft and searches for years. At last, a wise teacher takes him in, gives him the name Sun Wukong, and teaches him magic. Wukong learns seventy-two transformations and how to ride a cloud across enormous distances.

---

## A Magic Staff and a Job with Heaven's Horses

Back on Flower-Fruit Mountain, Wukong begins to act as if no one has the right to tell him what to do. Under the sea, he finds a weapon called the gold-banded staff. It is not an ordinary stick made of gold. It is a magic iron staff with gold bands at both ends. It can grow as tall as the sky or shrink to the size of a needle, which Wukong hides in his ear.

One day, guards from the underworld come for Wukong. In this story, the underworld is where people are taken after death. Its Book of Life and Death records how long everyone will live. Wukong storms in and crosses out his name and the names of the other monkeys.

The Dragon King and the rulers of the underworld complain to Heaven. The Jade Emperor decides to keep Wukong nearby by giving him a job. Wukong must count and feed Heaven's horses and care for their stables. He works hard at first. Then he learns that this is one of the lowest jobs in Heaven. Furious, he quits, returns home, and calls himself “Great Sage Equal to Heaven.”

Wukong soon battles Heaven's soldiers, and even they cannot stop him. What happens next? The Buddha holds out one hand and says, “If you can jump off my palm, you win.”

Wukong turns one mighty somersault and flies to what he thinks is the end of the sky. He sees five enormous pillars and leaves a mark on one of them. But when he returns, the mark is on one of the Buddha's fingers. Wukong never left the hand at all.

Wukong tries to leap away again. The Buddha turns his hand into Five Elements Mountain, trapping Wukong beneath it. This time, the Monkey King cannot move.

---

## Five Hundred Years Later, Tang Monk Sets Him Free

Wukong waits under Five Elements Mountain for five hundred years. He can speak and wave his hands, but he cannot lift the mountain from his back.

Then a Buddhist monk called Tang Monk passes by. He is traveling west to bring Buddhist scriptures back to China. Scriptures are books that hold Buddhist teachings. Tang Monk is a storybook character, but his journey was inspired by a real Tang-dynasty monk named Xuanzang. The real Xuanzang traveled to India to study Buddhism and returned to China with many Buddhist books.

Tang Monk removes the seal from the mountain, and Wukong finally leaps free. In return, Wukong promises to become his student and protect him on the road. The two travelers set out together.

Soon they meet a strong, pig-faced traveler named Zhu Bajie. He was once an officer in Heaven, but a punishment left him looking like a pig. He loves food and often complains. When trouble comes, he may be the first to ask, “Should we give up and go home?” Yet when a monster attacks, he usually picks up his nine-toothed rake and helps.

Sha Monk joins them later. He is quiet and carries much of the luggage. Tang Monk's white horse is really a dragon prince in the shape of a horse. At last, the noisy traveling team is complete.

---

## How Did the White Bone Demon Fool Tang Monk?

On a lonely mountain, the White Bone Demon spots Tang Monk. She believes that eating even one piece of him will make her live forever. Wukong is away picking peaches, but Zhu Bajie and Sha Monk are nearby, so she cannot simply rush in and grab him.

First, the demon changes into a girl carrying food. Wukong returns, sees through the disguise, and swings his staff. The demon escapes by leaving a false body behind. Tang Monk sees only a girl lying on the ground and thinks Wukong has hurt an innocent person.

The demon returns as an old woman looking for her daughter. Then she returns as an old man looking for his wife and daughter. Wukong sees the demon every time, but Tang Monk sees three helpless people. After the third disguise, Tang Monk refuses to listen and sends Wukong back to Flower-Fruit Mountain.

Without Wukong, the travelers have lost the one person who can see through a monster's tricks. Before long, another monster captures Tang Monk. Zhu Bajie must run to Flower-Fruit Mountain and ask Wukong to return. Wukong is still angry, but he goes back with Bajie and rescues his teacher once again.

---

## The Journey Is Not Over Yet

The White Bone Demon is only one stop on the long journey. The book says the travelers face eighty-one trials—nine times nine. They are not all monster fights. They also include traps, misunderstandings, hunger, rivers they cannot cross, and mountains that block the road.

Wukong keeps protecting Tang Monk, while Zhu Bajie and Sha Monk continue beside them. At last, the group reaches the Western Heaven, receives the scriptures, and starts home. But one final trial is still missing. On the return trip, they fall into the Tongtian River, and even a few of the books get wet.

At last, they carry the scriptures back to Chang'an. Chang'an was China's capital city at the time, where the emperor lived. Only then is the long journey truly over. Tang Monk, Wukong, Zhu Bajie, Sha Monk, and the White Dragon Horse have all made it to the end.
