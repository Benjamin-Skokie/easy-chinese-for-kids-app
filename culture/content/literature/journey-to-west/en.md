# Journey to the West: Adventure with the Monkey King

## A Monkey Jumps Out of a Stone

Long, long ago, on Flower-Fruit Mountain by the sea, there was a magical stone.

It had been there since the beginning of the world, drinking in sunlight and moonlight.

One day, the stone cracked open.

Out jumped a stone monkey.

The moment he was born, he could walk and talk. Golden light shot from his eyes all the way up to Heaven, startling even the Jade Emperor.

The stone monkey found Water Curtain Cave, a great cave hidden behind a waterfall.

There he became the Handsome Monkey King of Flower-Fruit Mountain.

For hundreds of years, he lived happily with the other monkeys.

Then one day, he stared at the fruit and vegetables on the table and became quiet.

An old monkey asked, "What is wrong?"

The Monkey King said, "We are happy now. But one day we will grow old, get sick, and die."

The old monkey told him to look for a magical teacher and learn the secret of living forever.

So Wukong built a wooden raft and crossed the sea.

He found a teacher named Bodhi, who gave him the name Sun Wukong.

Bodhi taught him seventy-two transformations.

He could change into a tree, a fish, a fly, or a stone.

Bodhi also taught him the cloud somersault.

With one flip, Wukong could travel an enormous distance: 108,000 *li*.

---

## Making Trouble in Heaven

After learning so many skills, Sun Wukong felt he deserved an important title.

First, he went to the Dragon Palace of the Eastern Sea to find a weapon.

He chose the golden-hooped staff, a magic iron pillar that could grow huge or shrink small enough to hide in his ear.

Then he rushed into the court of the King of Hell.

He crossed out his own name and the names of all his monkeys from the book of life and death.

His message was clear:

"Your list no longer has us. No one can take us away."

The Jade Emperor sent soldiers to catch him, but they could not win.

So Heaven tried another plan.

They gave him an official job.

First he became *Bimawen*, keeper of the heavenly horses.

After a few days, Wukong learned this was a very low job.

He became furious, smashed the horse office, fought out through the Southern Heaven Gate, and returned to Flower-Fruit Mountain.

There he gave himself a new title:

"Great Sage Equal to Heaven."

The Jade Emperor had to send heavenly soldiers and generals after him.

The Mighty Spirit God was knocked away by one strike.

Nezha fought with many arms and weapons, but still lost.

Erlang Shen chased him with his heavenly dog and many fighters.

At last, Laozi helped capture Wukong.

They tied him up in Heaven and tried blades, axes, and fire.

Nothing worked.

Laozi placed him inside the alchemy furnace and burned him for forty-nine days.

When the furnace opened, Wukong jumped out through the flames.

Now he had fiery golden eyes.

He grabbed his staff and fought all the way toward the Hall of Heaven.

The whole sky palace was thrown into chaos.

At last, the Jade Emperor asked the Buddha for help.

The Buddha held out one hand and asked, "Can you jump out of my palm?"

Wukong laughed and flipped away.

He traveled 108,000 *li* and saw five pillars at the edge of the sky.

He thought he had reached the end of Heaven.

So he wrote on the middle pillar, "The Great Sage Equal to Heaven was here."

Then he returned proudly.

The Buddha showed him his hand.

The five pillars were the Buddha's fingers.

Wukong had never left the Buddha's palm.

The Buddha turned his hand over, and the five fingers became Five Elements Mountain.

Sun Wukong was trapped under the mountain for 500 years.

---

## Tang Monk and His Three Disciples

Five hundred years later, Guanyin found Sun Wukong under the mountain.

"Let me out!" Wukong shouted.

Guanyin said, "I can. But you must protect someone traveling west to bring back Buddhist holy books."

Wukong agreed at once.

That person was Xuanzang, a monk from the Tang dynasty.

He left Chang'an and traveled west.

Guanyin arranged three disciples and a white horse to help him.

The first disciple was Sun Wukong.

Because Wukong was hard to control, Guanyin put a golden band around his head and taught Tang Monk a spell.

When Tang Monk recited the spell, the band tightened painfully.

This became Tang Monk's strongest way to stop Wukong when he lost control.

The second disciple was Zhu Bajie.

In his past life, he had been a heavenly commander.

But after making a serious mistake at a heavenly banquet, he was sent down to earth and accidentally reborn with a pig face, big ears, and a round belly.

He is greedy, lazy, and easily distracted.

He often complains about hunger and talks about quitting.

But he is not weak.

When real danger comes, his nine-toothed rake can still strike hard.

The third disciple was Sha Monk.

In his past life, he had served near the Jade Emperor.

After breaking a precious glass cup by mistake, he was punished and sent to the Flowing Sand River.

Guanyin later guided him, and he joined the group.

Sha Monk is the steady one.

He carries the luggage, looks after the teacher, calms arguments, and does not talk much.

But he rarely lets the team down.

The white horse is not ordinary either.

He is a dragon prince from the Western Sea, changed into a horse to carry Tang Monk.

Most of the time, he stays quiet.

Only in rare moments does he show who he really is.

---

## Eighty-One Trials

Tang Monk and his disciples travel for fourteen years and face eighty-one trials.

Not every trial is a monster battle.

Each one is like a test.

The story of the White Bone Demon is one of the hardest tests between Wukong and his teacher.

The demon first changes into a young village woman bringing food.

Wukong sees through her and strikes.

Tang Monk thinks Wukong has hurt an innocent person, so he recites the tightening spell.

Wukong cries that she was a demon, but Tang Monk does not believe him.

The demon returns as an old woman.

Then as an old man.

Each time, Wukong knows the danger.

Each time, Tang Monk sees only a person.

At last, Tang Monk sends Wukong away.

Wukong kneels, bows, and leaves with tears.

He truly cares about his teacher.

But his teacher does not trust him.

Later, Tang Monk is captured and almost killed.

Zhu Bajie goes to Flower-Fruit Mountain to ask Wukong to return.

Wukong could refuse.

He is the Great Sage Equal to Heaven.

He does not need the journey.

But the monkey returns.

The Flaming Mountain is another famous trial.

The whole mountain burns, and nothing can grow for hundreds of *li*.

To pass, the travelers need the Plantain Fan, which can put out the flames.

The fan belongs to Princess Iron Fan.

But she refuses to lend it because Wukong once caused trouble for her son, Red Boy.

Wukong tries tricks, changes shape, and battles back and forth with Princess Iron Fan and Bull Demon King.

After many turns, with help from heavenly forces, he finally gets the real fan.

The fire is put out.

The travelers cross the mountain.

---

## After They Receive the True Scriptures

At the last stop, Tang Monk reaches a special crossing called Lingyun Ferry.

The boat has no bottom.

When Tang Monk looks into the water, he sees his old human body floating away.

This means he is leaving behind ordinary earthly limits.

Only then is he ready to receive the holy books.

At Great Thunderclap Monastery, two honored disciples first give the travelers blank books.

Tang Monk takes them to the Buddha.

The Buddha calmly explains that the true books cannot be received too easily.

Tang Monk offers the purple-gold begging bowl given by the Tang emperor.

Then he receives the real books with writing inside.

On the way home, Guanyin counts the trials.

They have completed eighty.

One is still missing.

The travelers fall from the clouds into the Tongtian River, and the books get wet.

After this final trial, they return to Chang'an and bring the books to the Great Wild Goose Pagoda.

The holy books they carried back after fourteen years became part of Chinese culture's deep stream.

---

## Why Is Journey to the West China's Great Adventure Story?

Today, *Journey to the West* is usually tied to the Ming dynasty writer Wu Cheng'en.

But the story lived for a long time before it became the novel we know.

It traveled through storytellers' mouths, theater stages, and children's ears.

As people told it, it changed.

Later, there were even more versions.

Peking opera, puppet shows, shadow plays, cartoons, TV shows, and movies have kept telling it.

Ask many people in China, "Do you know Sun Wukong?"

They will probably smile and say, "Of course. That monkey."

People know his seventy-two transformations.

They know Zhu Bajie loves food.

They know the White Bone Demon and the Flaming Mountain.

Some learned these stories from the book.

Some heard them from family.

Some saw them on stage or television.

The monkey who jumped out of a stone has stayed with Chinese people for a very long time.

He is still telling stories today.

## Summary

A stone monkey jumps out of a magical rock on Flower-Fruit Mountain.

He crosses the sea, learns transformations and cloud somersaults, steals the golden-hooped staff, and makes trouble in Heaven.

After the Buddha traps him under Five Elements Mountain for 500 years, he joins Tang Monk on a fourteen-year journey west.

Along the way, they meet the White Bone Demon, the Flaming Mountain, and many other trials.

There are monsters and battles.

There are also hurt feelings, misunderstandings, and the choice to come back and keep going.

Even today, Sun Wukong and his companions are among the busiest and most adventurous heroes in Chinese stories.
