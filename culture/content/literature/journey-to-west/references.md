# Editorial References: Journey to the West

This file is for editorial research and does not render on the website.

Accessed: 2026-09-01

## 1. The stone monkey, Water Curtain Cave, and his teacher

Source: Wikisource, *Journey to the West*, Chapter 1
URL: https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E8%A5%BF%E9%81%8A%E8%A8%98/%E7%AC%AC001%E5%9B%9E

Supports:

- The monkey's birth from stone, his leap through the waterfall, the cave behind the hanging water, and his rise as Monkey King.
- His fear of death, long search for a teacher, new name, and magical training.

Editorial boundary:

- “Water Curtain Cave” is the common English name. The article also explains the image behind the name so a child does not have to guess what it means.
- The teacher's name and the details of Wukong's years at the school are omitted to keep the story moving.

## 2. The gold-banded staff, the underworld, and the heavenly stables

Source: Wikisource, *Journey to the West*, Chapter 3
URL: https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E8%A5%BF%E9%81%8A%E8%A8%98/%E7%AC%AC003%E5%9B%9E

- Confirms that the Ruyi Jingu Bang is an iron staff with gold bands that can grow or shrink, and that Wukong stores it in his ear at needle size.
- Confirms the Book of Life and Death episode in the underworld.

Source: Wikisource, *Journey to the West*, Chapter 4
URL: https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E8%A5%BF%E9%81%8A%E8%A8%98/%E7%AC%AC004%E5%9B%9E

- Confirms that Wukong is placed in charge of the heavenly horses, works at the stables, discovers the low rank of the post, leaves Heaven, and adopts the title Great Sage Equal to Heaven.

Editorial boundary:

- The English article uses “gold-banded staff” rather than the misleading “golden staff.”
- The underworld and Wukong's stable duties are explained inline for readers who do not know the story.

## 3. The Buddha's hand and Five Elements Mountain

Source: Wikisource, *Journey to the West*, Chapter 7
URL: https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E8%A5%BF%E9%81%8A%E8%A8%98/%E7%AC%AC007%E5%9B%9E

- Confirms that Wukong stands on the Buddha's palm, mistakes the five fingers for pillars, finds his mark on one of the Buddha's fingers, and is trapped beneath Five Elements Mountain.

Editorial boundary:

- A crude joke in the original marking scene is omitted from the story for this age group.

## 4. Tang Monk, Xuanzang, scriptures, and the traveling team

Source: Asia for Educators, Columbia University, “The Origins of the Story”
URL: https://afe.easia.columbia.edu/cosmos/main/using_monkey.pdf

- Explains that the novel's pilgrimage follows the monk Xuanzang's historical journey to India to gather Buddhist scriptures.

Source: Wikisource, *Journey to the West*, complete novel
URL: https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E8%A5%BF%E9%81%8A%E8%A8%98

- Chapters 14, 19, and 22 support Wukong's release and the addition of Zhu Bajie and Sha Monk to the pilgrimage team.
- The novel's later chapters support the team's eventual arrival, receipt of the scriptures, and return journey.

Editorial boundary:

- “Scriptures” is explained as books containing Buddhist teachings.
- The article distinguishes the fictional Tang Monk from the real Xuanzang in two direct sentences rather than using the awkward label “the model behind Tang Monk.”
- Only the details needed to recognize Zhu Bajie, Sha Monk, and the White Dragon Horse are included.

## 5. The White Bone Demon and Wukong's return

Source: Wikisource, *Journey to the West*, Chapter 27
URL: https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E8%A5%BF%E9%81%8A%E8%A8%98/%E7%AC%AC027%E5%9B%9E

- Confirms the demon's plan to eat Tang Monk for long life, her three disguises, the false bodies, Wukong's ability to recognize her, and Tang Monk's decision to send him away.
- Chapters 28–31 of the same edition continue the story through Tang Monk's next capture, Zhu Bajie's return to Flower-Fruit Mountain, and Wukong's rescue.

## 6. The eighty-one trials and the return to Chang'an

Source: Wikisource, *Journey to the West*, Chapter 99
URL: https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E8%A5%BF%E9%81%8A%E8%A8%98/%E7%AC%AC099%E5%9B%9E

- Confirms the recorded sequence of eighty-one trials, the missing final trial, the return to the Tongtian River, and the wet scriptures.

Source: Wikisource, *Journey to the West*, Chapter 100
URL: https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E8%A5%BF%E9%81%8A%E8%A8%98/%E7%AC%AC100%E5%9B%9E

- Confirms the party's return to Chang'an and delivery of the scriptures.

Editorial boundary:

- Chang'an is explained inline as China's capital city at the time and the city where the emperor lived.

## Image note

All four pictures are modern children's-book illustrations. They are not historical reconstructions. The second illustration reuses the Dragon Palace composition from the site's Monkey King story, with the staff corrected to show a dark iron shaft and gold bands at its two ends. It replaces the earlier Buddha-palm illustration.
