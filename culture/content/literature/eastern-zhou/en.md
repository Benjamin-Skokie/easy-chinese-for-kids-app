# Stories of the Eastern Zhou: An Age of Many Voices

## One Book Tells Five Hundred Years

*Stories of the Eastern Zhou States* is a historical novel that was put together in the Ming dynasty.

It tells stories from more than 500 years of the Eastern Zhou, a long age of fighting, competition, and change.

Inside are famous rulers called the Five Hegemons, the Seven Warring States, and many famous people and sayings.

The book turns the long history before the Qin dynasty into one story after another.

One interesting thing about it is how much it borrows from older books such as *Zuo Zhuan*, *Discourses of the States*, and *Strategies of the Warring States*.

But it is still a historical novel, not a word-for-word history record.

You can use it to feel the big shape of early Chinese history.

But you should not treat every scene as exact original history.

## Guan Zhong and Bao Shuya: Business Can Lose Money, Friendship Cannot Be Thrown Away

Guan Zhong and Bao Shuya were friends from childhood.

When they were young, they did business together.

Guan Zhong often put in less money but took more of the profit.

Other people whispered that Guan Zhong was greedy.

Bao Shuya stopped them.

"His family is poor," he said. "He needs more money to care for his mother. I am not being cheated. This is what should happen."

Later, the two friends served different masters.

Guan Zhong helped Prince Jiu.

Bao Shuya helped Prince Xiaobai.

When Prince Xiaobai became Duke Huan of Qi, he wanted to kill Guan Zhong.

Bao Shuya stopped him.

He said, "If you only want to run Qi, I am enough. But if you want to lead all the states, you must have Guan Zhong."

Duke Huan listened.

He brought Guan Zhong back from the state of Lu and made him the top minister.

Guan Zhong changed Qi's army and government, built wealth, and strengthened the state.

Duke Huan became the first great leader of the Spring and Autumn period.

Later, Guan Zhong said, "My parents gave birth to me. But Bao Shuya truly understood me."

## Sleeping on Firewood and Tasting Bitter Gall

After King Fuchai of Wu defeated Yue, he captured King Goujian of Yue.

Fuchai did not kill him.

Instead, he made Goujian serve him like a stable worker.

For three years, Goujian cleaned horse waste in the Wu stables. He ate whatever leftovers were given to him.

After three years, Fuchai softened and let him return to Yue.

When Goujian went home, he put firewood under his bed.

Every night, he lay on the rough wood and let it prick his back.

It reminded him of the shame he had suffered in the stables.

Above his bed, he hung a bitter gallbladder.

Every morning, before washing his face, he tasted it.

The bitterness filled his mouth and stomach.

Then he asked himself, "Have you forgotten the shame at Kuaiji?"

For ten years, Yue gathered people and supplies.

For another ten years, Yue trained and prepared.

After twenty years, Yue defeated Wu.

The story of Goujian became one of China's strongest pictures of patience, bitterness, and revenge.

## Shang Yang's Big Changes: Move a Log, Receive Gold

Qin was once poor and weak compared with the states to the east.

Duke Xiao of Qin invited Shang Yang from Wei to lead big changes.

Shang Yang knew the people of Qin did not really believe the government would keep its promises.

Old habits and not trusting the government had grown deep.

So he placed a tall wooden log at the south gate of the capital and posted a notice:

Whoever moves this log to the north gate will receive ten pieces of gold.

No one believed it.

People gathered in circles to watch, but no one moved.

Shang Yang raised the reward to fifty pieces of gold.

At last, a young farmer stepped out, lifted the log, and carried it through the capital.

When he set it at the north gate, Shang Yang gave him the reward in front of everyone.

The message was clear:

the Qin government keeps its word.

Later people loved retelling this scene as "moving the log to build trust."

It does not explain all of Shang Yang's changes, but it shows one important idea:

before new orders can work, people must believe they will be carried out.

Shang Yang's real power was that he changed Qin's land rules, army rewards, and government rules.

It would not be true to say everyone suddenly had a completely equal chance.

But Qin did become stronger and better at war because of these rules.

---

## Summary

*Stories of the Eastern Zhou States* makes more than 500 years of early Chinese stories lively and forceful.

It is not the same as a complete history of the time before Qin.

But it is a good doorway into an age of many thinkers, many states, and many struggles for power.

When you read it, you may remember Guan Zhong and Bao Shuya's friendship, Goujian tasting bitterness, and Shang Yang building trust with one log.

You may also begin to see how later Chinese people kept looking back to that early age for courage, friendship, patience, and trust.
