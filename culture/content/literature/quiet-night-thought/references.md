# Editorial References: Quiet Night Thought

This file is for editorial research and does not render on the website.

## 1. Poem text and textual variants

Source: Wikisource, Li Bai, “Quiet Night Thought” (《静夜思》)
URL: https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hans/%E9%9D%99%E5%A4%9C%E6%80%9D
Accessed: 2026-09-06

Supports:

- The poem’s attribution to Li Bai and the familiar twenty-character school version beginning “床前明月光.”
- The existence of textual variants such as “看月光” and “望山月” in other transmitted editions.

Editorial boundary:

- The article uses the version most familiar from modern children’s anthologies and classrooms; it does not claim that this wording is the only transmitted text.
- The English poem lines are an original child-facing translation made for this project, not a quotation from a published English translation.

## 2. Child-facing interpretation and reception

Source: Hong Kong Education Bureau, *Recommended Classical Chinese Passages for Primary Students*, “Quiet Night Thought”
URL: https://www.edb.gov.hk/attachment/tc/curriculum-development/kla/chi-edu/recommended-passages/Booklet_KS1_uploadver2.pdf
Accessed: 2026-09-06

Supports:

- The poem’s broad circulation and its place in primary-level classical-poetry learning.
- The reading of the poem as Li Bai seeing moonlight while away from home and moving from the image of frost to thoughts of his homeland.
- The traditional association between the moon, reunion, and missing home or distant friends.

Editorial boundary:

- The inn, the moment of waking, and the half-asleep reaction are child-facing scene details inferred from the poem and its traditional interpretation, not securely documented biographical facts about the poem’s composition.
- The image of *Quiet Night Thought* “opening a door” for later poems is a literary metaphor; moon and homesickness were associated in Chinese poetry before Li Bai.

## 3. Literary analysis

Source: Hong Kong Education Bureau, “Appreciating Classical Chinese Masterpieces (6)” (中國文言經典名篇賞析（六）)
URL: https://www.edb.gov.hk/attachment/tc/curriculum-development/kla/chi-edu/resources/primary/lang/pdp/20250308_PDF.pdf
Accessed: 2026-09-06

Supports:

- The poem’s movement from “疑” to “举头” and “低头,” and the way these few actions create a vivid scene of homesickness on a moonlit night.
- The poem’s plain language, accessibility to children, and long history of use in early learning.

Editorial boundary:

- The article keeps the interpretation concrete and suitable for ages six to ten rather than reproducing competing scholarly evaluations of the poem.
