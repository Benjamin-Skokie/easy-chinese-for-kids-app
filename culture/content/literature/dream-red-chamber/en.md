# Dream of the Red Chamber: A World Inside Grand View Garden

## A Stone Wants to Visit the Human World

When Nuwa repaired the sky, she made 36,501 stones.

After the sky was fixed, one stone was left over.

It was placed below Green Ridge Peak on a faraway wild mountain.

For countless years, the stone absorbed sunlight and moonlight. Slowly, it became magical.

It turned into a piece of jade called the "Spiritual Jade."

The jade wanted to visit the human world.

A limping Daoist teacher and a monk with a strange-looking head carried it down to earth.

There, it became the piece of jade found in a baby boy's mouth.

That boy was Jia Baoyu.

---

## A Boy Who Does Not Want to Grow Up

Jia Baoyu is a very unusual main character in Chinese fiction.

He does not like studying for exams.

He does not like spending time with people who care only about becoming officials.

He would rather be with the girls in the garden.

One of his most famous sayings is that girls are made of water, while men are made of mud.

That idea is unfair and childish in some ways.

But it also shows how different Baoyu is from the world around him.

Everyone in the Jia family wants him to pass the exams, become an official, and bring honor to the family.

Baoyu refuses to live that way.

What he loves most is poetry, beauty, and the gentle people inside the garden.

---

## Grand View Garden: A Garden of Youth

Baoyu's older sister becomes one of the emperor's important palace women.

When she returns home for a visit, the family builds a huge and beautiful garden for her.

It is called Grand View Garden.

After the visit ends, the garden is left empty.

Baoyu and his girl cousins move in.

Daiyu lives at Bamboo Lodge, surrounded by green bamboo.

Baochai lives at a simpler, clean, quiet courtyard.

Miaoyu practices Buddhism at Green Lattice Nunnery, though she still keeps her hair.

The garden becomes a place for young people.

What do they do there most often?

They hold poetry clubs.

The Crabapple Poetry Club. The Peach Blossom Poetry Club.

Everyone writes a poem, and the best one is chosen.

Lin Daiyu and Xue Baochai often take turns winning.

These two girls are among the most moving characters in the whole book.

One feels like poetry and impossible ideals.

The other feels like good sense and real life.

---

## Daiyu Buries Flowers

One of the most famous scenes in Grand View Garden is Lin Daiyu burying fallen flowers.

On a late spring afternoon, she carries a small flower hoe on her shoulder and a silk bag in her hand.

She picks up fallen petals one by one and buries them.

She cannot bear to see the flowers rot in mud and dirty water.

She would rather give them a quiet place herself.

Does Daiyu herself seem like one of those fallen flowers?

Many readers think so.

Later, Daiyu becomes very ill. She burns her writings and dies.

That part of the novel is written with great sadness and beauty.

It is fiction, of course.

But the feeling of flowers falling and filling the sky is something many readers never forget.

---

## The Jia Family Falls

The Jia family was once extremely grand.

At the gate stood stone lions and a golden sign showing the family's honor.

During festivals, people bringing gifts could line up for streets.

Then everything changed very quickly.

The emperor ordered the family property taken away.

Guards sealed the doors and carried out boxes.

The Jia family's wealth collapsed.

The girls and servants who had lived in Grand View Garden were forced out. Some met very sad endings.

Baoyu saw everything fall apart.

In the end, people imagine him in heavy snow: his head shaved, his feet bare, wearing a bright red cloak, following a monk and a Daoist teacher into the white distance.

He never comes back.

Behind him, Xue Baochai is left with the last small hope of the Jia family line.

---

## Why Is It Called China's Greatest Novel?

*Dream of the Red Chamber* is more than a novel.

It later created a whole field of study called "Redology."

Many scholars spend years studying its characters, objects, gardens, and different versions.

As for the author Cao Xueqin, we know some things about his life, but many parts are still unclear.

What seems clear is this:

he pressed family rise and fall, memories of youth, and very fine feelings into this book.

That is why the book feels so alive.

---

## Summary

*Dream of the Red Chamber* is wrapped in a shell of myth.

Inside, it is about human feeling, youth, family, and decline.

You may remember a magical jade.

You may remember a fallen flower, a garden, and a snowstorm.

Many Chinese readers return to this book again and again.

They are doing more than reading a story.

They are watching how, in the brightest and busiest place, beauty slowly begins to scatter.
