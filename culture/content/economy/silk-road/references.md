# Silk Roads — References and Editorial Notes

This file is for editorial research and does not render on the website.

## The Silk Roads as a network

Source: UNESCO, “About the Silk Roads”
URL: https://www.unesco.org/en/silk-roads/about-silk-roads?hub=196704
Accessed: 2026-09-05

Supports:

- The Silk Roads consisted of many land and sea routes rather than one continuous road.
- Caravans often traveled particular sections, stopping to rest, replenish supplies, sell cargo, and exchange goods with local communities.
- Silk and many other goods, languages, beliefs, skills, and ideas moved through this network; Han-period Chinese textiles have been found as far away as Egypt.

Editorial boundary:

- “Silk Roads” is used as a modern collective name; it was not the name ancient travelers gave to one marked highway.

## The Chang'an–Tianshan route network

Source: UNESCO World Heritage Centre, “Silk Roads: the Routes Network of Chang'an-Tianshan Corridor”
URL: https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1442
Accessed: 2026-09-05

Supports:

- The corridor formed one section of a much larger interconnected web of routes crossing deserts, rivers, grasslands, and snow-covered mountains.
- High-value goods, especially silk, could travel great distances through a chain of different merchants.

Editorial boundary:

- This World Heritage corridor is one part of the broader Silk Roads and should not be treated as the entire network.

## Zhang Qian's mission

Source: Sima Qian, *Shiji* (《史记·大宛列传》)
URL: https://ctext.org/shiji/da-wan-lie-zhuan/ens
Accessed: 2026-09-05

Supports:

- Zhang Qian was sent to seek an alliance with the Yuezhi, was detained by the Xiongnu for more than ten years, continued west after escaping, and returned after thirteen years.
- The account says that only two of the party that originally numbered more than one hundred returned together.

Editorial boundary:

- The mission was diplomatic rather than a shopping expedition or an order to create a trade road.

## Zhang Qian and later connections with Central Asia

Source: UNESCO Silk Roads Programme, “Zhang Qian”
URL: https://en.unesco.org/silkroad/content/zhang-qian
Accessed: 2026-09-05

Supports:

- Zhang Qian's original purpose was to seek an alliance with the Yuezhi against the Xiongnu.
- His reports gave the Han court new information about Central Asia and helped strengthen later official contact and trade.

Editorial boundary:

- The routes and regional exchanges existed before Zhang Qian; the story does not call him the sole founder of the Silk Roads.

## Cities and stopping places

Source: UNESCO Silk Roads Programme, “Cities along the Silk Roads”
URL: https://en.unesco.org/silkroad/silk-road-themes/cities-silk-roads
Accessed: 2026-09-05

Supports:

- Cities allowed travelers to rest their animals, find supplies, buy and sell goods, and meet other travelers.
- These stopping places supported the exchange of languages, customs, skills, and ideas as well as material goods.

Editorial boundary:

- The oasis-market illustration combines representative travelers and goods; it does not document one identified transaction at a single historical market.
