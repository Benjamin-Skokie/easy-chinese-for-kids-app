# Tang Chang'an — References and Editorial Notes

This file is for editorial research and does not render on the website.

## Central Asian influences in Tang life

Source: The Metropolitan Museum of Art, “Internationalism in the Tang Dynasty (618–907)”
URL: https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/internationalism-in-the-tang-dynasty-618-906
Accessed: 2026-09-05

Supports:

- Central Asian influences on Tang clothing, music, dance, polo, objects, and artistic styles.
- Tang craftspeople adapted new techniques, shapes, and designs from objects that traveled to China.

Editorial boundary:

- The story describes cultural influence and adaptation rather than claiming that every Tang person adopted the same foreign fashions.

## Visitors in Chang'an

Source: The Metropolitan Museum of Art, “Tang Dynasty (618–907)”
URL: https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/tang-dynasty-618-906
Accessed: 2026-09-05

Supports:

- Merchants, religious figures, and envoys from India, Persia, Arabia, Syria, Korea, Japan, and other regions were present in Chang'an.
- Chang'an was one of the largest cities in the world during the Tang period.

Editorial boundary:

- The story does not treat these varied visitors as one uniform community or invent a specific meeting among them.

## Sogdian traders, wine, entertainment, and vessels

Source: British Museum, “Who Were the Sogdians?”
URL: https://www.britishmuseum.org/blog/who-were-sogdians
Accessed: 2026-09-05

Supports:

- Sogdians played an important role in travel, trade, entertainment, and cultural exchange across the Silk Roads.
- In Tang Chang'an, Sogdians helped popularize grape wine and new drinking practices.
- Chinese craftspeople made drinking vessels inspired by Sogdian shapes, including lobed bodies and ring handles.

Editorial boundary:

- The illustrated stall is not identified as a documented shop, and its vessels are not presented as exact copies of surviving artifacts.

## Chang'an and Japanese missions

Source: British Museum, *Silk Roads* Exhibition Guide
URL: https://www.britishmuseum.org/exhibitions/silk-roads/large-print-guide
Accessed: 2026-09-05

Supports:

- Tang Chang'an had an international population and received travelers from distant regions.
- Japanese missions brought envoys, monks, students, officials, and other travelers to Tang China.
- Central Asian influences became part of Tang life, while Tang ideas were adapted in places including Nara.

Editorial boundary:

- The story describes travelers selecting and adapting ideas rather than copying Chang'an exactly.

## The Jingjiao Stele

Source: Xi'an Beilin Museum, “大秦景教流行中国碑”
URL: https://www.beilin-museum.com/index.php?a=index&aid=2999&c=View&lang=en&m=home
Accessed: 2026-09-05

Supports:

- The Church of the East stele was erected in Chang'an in 781 and records the history of Jingjiao in Tang China.
- The monument has a long Chinese inscription, Syriac names, and a cross at the top.

Editorial boundary:

- The stele documents one religious community; it is not used to estimate how many people in Chang'an followed that tradition.

## The first recorded Church of the East mission

Source: British Museum, “The First Christian Missionaries,” in the *Silk Roads* Exhibition Guide
URL: https://www.britishmuseum.org/exhibitions/silk-roads/large-print-guide
Accessed: 2026-09-05

Supports:

- The first recorded Church of the East mission reached China in 635.
- The Chang'an stele combines Chinese text, Syriac names, and a cross.

Editorial boundary:

- The story uses “Church of the East” alongside the Tang Chinese name Jingjiao and does not invent the travelers' exact route or conversations.
