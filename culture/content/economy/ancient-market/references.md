# Ancient Markets — References and Editorial Notes

This file is for editorial research and does not render on the website.

## Periodic rural markets

Source: Zhengying Liu, Qiyang Liu, Pengjun Zhao, Junqing Tang, and Zhaoya Gong, “Investigating Access to Periodic Markets in Rural China,” *Transportation Research Part D: Transport and Environment* 103 (2022), article 103162
URL: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.trd.2021.103162
Accessed: 2026-09-05

Supports:

- Periodic markets remain an important way for rural residents in China to reach sellers and services.
- Buyers and sellers gather at recurring markets rather than requiring a permanent daily market in every village.

Editorial boundary:

- This modern access study reviews the long history of periodic markets but does not establish one universal schedule for every region or historical period.

## Rural fairs and their names

Source: Fang Ren, “The Rural Market in Late Imperial China,” *Asian Social Science* 6, no. 6 (2010)
URL: https://www.ccsenet.org/journal/index.php/ass/article/view/6220
Accessed: 2026-09-05

Supports:

- Regular rural fairs were known by regional names including *jí*, *xū*, *chǎng*, and *shì*.
- Such fairs linked villages, towns, producers, traders, and customers in traditional rural commerce.

Editorial boundary:

- The article is strongest for late imperial China; the child-facing story therefore avoids assigning its detailed classifications to all earlier periods.

## Tang Chang'an and its two markets

Source: National Museum of China, “Looking Back at Chang'an” (《回望长安》)
URL: https://www.chnmuseum.cn/yj/xscg/xslw/201812/t20181224_33181.shtml
Accessed: 2026-09-05

Supports:

- Tang Chang'an was divided into walled residential wards arranged along broad streets.
- The East Market and West Market were major commercial districts, each occupying two wards.

Editorial boundary:

- The article supports the planned urban setting, not every invented person, stall, or object shown in the accompanying illustration.

## Night markets in the Tang and Song eras

Source: Takugo Shio, “The Night Market during the Tang and Song Eras,” *Tōyōshi Kenkyū* 73, no. 2 (2014)
URL: https://doi.org/10.14989/227432
Accessed: 2026-09-05

Supports:

- Tang Chang'an operated within a tightly controlled *fāng-shì* urban system.
- Commerce and night trading gradually spread beyond older restrictions, while Song capitals became especially well known for lively night markets.

Editorial boundary:

- The transition was gradual rather than a single overnight change from Tang restrictions to unrestricted Song commerce.

## Kaifeng night markets and the “ghost market”

Source: Meng Yuanlao, *Dongjing Menghua Lu* (《东京梦华录》), volumes 2–3
URL: https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hans/%E6%9D%B1%E4%BA%AC%E5%A4%A2%E8%8F%AF%E9%8C%84/%E5%8D%B7%E4%BA%8C
Accessed: 2026-09-05

Supports:

- The account describes Kaifeng night markets, food stalls, shops reopening before dawn, carts bringing goods into the city, and vendors calling in the streets.
- Volume 2 records a pre-dawn market selling clothes, paintings, and flower decorations that dispersed at daylight and was called the “ghost market” (*guǐshìzi*).

Editorial boundary:

- Meng's memoir describes particular places in Northern Song Kaifeng; it does not mean that every Song market kept the same hours or sold the same goods.
