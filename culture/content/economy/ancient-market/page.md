[section]
page: 1
layout: two-column
heading_zh: 什么是赶集？
heading_en: Going to Market
image: 1-1-market-day.webp
caption_zh: 赶集那天，小镇一下子热闹起来
caption_en: On market day, a quiet town suddenly fills with people, goods, and noise.
[zh]
古时候没有超市，也没有网购。想买东西，去哪里买？去"集市"。

集市不是天天都有的。很多地方，每隔五天或十天才有一次集市。到了那一天，周围十里八村的人，挑着担子、推着小车、牵着牛羊，从四面八方赶过来。"赶集"这个词，就是"赶着去集市"的意思。

你平常安安静静的小镇上，赶集那天突然就热闹了。街上全是人，卖东西的喊声一个比一个大，空气里到处都是食物的香味。小孩子最喜欢赶集了-可以看热闹，有时候大人还会给买一块糖。
[/zh]
[en]
Long ago, there were no supermarkets and no online shopping. If people wanted to buy something, where did they go? They went to the market.

The market was not open every day. In many places, it came only once every five days or once every ten days. On that day, people from all the nearby villages came from every direction. They carried shoulder poles, pushed small carts, and led cows or sheep. The Chinese phrase *gan ji* means hurrying to the market.

On ordinary days, a small town might be quiet. But on market day, it suddenly became lively. The street filled with people. Sellers shouted louder than each other, and the air smelled of food. Children loved market day most of all. They could watch the excitement, and sometimes an adult even bought them a piece of candy.
[/en]
[/section]

[section]
page: 2
layout: two-column reverse
heading_zh: 长安的东市和西市
heading_en: The East and West Markets of Chang'an
image: 1-3-east-west-market.webp
caption_zh: 长安的大市场里，能见到本地货和远方来的货
caption_en: In Chang'an's great markets, goods from nearby and far away sat side by side.
[zh]
唐朝的都城长安，是当时世界上数一数二的繁华大都市。长安城里有专门的市场-东市和西市。

东市卖的东西贵一些，主要服务住在城东的有钱人和官员。西市更热闹，什么都有-吃的、穿的、用的，还有从丝绸之路远道而来的外国商品：波斯的玻璃杯、西域的葡萄酒、印度的香料。金头发蓝眼睛的外国商人站在摊子后面，用不流利的汉语和顾客讨价还价。

东市和西市每天中午开门。开门的时候要敲鼓-咚、咚、咚-敲两百下。听到鼓声，大家都往市场走。关门的时候也要敲鼓-敲三百下。鼓声一响，商家收摊，顾客回家。错过了，明天再来。
[/zh]
[en]
Chang'an, the capital of the Tang dynasty, was one of the grandest cities in the world. Inside the city were two special markets: the East Market and the West Market.

The East Market sold more expensive things and mainly served rich families and officials living in the eastern part of the city. The West Market was even livelier. It had everything - food, clothing, daily tools, and foreign goods that had come a long way along the Silk Road: Persian glass cups, wine from the Western Regions, and spices from India. Foreign merchants with golden hair and blue eyes stood behind their stalls and bargained with customers in not-quite-perfect Chinese.

The East Market and West Market opened at noon every day. When it was time to open, drums were beaten - boom, boom, boom - two hundred times. When people heard the drums, they all walked toward the market. At closing time, drums were beaten again - three hundred times. Once the drums sounded, shopkeepers packed up, customers went home, and if you missed it, you had to come back tomorrow.
[/en]
[/section]

[section]
page: 3
layout: two-column reverse
heading_zh: 宋朝的夜市
heading_en: Night Markets
image: 1-2-night-market.webp
caption_zh: 夜市让买东西也变成了看热闹
caption_en: A night market was not just for shopping - it was part of city life.
[zh]
唐朝的东市西市有严格的时间限制，到了晚上必须关门。宋朝不一样。

宋朝的市场可以开到深夜。天黑以后，街道上挂起灯笼，整条街亮堂堂的，比白天还好看。这就是"夜市"。

《清明上河图》是宋朝画家张择端画的一幅长卷，上面画了宋朝都城的街景。画里有卖布的、卖药的、算命的、修鞋的，还有说书的-一个人在街边搭个台子讲故事，周围围了一大圈人，听得入了迷。所有这一切，都被张择端画进了五米长的画里。今天你如果在博物馆看到这幅画，就能亲眼看到宋朝市场的样子。
[/zh]
[en]
In the Tang dynasty, the East and West Markets had strict time rules. At night, they had to close. The Song dynasty was different.

In the Song, markets could stay open late into the night. After dark, lanterns were hung along the streets, and the whole road glowed. Sometimes it looked even prettier than it did by day. This was the night market.

*Along the River During the Qingming Festival* is a long painting made by the Song painter Zhang Zeduan. It shows street scenes from the Song capital. In the painting, you can find cloth sellers, medicine sellers, fortune-tellers, shoe repairers, and storytellers - one person standing on the street telling a story while a whole crowd gathers around and listens. Zhang Zeduan painted all of this into one scroll more than five meters long. If you see the painting in a museum today, you can look right into a Song dynasty market.
[/en]
[/section]

[section]
page: 4
layout: two-column
heading_zh: 怎么卖东西？
heading_en: How Did People Sell Things?
image: 1-4-street-sellers.webp
caption_zh: 小贩靠喊声、幌子和声音让大家知道自己卖什么
caption_en: Street sellers used calls, signs, and sounds to let people know what they sold.
[zh]
古代的商人，有很多聪明的办法来吸引顾客。

走街串巷的小贩，挑着担子，一边走一边喊。卖糖葫芦的喊"糖-葫-芦-"，卖豆腐的喊"豆-腐-"。每个行当都有自己的叫法，老远一听就知道是卖什么的。

有固定店铺的商人，会在门口挂一个牌子，叫"幌子"。卖酒的挂一个葫芦，卖药的挂一个药葫芦，卖布的挂一块彩色的布条。你不识字也没关系，看到幌子就知道这家店卖什么。

还有用响声做广告的。磨刀的人手上拿一串铁片，走几步哗啦一响，大家都知道磨刀的来了。剃头的人手里拿着一个铁叉子，用铁棍一划-"嗡-"的一声，这条街的人都听到了。
[/zh]
[en]
Ancient merchants had many clever ways to attract customers.

Street peddlers carried shoulder poles and called out as they walked. A seller of candied fruit might sing, "Tang-hu-lu!" A tofu seller might call, "To-fu!" Each kind of seller had a special call. You could hear the sound from far away and know what was being sold.

Shopkeepers with fixed stores hung signs outside the door. These shop signs were called *huangzi*. A wine shop might hang a gourd. A medicine shop might hang a special medicine gourd. A cloth shop might hang a strip of colorful fabric. Even if you could not read, you could still tell what the store sold.

Some people used sound to advertise. The knife sharpener carried a string of metal pieces and shook them with a clatter. Everyone knew the sharpener had arrived. The barber carried an iron tool and scraped it with a rod - weng! - and the whole street could hear it.
[/en]
[/section]

heading_zh: 小结
heading_en: Summary
[zh]
古代没有大超市，但是集市比超市热闹一百倍。各色各样的人、千奇百怪的货物、好听的叫卖声、诱人的食物香味，全都塞在一条街上。

赶集不只是去买东西。赶集是去看人，去听故事，去凑热闹，去感受一个时代最鲜活的日子。
[/zh]
[en]
Ancient China did not have giant supermarkets, but its markets could be one hundred times livelier. All kinds of people, all kinds of goods, bright selling calls, and tempting food smells crowded into one street.

Going to market was not just for buying things. It was for seeing people, hearing stories, joining the excitement, and feeling one of the liveliest parts of the age.
[/en]
[/ending]
