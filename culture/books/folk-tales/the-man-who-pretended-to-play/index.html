滥竽充数

The Man Who Pretended to Play

很久以前，Qí Xuān Wáng · 战国时期齐国国君，故事里喜欢听许多乐师合奏。很喜欢听人吹竽。竽是一种古代的吹奏乐器。不过，他不喜欢一个人单独吹，而是喜欢几百个人一起合奏。有一个叫Nánguō Xiānsheng · 滥竽充数故事中不会吹竽却混进乐队的人。的人听说以后，也混进了乐队。可是，他有一个大问题：他根本不会吹竽。每次演奏时，南郭先生就坐在大家中间，拿着竽装作自己也在吹。几百个人一起演奏，谁也没有发现。这个办法一直很管用。后来，Qí Mǐn Wáng · 战国时期齐国国君，故事里喜欢听乐师独奏。做了新的国君。他也喜欢听竽，却偏偏喜欢让乐师一个一个单独演奏。南郭先生一听，知道麻烦来了。只要轮到他，大家马上就会发现：他根本不会吹。于是，还没等到自己的那一天，他就悄悄逃走了。这就是“滥竽充数”的故事。今天，如果一个人没有真正的本领，却混在别人中间假装自己会，我们就可以说他在“滥竽充数”。混在人群里，也许不容易被发现。直到轮到自己。

Long ago, Qí Xuān Wáng · 齐宣王 · A ruler of Qi who, in the story, enjoyed hearing a large ensemble play together. loved listening to the yú, an ancient wind instrument made with bamboo pipes. But he did not like hearing just one musician. He wanted hundreds of musicians to play together. A man named Nánguō Xiānsheng · 南郭先生 · The man in the story who joins the orchestra even though he cannot play the yu. heard about this and joined the group. There was only one problem. He could not play the yú at all. Whenever the musicians performed, Nanguo sat among them, held up his instrument, and pretended to play. With so many people playing at once, no one noticed. For years, his trick worked. Then King Xuan died, and Qí Mǐn Wáng · 齐湣王 · A ruler of Qi who, in the story, wanted to hear each musician play alone. became the new ruler. King Min liked the yú too—but he wanted to hear each musician play one at a time. Nanguo knew he was in trouble. The moment he played alone, everyone would discover that he could not play at all. So before his turn came, he quietly ran away. This story gave us the expression làn yú chōng shù. Today, it describes someone who does not really have the skill but hides among others and pretends that they do. It may be easy to disappear in a crowd. Until it is your turn.
