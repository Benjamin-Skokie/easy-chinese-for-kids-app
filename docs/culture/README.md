# Culture Guide

Last updated: 2026-08-09

This is the entry point for culture articles, culture visuals, and the culture index. Use the detailed companion documents in this folder when a task needs their specific guidance.

## Purpose

Culture pages should be child-friendly, historically responsible, and visually calm. They explain one teaching idea at a time rather than recreating textbook pages or turning an article into a dense adult reference.

## Article structure

- Begin with a clear, age-appropriate idea or story beat.
- Use short sections, plain English support, and Chinese terms where they materially help learning.
- Provide touch-friendly glossary help for names, dynasties, regions, book titles, and unfamiliar historical terms when inline explanation would interrupt the story.
- Prefer no image to filler art. Every illustration must teach a distinct visual beat.

## Visual direction

- Use original, polished children's-storybook illustration; never copy textbook art or composition.
- Preserve historical specificity intentionally: clothing, architecture, objects, and people should support the article’s period and place.
- Keep compositions uncluttered, readable at app size, and free of baked-in text or watermarks.
- Generate visuals as a coherent batch when an article needs several images; avoid repetitive scenes and drifting styles.
- Final assets are WebP, optimized, recorded in a manifest, and checked in their rendered container.

## Image workflow

1. Write the article’s teaching beats first.
2. Decide which beats truly require an image and what each image must show.
3. Check the current asset library before generating anything.
4. Generate and review a consistent batch; revise historical accuracy, identity, or composition before styling details.
5. Add accepted assets to the correct folder and manifest, then wire them into the article.

## Culture index and navigation

The index is a visual category entrance, followed by complete sections and a compact navigation aid once the initial category board has scrolled away. Navigation earns its place only when it helps a reader move among peer sections; it must not obscure the article or create a second content hierarchy.
