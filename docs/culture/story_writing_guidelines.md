## Nihao World Culture Story 写作原则

1. **先讲故事，不要先上课**
   从人物、场景、问题、意外或正在发生的事情开始，不要从历史背景、定义、朝代和年份开始。
2. **每篇故事只抓住一个中心**
   一篇文章只解决一个最主要的问题、发现或想法。和主线关系不大的知识宁可删掉。
3. **要有真正的开头、中间和结尾**
   事情要往前发展，不要写成“一个事实 → 另一个事实 → 总结”。故事性强的题目可以先想 8–12 个 story beats。
4. **像一本好的儿童故事书，而不是教科书**
   面向 6–10 岁。语言温暖、自然、生动，有一点文学感，但不要幼稚，也不要 museum / encyclopedia / AI presenter 口气。
5. **趣味来自故事本身，不要硬讲笑话**
   幽默来自情节、人物反应、反差、小误会、重复、timing 和轻微夸张。不要硬加网络梗、sarcasm 或吐槽。
6. **语言简单，但不能幼稚**
   不追求“最简单的词”，而追求清楚、自然、生动、优美，而且孩子能够理解。
7. **尽量使用孩子能看见、能画出来的词**
   多写具体的人、东西、动作和场景，少写抽象概念。重要的抽象概念尽量通过故事说明。
8. **人名、地名、朝代、年份和专有名词越少越好**
   只有真正帮助孩子理解故事的名字才留下。人物第一次出现时先告诉孩子“他是谁”。
9. **英文里的中文词和名字统一使用带声调的拼音**
   英文写 `Sūn Wùkōng`、`èrhú`，不要再括号加汉字；中文版本直接用汉字。
10. **Fact Pack 是护栏，不是文章**
    事实必须可靠，但不能把 Fact Pack 的 bullet points 改成句子拼起来。不要编造没有依据的对白、动机和历史事件。
11. **English first, Chinese second**
    英文是 primary version，要像 native English children’s writing。中文根据同一个故事重新自然写，不逐句翻译。
12. **控制长度，但不要为了凑字数硬写**
    普通英文大约 650–800 words，通常约 700；中文约 900–1,200 字。简单题目可以更短，flagship story 可以更长。
13. **必须写成正常段落，不要一句一句单独换行**
    不靠大量短句和断行制造戏剧感。只有场景、story beat、说话人或思路真正变化时才换段。
14. **尽量保留现有图片，不要为了改文字把整套图推翻**
    主要任务是 rewrite the writing。原图能支持更好的故事，就尽量保留；确实无法配合时再换图。
15. **避免和网站已有专题重复**
    每篇文章应该有自己的工作。其他专题已经详细讲过的内容，只在必要时一句带过，不要重新展开。

## Nihao World Culture Story Writing Guidelines

1. **Story First, Lesson Second**
   Start with a person, a scene, a problem, a surprise, or something happening. Do not open with historical background, definitions, dynasties, or dates.
2. **One Clear Center per Story**
   Each story should revolve around one main question, discovery, or idea. Leave out facts that do not serve the main story.
3. **Give the Story a Real Beginning, Middle, and End**
   Something should actually develop. Do not write “fact → fact → fact → summary.” For story-rich topics, it can help to outline about 8–12 story beats first.
4. **Write Like a Good Children’s Storybook, Not a Textbook**
   Write for children ages 6–10. The voice should be warm, natural, vivid, and lightly literary without sounding babyish, academic, museum-like, encyclopedic, or like an AI presenter.
5. **Let the Story Create the Fun**
   Humor should come from the situation, character reactions, contrast, small misunderstandings, repetition, timing, or gentle exaggeration. Avoid forced jokes, internet humor, sarcasm, or snark.
6. **Keep the Language Simple, but Not Childish**
   Do not aim for the simplest possible vocabulary. Aim for language that is clear, natural, vivid, graceful, and understandable to children.
7. **Prefer Words Children Can See and Picture**
   Prefer concrete people, objects, actions, and scenes over abstract concepts. When an abstract idea matters, show it through what people do and what happens.
8. **Minimize Names, Dates, Dynasties, and Specialist Terms**
   Keep only the names and terms children truly need. When a historical person first appears, explain who the person is before expecting the child to remember the name.
9. **Use Tone-Marked Hanyu Pinyin in English**
   In English, write Chinese names and terms in tone-marked Hanyu Pinyin, such as `Sūn Wùkōng` or `èrhú`, without adding Chinese characters in parentheses. In the Chinese version, use Chinese characters without pinyin.
10. **The Fact Pack Is a Guardrail, Not the Story**
    Facts must be reliable, but never turn Fact Pack bullets into sentences and stitch them together. Do not invent unsupported dialogue, motives, or historical events.
11. **English First, Chinese Second**
    English is the primary version and should read like original writing for native English-speaking children. Then rewrite the same story naturally in Chinese rather than translating sentence by sentence.
12. **Control Length, but Never Pad the Story**
    A typical English story is about 650–800 words, often around 700; Chinese is usually about 900–1,200 characters. Simpler topics may be shorter, while flagship stories may be longer.
13. **Use Normal Cohesive Paragraphs**
    Do not create drama by stacking short sentences on separate lines. Start a new paragraph mainly when the scene, story beat, speaker, action, emotion, or line of thought genuinely changes.
14. **Preserve Existing Images Whenever Possible**
    The main task is to improve the writing. Keep existing images and the approximate visual structure whenever they can support the stronger story. Replace images only when truly necessary.
15. **Avoid Repeating Topics Already Covered Elsewhere**
    Every article should have its own job. If another Nihao World story already covers a topic in depth, mention it briefly only when necessary and return quickly to this story’s own center.
